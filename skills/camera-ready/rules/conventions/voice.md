# Voice and person

- Default to active voice with first-person "we" for the authors' actions and decisions. In derivations and proofs, "we" may also mean the author and reader together.
- Choose one agent for the steps of a method or algorithm: either the method ("the algorithm refines the estimate") or "we" ("we refine the estimate"). Use that agent throughout the description. Always use "we" for the authors' choices about the method ("we set k = 5").
- State actions directly. When the work described includes the action, replace "We can compute X" with "We compute X", and replace "aims to" and "seeks to" with the verb itself. Preserve the wording of an unused capability or a goal explicitly marked as unattained, since changing it would [change the claim](../../references/guide.md#claims). Replace the ungrammatical "allows to model" with "models" or "allows us to model", according to the draft's meaning.
- Passive voice is appropriate when the agent is obvious or irrelevant ("Images are normalized to zero mean"), when it keeps [old information in the subject position](../../references/guide.md#information-flow-within-a-sentence), or when it moves a long phrase naming the agent to the end.
- In single-authored writing, keep the author's choice of "I" or "we" and apply it consistently. Do not change "we" to "I".
