# Keep old information first in passives, fronting, and inversions

In a passive with a by-phrase, keep the subject at least as familiar as the actor in that phrase. A fronted phrase before the subject, or an inversion that puts the verb before the subject ("Closest to our work is X"), must begin with old information. Otherwise, use ordinary subject-verb-object order, which may open with new information when doing so is clearer. Keep a passive, "there is", or "it ... that" construction when it introduces new information without making that information the subject.

## Details

This rule has no further details.

## Examples

**Example 1.** The draft has introduced the crane but has not mentioned the beam.

> **Bad.** A steel beam is lifted by the crane.
>
> **Good.** The crane lifts a steel beam.

**Example 2.** The draft has introduced the gate but has not mentioned the padlock or bush.

> **Bad.** Beside a thorny bush stands the gate. The gate is secured by a padlock.
>
> **Good.** The gate stands beside a thorny bush. The gate is secured by a padlock.

**Example 3.** The draft introduces a courtyard in this passage and then describes it.

> **Bad.** There is a courtyard behind the house. In a ring of stones sits the courtyard.
>
> **Good.** There is a courtyard behind the house. The courtyard sits in a ring of stones.

**Example 4.** In a standalone description, the draft introduces the room and then its lighting.

> **Bad.** Behind a heavy curtain lies a small room. Lamps light the room.
>
> **Good.** A small room lies behind a heavy curtain. Lamps light the room.
