# Keep old information first in passives, fronting, and inversions

In a passive with a by-phrase, keep the subject at least as familiar as the actor in that phrase. A fronted phrase or an inversion ("Closest to our work is X") must begin with old information; otherwise, use ordinary subject-verb-object order. That order may depart from [old before new](old-before-new.md) and [final emphasis](stress-position.md) when it is clearer. Keep a passive, "there is", or "it ... that" construction when it introduces new information without making it the subject.

## Details

This rule has no further details.

## Examples

**Edit 1.** Suppose the preceding text introduces the crane but not the beam.

> **Before.** A steel beam is lifted by the crane.

> **After.** The crane lifts a steel beam.

**Edit 2.** Suppose the preceding text introduces the gate but not the padlock or bush.

> **Before.** Beside a thorny bush stands the gate. The gate is secured by a padlock.

> **After.** The gate stands beside a thorny bush. The gate is secured by a padlock.

The second sentence keeps the passive because it puts the familiar gate before the new padlock.
