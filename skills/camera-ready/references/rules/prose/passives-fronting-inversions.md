# Keep old information first in passives, fronting, and inversions

In a passive with a by-phrase, keep the subject at least as familiar as the agent named in the by-phrase. A fronted phrase (one placed before the subject) or an inversion ("Closest to our work is X") must begin with information the passage has established; otherwise use ordinary subject-verb-object order. A sentence in ordinary subject-verb-object order may deliberately open with new information when doing so is clearer. Preserve a passive, a "there is" opening, or an "it ... that" construction when it introduces new material without making that material an unfamiliar subject.

## Details

This rule has no further details.

## Examples

> **Context.** The preceding paragraph introduces the garden but has not mentioned the fence.
>
> **Bad.** Behind a wooden fence lies the garden.
>
> **Good.** The garden lies behind a wooden fence.

> **Context.** The preceding text introduces the crane but has not mentioned the beam.
>
> **Bad.** A steel beam is lifted by the crane.
>
> **Good.** The crane lifts a steel beam.

> **Context.** The preceding text introduces the bird but has not mentioned the shelf.
>
> **Bad.** On a narrow shelf, the bird nests.
>
> **Good.** The bird nests on a narrow shelf.

> **Context.** The preceding text introduces the gate but has not mentioned the padlock or bush.
>
> **Bad.** Beside a thorny bush stands the gate. The gate is secured by a padlock.
>
> **Good.** The gate stands beside a thorny bush. The gate is secured by a padlock.

> **Context.** The passage introduces a new courtyard and then describes it.
>
> **Bad.** There is a courtyard behind the house. In a ring of stones sits the courtyard.
>
> **Good.** There is a courtyard behind the house. The courtyard sits in a ring of stones.

> **Context.** The preceding text discusses identifying a leak’s source but has not mentioned the cracked seal or metal rim.
>
> **Bad.** It is a cracked seal that causes the leak. Under a metal rim sits the seal.
>
> **Good.** It is a cracked seal that causes the leak. The seal sits under a metal rim.

> **Context.** A stand-alone description introduces the room, then its lighting.
>
> **Bad.** Behind a heavy curtain lies a small room. Lamps light the room.
>
> **Good.** A small room lies behind a heavy curtain. Lamps light the room.
