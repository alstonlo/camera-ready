# Say what literally happens

Name the actual event, object, and quantity in words you would use at a whiteboard. Replace "utilize" or "leverage" with "use" when the meaning is the same. Replace abstract words such as "approach", "framework", "strategy", or "issue" with the specific things they stand for, unless defined and reused as shorthand. Name an object before describing its role in the authors' setup: write "the word *true* or *false*" before calling it "the assessment word".

Replace figurative verbs with the literal events stated in the draft, without guessing from the image. Rewrite a noun used as a temporary verb as a verb phrase naming the action ("we footnote the caveat" becomes "we state the caveat in a footnote"), following the [parts-of-speech rule](../conventions/parts-of-speech.md). Rewrite unusual word pairs that readers must decode ("verified red") in ordinary wording ("verified that the test fails on the original code"). Keep established technical terms, verbs, and word combinations. Replace opaque phrasal verbs only with exact equivalents.

Keep analogies whose correspondence the draft makes clear. Replace decorative or misleading imagery with literal wording, and never add a new analogy. For slogan-like sentences, follow the [aphorism rule](../slop/aphorisms.md). When explaining an unfamiliar term, keep the population, metric, and denominator it covers. Clarify ambiguous prepositions and which item "replace" or "substitute" removes and which takes its place, using only meanings the draft establishes; otherwise, flag the ambiguity.

## Details

- Prepositions: "With", "by", or "in" can indicate an instrument, something accompanying the action, a cause, a method, or a time.
- Analogies: List which familiar object stands for each technical object, and state the relation that holds in both cases. Correct only specific misleading implications. Do not add a general warning that the analogy is imperfect or spell out an obvious comparison. Flag conclusions the analogy suggests but the evidence does not support, following the [scope rule](../claims/scope.md).

## Examples

**Edit 1.** Suppose the draft identifies a foam pad as the support.

> **Before.** Our support strategy holds the vial upright.

> **After.** A foam pad holds the vial upright.

**Edit 2.** Suppose the preceding sentence defines "the framework" as the steel frame around the tank.

> **Before.** We utilize the framework to support the tank.

> **After.** We use the framework to support the tank.

The preceding definition already tells readers which object "the framework" names.

**Edit 3.**

> **Before.** Like a sieve retaining pebbles, the mesh retains particles larger than its holes. We utilize it to filter water.

> **After.** Like a sieve retaining pebbles, the mesh retains particles larger than its holes. We use it to filter water.

The sieve explains which particles the mesh retains, so the comparison helps readers understand the operation.

**Edit 4.** Suppose the draft states that the spring closes the lid.

> **Before.** The spring is the lid’s tireless guardian.

> **After.** The spring closes the lid.
