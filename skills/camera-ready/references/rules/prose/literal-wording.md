# Say what literally happens

Name the actual event, object, and quantity, in words you would use at a whiteboard. Replace "utilize" or "leverage" with "use" when the meaning is the same. Replace an abstract word such as "approach", "framework", "strategy", or "issue" with the more specific thing it stands for, unless the word is defined and reused as shorthand: "we adopt a data-augmentation strategy to address the issue of overfitting" becomes "we augment the training data to reduce overfitting". A functional label names an object by its role in the authors' setup ("the assessment word" for "the word *true* or *false*"); name the object before describing its role.

Replace a figurative verb with the literal event the draft states, without guessing from the image: a loader "retires" malformed rows (the rows are deleted), or a filter "passes on" borderline examples (the filter accepts, or rejects, them). Rewrite a noun used as a temporary verb as a verb phrase naming the action ("we footnote the caveat" becomes "we state the caveat in a footnote"), following the [parts-of-speech rule](../conventions/parts-of-speech.md). Rewrite unusual word pairs that readers must decode ("verified red") in ordinary wording ("verified that the test fails on the original code"). Keep established technical terms, verbs ("we benchmark"), and word combinations. Replace an opaque phrasal verb ("dial back") only with an exact equivalent.

Keep an analogy whose correspondence the draft makes clear, replace decorative or misleading imagery with literal wording, and never add a new analogy. For slogan-like sentences, follow the [aphorism rule](../deslopping/aphorisms.md). When explaining an unfamiliar term, keep the population, metric, and denominator it covers. Only when the draft makes the meaning clear, rewrite an ambiguous preposition such as "with", "by", or "in", and make clear which item "replace" or "substitute" removes and which takes its place; otherwise, flag the ambiguity.

## Details

A preposition such as "with", "by", or "in" can name an instrument ("cut with a knife"), something that accompanies the action ("stored with the samples"), a cause, a method, or a time.

To check an analogy, list which familiar object stands for each technical object, and state the relation that holds in both cases. Correct only a specific misleading implication of the analogy. Do not add a general warning that the analogy is imperfect, and do not spell out a comparison that is already obvious. Flag conclusions that the analogy suggests but the evidence does not support, following the [scope rule](../claims/scope.md).

## Examples

**Edit 1.** Suppose the draft identifies a foam pad as the support.

> **Before.** Our support strategy holds the vial upright.

> **After.** A foam pad holds the vial upright.

**Edit 2.** Say the preceding sentence defines "the framework" as the steel frame around the tank.

> **Before.** We utilize the framework to support the tank.

> **After.** We use the framework to support the tank.

The preceding definition already tells readers which object "the framework" names.

**Edit 3.**

> **Before.** Like a sieve retaining pebbles, the mesh retains particles larger than its holes. We utilize it to filter water.

> **After.** Like a sieve retaining pebbles, the mesh retains particles larger than its holes. We use it to filter water.

The sieve explains which particles the mesh retains, so the comparison helps readers understand the operation.

**Edit 4.** Imagine the draft states that the spring closes the lid.

> **Before.** The spring is the lid’s tireless guardian.

> **After.** The spring closes the lid.
