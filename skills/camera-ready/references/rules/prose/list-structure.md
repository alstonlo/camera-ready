# Use lists when readers need separate items

Use a list for items readers must distinguish or follow one by one. If bullets make an argument but hide reasons or connections between claims, rewrite them as connected prose. Remove item numbers that imply an unsupported order or ranking, unless the text refers to them. Flag any change to a list of contributions or objectives instead of making it.

## Details

When turning bullets into prose, connect ideas only as the draft states or establishes. Do not add a word such as "therefore" merely to make the sentences read smoothly. Keep short, incidental examples within a sentence when readers do not need to refer to them separately. Do not format minor details as a displayed list that makes them look like contributions.

Group a long list only by categories established in the draft. Do not require groups of three or impose a fixed limit on the number of items. Keep every item and qualification, and preserve the distinction between primary and secondary items. Give equivalent entries [parallel form](parallel-form.md). For a list of contributions or objectives, flag any proposed regrouping, renumbering, or conversion to prose instead of editing the list.

When a table would help readers compare several objects on the same properties, suggest one instead of creating it yourself or supplying missing entries.

## Examples

**Edit 1.** The scale's limit is explicitly given in the draft as the reason for the weighing failure.

> **Before.** • The scale reads only up to 5 kg. • The scale cannot weigh the 8 kg sack.
>
> **After.** The scale reads only up to 5 kg, so it cannot weigh the 8 kg sack.

**Edit 2.** The symbol ↵ marks a line break.

> **Before.** First, close the valve; second, disconnect the hose; third, remove the filter.
>
> **After.** 1. Close the valve. ↵ 2. Disconnect the hose. ↵ 3. Remove the filter.

**Edit 3.** All requirements have equal priority, and none of their numbers is referenced in the draft. The symbol ↵ marks a line break.

> **Before.** 1. The lid must seal. ↵ 2. The handle must fold.
>
> **After.** • The lid must seal. ↵ • The handle must fold.

**Edit 4.** The draft establishes measurement and prediction as the two groups; ↵ marks a line break. After is a proposal to the author.

> **Before.** Our objectives are: ↵ • Record pressure. ↵ • Forecast rainfall. ↵ • Record temperature.
>
> **After.** Our objectives are: ↵ • Measurement: record pressure and temperature. ↵ • Prediction: forecast rainfall.
