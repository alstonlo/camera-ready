# Keep old information first in passives, fronting, and inversions

In a passive with a by-phrase, keep the subject at least as familiar as the agent named in the by-phrase. A fronted phrase (one placed before the subject) or an inversion ("Closest to our work is X") must begin with information the passage has established; otherwise use ordinary subject-verb-object order. A sentence in ordinary order may deliberately open with new information when clearer. Preserve a passive, a "there is" opening, or an "it ... that" construction when it introduces new material without making that material an unfamiliar subject.

## Details

Compare what readers already know about a passive sentence's subject and its agent. Before changing it to active voice, check whether the active subject would introduce an unfamiliar object ahead of what the paragraph is about. Apply the same check to fronted phrases and inversions.

A "there is" opening can introduce an object that readers do not yet know. Replacing every such opening with "exists" or "occurs" can keep the assertion but make the new object harder to follow. Remove an empty opening only when the resulting sentence reads more clearly in context.

## Examples

The preceding paragraph introduces the garden but has not mentioned the fence.

- Bad: Behind a wooden fence lies the garden.
- Good: The garden lies behind a wooden fence.
