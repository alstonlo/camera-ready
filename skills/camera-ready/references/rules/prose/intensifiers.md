# Cut words that add only emphasis

Cut words that add only emphasis, such as "very" and "extremely", or replace them with a [measurement](quantified-comparatives.md) from the draft. Treat "essentially" and "largely" as [softeners](../claims/hedges.md); preserve their scope or uncertainty unless the draft supplies a precise replacement. Delete "Note that" and similar prompts unless they prevent a likely misreading. Keep "obviously", "clearly", or "trivially" only before a proof step routine for readers outside the subfield. Remove them from empirical claims, and flag any unexplained nonroutine step.

## Details

When "highly", "remarkably", or "exceptionally" adds only emphasis, cut it or replace it with a measurement from the draft. Words such as "somewhat" and "relatively" soften a claim, as "largely" does; deleting them strengthens the claim without evidence. When a modifier asserts uniqueness, completeness, or superiority, follow [unsupported labels](../claims/unsupported-labels.md) rather than deleting the factual claim. Remove words such as "clearly" from empirical statements such as "clearly outperforms".

## Examples

**Edit 1.** Suppose the draft reports the cable's measured length as 40 m.

> **Before.** The cable is extremely long.

> **After.** The cable is 40 m long.

**Edit 2.**

> **Before.** Note that the coating largely prevents rust.

> **After.** The coating largely prevents rust.

The word "largely" stays because the coating is not claimed to prevent all rust.

**Edit 3.** Imagine the preceding sentence defines a zero score as no detected damage.

> **Before.** Note that a zero score does not rule out damage. The test is very useful.

> **After.** Note that a zero score does not rule out damage. The test is useful.

The prompt stays to prevent readers from mistaking a lack of detected damage for proof that no damage exists.

**Edit 4.** Say the draft's proof establishes that the last term is zero.

> **Before.** Clearly, adding zero leaves the sum unchanged, so we can very safely drop the last term.

> **After.** Clearly, adding zero leaves the sum unchanged, so we can safely drop the last term.

The word "Clearly" can stay because adding zero is a routine step for readers outside the subfield.

**Edit 5.**

> **Before.** The treated seeds clearly sprout sooner.

> **After.** The treated seeds sprout sooner.
