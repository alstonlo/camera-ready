# Cut intensifiers

Cut empty intensifiers such as "very" and "extremely"; use a [measurement](quantified-comparatives.md) when supplied. Treat "essentially" and "largely" as [softeners](../claims/hedges.md), preserving their scope or uncertainty unless the draft supplies a precise replacement. Delete "Note that" and similar prompts unless they prevent a likely misreading. Keep "obviously", "clearly", or "trivially" only for a proof step that is routine for the reader outside the subfield. Remove them from empirical claims such as "clearly outperforms", and flag a nonroutine step whose explanation is missing.

## Details

Inspect "highly", "remarkably", and "exceptionally" for emphasis without content. Distinguish them from softeners such as "somewhat" and "relatively", whose deletion can strengthen a claim. A precise replacement states the condition or magnitude already supplied by the draft.

Reader prompts such as "It is worth mentioning" or "Observe that" usually delay the assertion. Keep a prompt when it directs attention away from a plausible misreading that the surrounding text would otherwise encourage.

A modifier asserting uniqueness, completeness, or superiority makes a claim. Apply the [overclaiming rule](../claims/overclaiming.md) rather than mechanically deleting factual content. In a proof, removing an assurance does not repair a missing argument; flag the absent explanation when the step is not routine for the intended reader.

## Examples

- Bad: Note that the parser processes 24,000 records per second on the audit workload.
- Good: The parser processes 24,000 records per second on the audit workload.
