# Quantify comparatives

Replace a vague comparative with the measurement the draft reports, and name what it is compared with. Lead with the [headline number](../story/headline-number.md), describe the pattern, and refer to the table for other values. A label such as "large" needs a stated comparison or threshold; it cannot replace the measurement. Do not calculate an unreported speedup merely to make the prose more concrete.

## Details

Use the quantities the draft reports, with the baseline named. A reader should not have to infer what "faster" compares or which of several experiments supplies the numbers. Do not derive a ratio simply because the two runtimes are available.

Terms such as "high", "low", and "small" need a stated comparison or threshold when they add a claim. Keep a meaningful qualitative conclusion, but do not let it replace the measurement. Apply the [comparison-evidence rule](../claims/uncertainty.md) before presenting measurements as comparable.

## Examples

The draft reports that cart A carries 80 kg and cart B carries 50 kg in the same test.

- Bad: In this test, cart A carries more weight than cart B.
- Good: In this test, cart A carries 80 kg, compared with 50 kg for cart B.
