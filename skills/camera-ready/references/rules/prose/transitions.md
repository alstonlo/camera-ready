# Use connecting words only for relations the draft supports

Replace "as", "since", or "while" when readers cannot tell whether it signals time, cause, or contrast. Use "conversely" only when the second statement reverses the first. Remove redundant connecting words, but keep those readers need. Flag "therefore" if the preceding statements do not support the conclusion. Follow the [causal-evidence rule](../claims/causal-language.md) for causal words.

## Details

For contrast, distinguish a comparison, an exception, a direct denial, and a true reversal; two results that merely differ are not converses. A connecting word is redundant when the rest of the sentence already expresses the relation. Flag a connection that the surrounding draft leaves unclear.

## Examples

**Edit 1.** Suppose the draft establishes the timing without establishing a cause.

> **Before.** As the pump stops, the lamp flashes.

> **After.** When the pump stops, the lamp flashes.

**Edit 2.**

> **Before.** Glass transmits light. Conversely, wool retains heat.

> **After.** Glass transmits light. Wool retains heat.

**Edit 3.**

> **Before.** Because the gasket is cracked, therefore the lid leaks.

> **After.** Because the gasket is cracked, the lid leaks.

**Edit 4.** Suppose the draft states elsewhere that every cracked jar leaks.

> **Before.** This jar is cracked. Therefore, it leaks.

> **After.** Every cracked jar leaks. This jar is cracked. Therefore, it leaks.

The edit proposes supplying the missing premise from elsewhere in the draft.
