# Use transitions that express a valid logical relation

Use a connective, such as "therefore" or "however", only for the relation the draft supports. When "as", "since", or "while" could mean time, cause, or contrast, reword it so that one reading remains; "conversely" needs an actual reversal. Remove a redundant connective when the sentence already expresses that relation, but keep a link the reader needs. If "therefore" introduces a conclusion the preceding premises do not support, flag the missing inference. Causal wording must satisfy the [causal-evidence rule](../claims/causal-language.md).

## Details

Identify the premises before a consequence connective and the conclusion after it. Check whether the draft supplies the reasoning between them. A connective cannot repair a missing inference.

Distinguish a comparison, an exception, a direct denial, and a reversal of a relation. Two different results do not become converses merely because the sentences discuss different methods.

Use the surrounding draft to resolve a connective with more than one reading. If it does not settle the relation, flag the ambiguity. Remove a repeated connective only when another connective or the sentence structure already expresses the same relation.

## Examples

- Bad: The lid prevents spills. However, the handle makes the jug easy to carry.
- Good: The lid prevents spills. The handle makes the jug easy to carry.
