# State claims and reasons in complete, literal sentences

Replace conversational idioms such as "Call it a wash" and editorial commands with complete, literal statements of the claim and its reason. Use only reasons the draft supplies; flag a missing reason. Keep a deliberately short sentence when it is complete and literal. Preserve the author's supported stance under [author voice](../deslopping/author-voice.md).

## Details

This rule has no further details.

## Examples

**Edit 1.** Suppose the draft says elsewhere that the second trial tests the seal under pressure.

> **Before.** Do not overlook the second trial.

> **After.** The second trial matters because it tests the seal under pressure.

**Edit 2.**

> **Before.** Neither pump moves water. Regrettably, that is the long and short of it.

> **After.** Regrettably, neither pump moves water.

"Regrettably" preserves the author's judgment about the result without asking readers to interpret an idiom.
