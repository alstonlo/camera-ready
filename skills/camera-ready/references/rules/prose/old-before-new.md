# Put old information before new

Open each sentence with a term from the preceding text, or a part, property, or alternative readers can infer from it. A concept known in the field is not old information until the text introduces it. Among several old items, open with the one the sentence discusses, or the most recent if they are equally relevant. When changing voice or clause order, keep the paragraph's topic as the main subject. Keep useful passives under [alternative sentence orders](passives-fronting-inversions.md).

## Details

An opening condition can refer to the preceding sentence while the main subject names the paragraph's topic. After reordering clauses or changing voice, check both the opening's link to old information and the main subject's link to the topic. Repeating an incidental word from the preceding sentence does not help readers follow a sentence about a different topic.

## Examples

**Edit 1.**

> **Before.** We examined a bicycle. A loose bolt held its front wheel.

> **After.** We examined a bicycle. Its front wheel was held by a loose bolt.

**Edit 2.** Suppose the paragraph discusses the fan and dust is a test condition.

> **Before.** We tested the fan in dust. Dust coated the fan’s three blades.

> **After.** We tested the fan in dust. The fan’s three blades were coated with dust.

**Edit 3.** Suppose the preceding sentence introduces cold conditions and the paragraph discusses the sensor.

> **Before.** A voltage of 5 V is required by the sensor in cold conditions.

> **After.** In cold conditions, the sensor requires 5 V.
