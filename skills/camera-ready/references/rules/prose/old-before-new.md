# Put old information before new

Open each sentence with something the preceding text has given the reader: a term it has used, or a part, property, or alternative the reader can infer from it. A concept is not old information merely because the field knows it. When several elements are old, open with the one the new sentence builds on; if they are equally relevant, choose the most recently mentioned one. When changing voice or clause order, keep what the paragraph is about as the main subject, and preserve a useful passive as [alternative sentence orders](other-sentence-orders.md) allows.

## Details

An initial condition or contextual phrase can connect a sentence to the preceding text while its main subject keeps attention on what the paragraph is about. Check both connections when moving clauses or changing voice.

Repeating a word does not establish a useful link if the word names an incidental detail and the next sentence develops something else. Choose the opening according to the explanation readers are following.

## Examples

- Bad: We fitted the telescope with a new lens. A thin layer of dust covered the lens.
- Good: We fitted the telescope with a new lens. The lens was covered by a thin layer of dust.
