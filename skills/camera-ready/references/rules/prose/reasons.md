# State the reasons for a choice

Unless the omitted items are obvious, replace "for various reasons", or an "etc." that ends a list, with the reasons or items the draft supplies; if the draft omits them, flag the gap. When an unexplained method choice is central to a stated claim, give the rationale the draft states, or flag the missing rationale. Do not request justifications for routine choices whose role is clear. A stated reason for a choice is not evidence that the choice caused a result; claims of cause follow [causal language](../claims/causal-language.md).

## Details

A reason names the property of a choice that makes it suit the task. A nonstandard input transformation or a software capability central to the method may need a reason. Do not fill that gap with a generic assertion about efficiency, fairness, or robustness.

Routine methods need less explanation than novel operations or choices that define the contribution. Judge what is clear to a computer scientist outside the subfield. The purpose of an algorithm and its conceptual operations belong under [definitions and procedures](definitions-procedures.md).

## Examples

The draft states that amber bottles block light that breaks down the dye.

- Bad: We use amber bottles for various reasons.
- Good: We use amber bottles because they block light that breaks down the dye.
