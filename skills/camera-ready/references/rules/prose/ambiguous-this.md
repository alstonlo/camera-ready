# Add an accurate noun after an ambiguous "this"

When "this", "that", or "it" could refer to several nouns or claims, add a concise noun identifying the supported referent. When the draft does not settle the referent, flag it rather than choosing one. The noun must preserve the earlier statement's status and evaluation: a conjecture is not a finding, and a change is not necessarily an improvement. A bare demonstrative may remain in a clear interpretive opening such as "This suggests that" after one unambiguous claim.

## Details

Identify whether the pronoun refers to an observation, assumption, prediction, proposal, or attributed view. Choose a noun with the same status. A short label often identifies the referent more clearly than a long restatement, but an evaluative label must not silently endorse the earlier claim.

When several referents fit, use the surrounding draft to identify the intended one. Do not resolve the ambiguity by selecting the most plausible scientific explanation.

## Examples

The draft identifies the overflow, rather than the stopped pump, as the cause of the delay.

- Bad: The pump stopped, and the tank overflowed. This delayed the experiment.
- Good: The pump stopped, and the tank overflowed. This overflow delayed the experiment.
