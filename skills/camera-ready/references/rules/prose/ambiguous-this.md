# Add an accurate noun after an ambiguous "this"

When "this", "that", or "it" could refer to several nouns or claims, add a concise noun identifying the supported referent. If the draft does not settle the referent, flag the ambiguity rather than choosing one. The noun must preserve the earlier statement's status and evaluation: a conjecture is not a finding, and a change is not necessarily an improvement. A bare demonstrative may remain in a clear interpretive opening such as "This suggests that" after one unambiguous claim.

## Details

This rule needs no details beyond its summary.

## Examples

The draft identifies the overflow, rather than the stopped pump, as the cause of the delay.

> Bad: The pump stopped, and the tank overflowed. This delayed the experiment.
>
> Good: The pump stopped, and the tank overflowed. This overflow delayed the experiment.

The draft says the conjecture motivates the next test.

> Bad: We conjecture that salt weakens the seal; the lid also bends. This motivates the next test.
>
> Good: We conjecture that salt weakens the seal; the lid also bends. This conjecture motivates the next test.

The draft reports that the pressure rose but does not say whether the rise helps.

> Bad: We replaced the valve, and the pressure rose. This improvement triggered the alarm.
>
> Good: We replaced the valve, and the pressure rose. This rise in pressure triggered the alarm.

The draft identifies the dish in the warm room as the one that froze.

> Bad: We left one dish of water in a cold room and one in a warm room. It froze overnight. This suggests that the heating failed.
>
> Good: We left one dish of water in a cold room and one in a warm room. The dish in the warm room froze overnight. This suggests that the heating failed.
