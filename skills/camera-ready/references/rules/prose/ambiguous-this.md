# Add an accurate noun after an ambiguous "this"

When "this", "that", or "it" could refer to several nouns or claims, add a noun naming the supported referent. If the draft leaves the referent unclear, flag the ambiguity instead of choosing one. The noun must preserve the statement's status and evaluation: a conjecture is not a finding, and a change is not necessarily an improvement. A bare "This" may open an interpretation like "This suggests that" after one unambiguous claim.

## Details

This rule has no further details.

## Examples

**Edit 1.** In the draft, the conjecture motivates the next test.

> **Before.** We conjecture that salt weakens the seal; the lid also bends. This motivates the next test.
>
> **After.** We conjecture that salt weakens the seal; the lid also bends. This conjecture motivates the next test.

**Edit 2.** The draft leaves open whether higher pressure is beneficial.

> **Before.** We replaced the valve, and the pressure rose. This improvement triggered the alarm.
>
> **After.** We replaced the valve, and the pressure rose. This rise in pressure triggered the alarm.

**Edit 3.** The dish that froze is the one in the warm room, according to the draft.

> **Before.** We left one dish of water in a cold room and one in a warm room. It froze overnight. This suggests that the heating failed.
>
> **After.** We left one dish of water in a cold room and one in a warm room. The dish in the warm room froze overnight. This suggests that the heating failed.
