# Put important new information in the stress position

The stress position is the end of a sentence or independent clause. If a qualifier such as "in these tests" follows the main point, move the qualifier to the front instead of deleting it. If several important items share one ending, split the sentence so that each gets its own ending. Check whether a paragraph's first sentence ends with the topic that the paragraph develops. When joining or subordinating clauses, keep the relative emphasis of findings and qualifications. Move an essential qualification out of parentheses if they make it look optional. Keep each scope condition with the claim it limits. Follow [pause punctuation](../deslopping/pause-punctuation.md) when using colons or semicolons.

## Details

The stress position is where a sentence or independent clause becomes grammatically complete. An independent clause would be a complete sentence by itself. A subordinate clause, such as one that begins with "although", would not. Readers usually give more weight to an independent clause than to a subordinate clause or a phrase. A comma or the end of a subordinate clause does not create a stress position by itself. At the end of each sentence, paragraph, and section, check whether incidental detail follows the central point. Do not add sentence fragments or repeat claims to create emphasis. For words that only label a point as important, follow [self-labeling](../deslopping/self-labeling.md).

## Examples

**Edit 1.** Suppose the paragraph gives both findings equal weight.

> **Before.** In these tests, the shield reduces heat loss and noise.

> **After.** In these tests, the shield reduces heat loss. In these tests, it also reduces noise.

Both findings are limited to these tests; the second sentence must not read as a general claim.

**Edit 2.** Say the draft gives the leak and the faster flow equal importance.

> **Before.** Although the joint leaks, it allows faster flow.

> **After.** The joint leaks, but it allows faster flow.

**Edit 3.**

> **Before.** The sensor works (only below 40 °C).

> **After.** The sensor works only below 40 °C.

**Edit 4.** Say this paragraph develops the finding about boiling time.

> **Before.** The kettle (model K2) boils water in 90 s in this test.

> **After.** In this test, the kettle (model K2) boils water in 90 s.
