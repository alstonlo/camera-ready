# Put important new information in the stress position

The stress position is the end of a sentence or independent clause. Check sentence, paragraph, and section endings for undeserved emphasis; move incidental material earlier. Move a qualifier such as "in these tests" from after the main point to the front instead of deleting it, unless it is a short condition that is clear at the end ([first reading](first-reading.md)). If several important items share one ending, split the sentence to give each its own ending. Check whether a paragraph's first sentence ends with the topic the paragraph develops. When joining or subordinating clauses, preserve the relative emphasis of findings and qualifications. Move an essential qualification out of parentheses if they make it look optional. Keep each scope condition with the claim it limits. Follow [pause punctuation](../slop/pause-punctuation.md) when using colons or semicolons.

## Details

The stress position is where a sentence or independent clause becomes grammatically complete. Readers usually give more weight to an independent clause than to a subordinate clause or phrase. A comma or the end of a subordinate clause alone does not create a stress position. Do not add sentence fragments or repeat claims to create emphasis. For words that only label a point as important, follow [self-labeling](../slop/self-labeling.md).

## Examples

**Edit 1.** Suppose the paragraph gives both findings equal weight.

> **Before.** In these tests, the shield reduces heat loss and noise.

> **After.** In these tests, the shield reduces heat loss. In these tests, it also reduces noise.

Both findings are limited to these tests; the second sentence must not read as a general claim.

**Edit 2.** Suppose the draft gives the leak and the faster flow equal importance.

> **Before.** Although the joint leaks, it allows faster flow.

> **After.** The joint leaks, but it allows faster flow.

**Edit 3.**

> **Before.** The sensor works (only below 40 °C).

> **After.** The sensor works only below 40 °C.

**Edit 4.** Suppose this paragraph develops the finding about boiling time.

> **Before.** The kettle (model K2) boils water in 90 s in this test.

> **After.** In this test, the kettle (model K2) boils water in 90 s.
