# Combine signposts with the content they introduce

Replace sentences about the text, such as "The previous section analyzed the encoder. This section presents the decoder.", with the content they announce and, if needed, a reference to its location. Keep references that locate evidence or a proof, and keep a roadmap of at most two sentences at the end of an introduction or chapter opening. Delete three kinds of signpost: announcements that name no content ("Section 7 concludes"), roadmaps within a section that repeat its subheadings, and signposts that take more effort to understand than they save. Preserve [genuine transitions and summaries](../cutting/protect.md). For a retained roadmap, check its count, order, labels, and distinctions against the actual sections or items. Repair a mismatch that the draft resolves unambiguously; flag conflicting categories and any repair that would change the section order or the contributions.

## Details

A reference can help readers locate evidence without merely announcing that a section exists. Attach the reference to the relevant content when the two can be stated together clearly.

For a roadmap, list the promised items and the actual headings in order. Check for missing, extra, renamed, or overlapping categories. Do not invent categories to reconcile a conflict. Correct the description when the existing organization makes the correction unambiguous; do not reorganize sections to match a faulty roadmap.

Compare the organization of genuinely parallel sections while preserving meaningful differences. Apply [parallel form](parallel-form.md) to their prose.

## Examples

- Bad: Section 4 describes the access-control check. The check rejects requests without a matching grant.
- Good: The access-control check (Section 4) rejects requests without a matching grant.
