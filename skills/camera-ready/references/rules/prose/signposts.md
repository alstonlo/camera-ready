# State the content a signpost announces

A signpost announces what the text discusses or where to find it. Replace it with the content it announces and, if needed, a location reference. Keep references to evidence or proofs, [genuine transitions and summaries](../cutting/protected-sentences.md), and a roadmap of at most two sentences at the end of an introduction or chapter opening. Delete announcements that state no content ("Section 7 concludes"), roadmaps within a section that repeat its subheadings, and signposts that take more effort to understand than they save. In a kept roadmap, correct mismatches with the actual sections or items when the draft resolves them unambiguously (see Details). Flag conflicting categories and any revision that would change the section order or contributions.

## Details

For example, replace "The previous section described the apparatus. This section presents the measurements." with the measurements and, if needed, a reference to the apparatus section. Keep a location reference such as "as proved in Appendix A".

A roadmap previews the sections or items that follow. For each roadmap you keep, compare its items in order against the actual headings or text. Check that the count, order, names, and distinctions match, and look for missing, extra, renamed, or overlapping categories. For sections with the same logical role, apply [parallel form](parallel-form.md).

## Examples

**Example 1.**

> **Bad.** Section 4 describes the filter. The filter traps particles larger than 5 micrometers.
>
> **Good.** The filter (Section 4) traps particles larger than 5 micrometers.

**Example 2.**

> **Bad.** The seal leaks above 20 kPa (Table 3). Section 7 concludes.
>
> **Good.** The seal leaks above 20 kPa (Table 3).

**Example 3.** In this draft, the section uses “Heat test” and “Pressure test” as subheadings. The symbol ↵ marks a line break.

> **Bad.** This section covers the heat test and the pressure test. ↵ **Heat test.** The seal survives heating.
>
> **Good.** **Heat test.** The seal survives heating.

**Example 4.**

> **Bad.** The point to which we now turn, and toward which the preceding discussion has been building, concerns the lid. The lid leaks above 20 kPa.
>
> **Good.** The lid leaks above 20 kPa.

**Example 5.** In this draft, a roadmap closes the introduction, and the draft’s headings place drying in Section 2 and weighing in Section 3.

> **Bad.** Section 2 describes weighing; Section 3 describes drying.
>
> **Good.** Section 2 describes drying; Section 3 describes weighing.

**Example 6.** In this draft, the text describes two tests, heating and freezing, but no cooling test.

> **Bad.** The next section presents three tests: heating and cooling.
>
> **Good.** The next section presents two tests: heating and freezing.

**Example 7.**

> **Bad.** Heat loss is at most 5 W, as proved in Appendix A. The next section presents the measurements. Measured heat loss is 4 W.
>
> **Good.** Heat loss is at most 5 W, as proved in Appendix A. Measured heat loss is 4 W.
