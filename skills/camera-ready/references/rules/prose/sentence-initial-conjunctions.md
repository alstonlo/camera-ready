# Do not open a sentence with "And", "But", "So", or "Plus"

Do not begin a sentence with "And", "But", "So", or "Plus". Join it to the preceding sentence or replace the opening, keeping its relation. Keep a contrast as a contrast, and mark a consequence only when the draft supports it. For the final item of a series, use "Finally", not "In addition", "Moreover", or "Furthermore", which mark further support. For a cited author's objection, follow [prior-work attribution](../story/prior-work.md). Keep clear coordination within a sentence.

## Details

This prohibition is a style convention, not a claim that initial conjunctions are ungrammatical. A contrast may join the preceding sentence with "but" or begin with a contrast marker such as "However". An addition may join the earlier clause; a final series item needs a sequence marker. Use a consequence marker only when the inference holds.

Additive "Plus" does not by itself establish a stronger argumentative relation. Preserve the distinction between listing another implementation fact and adding a supporting premise.

## Examples

- Bad: The tokenizer preserves whitespace. Plus, it retains source offsets.
- Good: The tokenizer preserves whitespace, and it retains source offsets.
