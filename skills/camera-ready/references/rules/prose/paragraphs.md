# Develop one point per paragraph

Develop one point per paragraph, and state it as a specific claim in the paragraph's first sentence. "Results were mixed" and "We analyze robustness" [announce](signposts.md) a topic; "Accuracy falls after pruning" states a claim. Allow brief setup only when the claim cannot be understood first: one sentence of necessary context from the draft, immediately followed by the claim. A results paragraph may open with the specific question it tests if the next sentence answers it. Put the evidence after the answer. A procedural or descriptive paragraph may begin with its first fact or step. Keep both sides of a comparison in one paragraph when they support one point. Split a paragraph where its point changes, and merge short paragraphs only when they develop the same point. Judge a paragraph by development and coherence, not length.

## Details

A topic label names an area without saying what the paragraph establishes. A procedure paragraph can explain how sampling works without claiming that the procedure improves a result. Its opening should begin that explanation rather than promise one.

Use brief setup only when the claim would otherwise depend on an unexplained term or relation. A popularity statement or broad announcement supplies neither. A question used merely to connect paragraphs is not a specific question tested by a results paragraph; answering it immediately does not make that opening useful. Do not manufacture questions to vary the rhythm.

End on the developed point or a supported implication. Keep a useful bridge to the next paragraph, but do not end on an unrelated issue or append a summary to every short paragraph.

## Examples

This results paragraph describes a freezing test of six clay pots and six steel pots.

- Bad: We compared two pot materials. All six clay pots cracked, and all six steel pots remained intact.
- Good: Only the clay pots cracked. All six clay pots cracked, and all six steel pots remained intact.
