# Apply the deletion test without changing meaning

Delete a word or clause only when it adds nothing and the sentence remains as easy to understand. For every deletion, replacement, or restructuring, check that the revision keeps the original's referents, actors, actions, definitions, logical relations, modality (whether something is possible, required, or actual), scope, and authorial stance. Preserve distinctions even when the simpler version conveys the gist. Keep referents and [structural words](first-reading.md) the reader needs. Apply [claim rules](../claims/scope.md) to substantive changes. When equivalence is uncertain, keep the original and flag the ambiguity. Brevity alone does not justify an edit.

## Details

When replacing an action noun, changing voice, or choosing an apparent synonym, check that no operation or participant disappears. A possibility must not become an observed fact, and a defining condition must not become merely one property of the object.

Compare the cases in which the original and revision hold, including equality at a threshold, the population measured, and the denominator of a quantity. A shorter description of a mathematical restriction is useful only if it admits exactly the same cases. Keep an explicit restriction when its redundancy is uncertain.

Shortening wording differs from removing an idea. Follow [idea-level cutting permissions](../cutting/idea-level.md) and preserve [protected content](../cutting/protect.md).

## Examples

The original draft says the sensor works only below 40 °C; Bad is an edit that dropped this restriction.

- Bad: The sensor works.
- Good: The sensor works only below 40 °C.
