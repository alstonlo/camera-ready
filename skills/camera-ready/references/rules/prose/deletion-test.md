# Apply the deletion test without changing meaning

Test every clause by deleting it. Keep a deletion only if the word or clause adds nothing and the sentence remains as easy to understand. Every deletion, replacement, or restructuring must keep the [structural words](first-reading.md) needed on the first reading and preserve who does what, what each word refers to, whether something is possible, required, or actual, definitions, logical relations, distinctions, cases covered, and the author's stance. Apply the [claim rules](../claims/scope.md) to any substantive change. If you are unsure whether an edit changes the meaning, keep the original and flag the ambiguity.

## Details

Brevity alone does not justify an edit. Keep distinctions even when simpler wording conveys the general idea. Compare the exact cases in which each version holds, including values equal to a threshold, the population measured, and the denominator of any rate or fraction. Structural words include articles and "that" after verbs such as "show" and "find". When cutting ideas rather than tightening wording, follow [cutting permissions](../cuts/idea-cuts.md) and [protected content](../cuts/protected-sentences.md).

## Examples

**Edit 1.** Suppose the draft says that the nurse asks the patient to lift the weight.

> **Before.** The nurse lifts the weight.

> **After.** The nurse asks the patient to lift the weight.

The original text is an editor's shortening that assigns the lifting to the nurse. The edited text restores who asks and who is asked to act.

**Edit 2.** Suppose the draft counts a score of exactly 60 as passing.

> **Before.** A passing score exceeds 60.

> **After.** A passing score is at least 60.

The original text is an editor's rewrite that excludes a score the draft counts as passing. The edited text restores the intended threshold.

**Edit 3.** Suppose the draft says the lamp cannot turn on while the door is closed and may stay off while it is open.

> **Before.** The lamp turns on if the door opens.

> **After.** The lamp stays off unless the door opens.

The original text is an editor's rewrite; the edited text restores the draft. An open door does not guarantee that the lamp will turn on.

**Edit 4.**

> **Before.** We found that the scale that we used was faulty in nature.

> **After.** We found that the scale that we used was faulty.

The two instances of "that" keep the finding and the description of the scale easy to follow.

**Edit 5.** Suppose the draft establishes that the latch can lock automatically but does not report that it does so.

> **Before.** The latch locks automatically.

> **After.** The latch can lock automatically.

The original text is an editor's shortening. The edited text restores what the draft says the latch can do without claiming that it actually locks.
