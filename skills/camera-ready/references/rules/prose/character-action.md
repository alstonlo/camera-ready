# Character-action principle

Use the grammatical subject to name the actor and the verb to name the action. A nominalization expresses an action as a noun, often ending in *-tion, -ment, -ance,* or *-ing*. Rewrite it as a verb when it expresses the clause's main action after a verb with little meaning of its own ("perform an evaluation of", "lead to an improvement in"), or when several nominalizations occur together in one clause. Keep a nominalization that names a technique ("quantization"), refers to the previous sentence ("This reduction in memory ..."), replaces an awkward "the fact that", or appears alone in an otherwise clear sentence.

Keep the subject-verb pairing literal. The active but figurative "the schedule launches the jobs" needs the actual actor ("we launch the jobs on the schedule"). A component that performs the operation may be the subject ("the filter removes duplicates"); a schedule performs no operation. Only a person or animal may be the subject of a verb that normally requires one, such as a verb of intention, thought, perception, feeling, memory, or eating (*promises, hopes, misunderstands, forgets, bites, swallows*). With an inanimate subject, the reader may need to reinterpret the verb as a figure of speech, so name the literal event ("the docstring promises that hidden folders are skipped" becomes "the docstring states that hidden folders are skipped"; "the parser swallows the error" becomes "the parser catches the error and returns False"). Keep verbs the field uses routinely for programs and models ("the function expects an integer", "the network learns features"). Use a [reporting verb](../claims/verb-ladder.md) for a result or figure ("the results suggest").

To find actions expressed as nouns or left unstated, list the paragraph's main verbs. If most are *is*, *has*, or *leads to*, the actions are in nouns or missing. If the passage never says what one actor does to another, flag the missing action.

## Examples

- Bad: "The optimization of the objective through the application of gradient descent led to an improvement in accuracy."
- Good: "We optimize the objective with gradient descent, which improves accuracy."
