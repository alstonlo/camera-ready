# Put actors in subjects and actions in verbs

A nominalization is an action expressed as a noun. When a phrase combines a verb with little meaning and a nominalization ("perform an evaluation of", "lead to an improvement in"), or several nominalizations occur in one clause, put the actor in the subject and the action in the verb. Keep a nominalization that names a technique ("quantization") or a concept the text keeps discussing, that refers back to the previous sentence ("This reduction in memory ..."), that replaces an awkward "the fact that" clause, or that appears alone in an otherwise clear sentence. Preserve every action. If the draft never says who does what, flag it. With an inanimate subject, replace a verb that requires intention, thought, perception, feeling, memory, or eating ("hopes", "forgets", "swallows") with a verb that names the literal event. Keep established technical usage, such as a model learning or a function expecting input, and keep a component as the subject of the operation it performs. A result or figure may be the subject of a [reporting verb](../claims/verb-ladder.md) ("the results suggest"). Follow [voice](../conventions/voice.md) and [information flow](other-sentence-orders.md).

## Details

Nominalizations often end in -tion, -ment, -ance, or -ing. List the paragraph's main verbs to locate an obscured action. Repeated use of "is", "has", or "leads to" is a reason to inspect the sentences, not proof that they need rewriting. Abstract subjects may be the actual topic of theoretical work.

Before converting a noun to a verb, identify every action asserted and check that each survives. A shorter sentence can retain the main event while dropping the operation that caused it. Do not assign an unidentified operation to the authors merely to obtain an active sentence.

Distinguish literal software operations and conventional technical usage from verbs that attribute intention, perception, feeling, memory, or eating. A component may perform an operation; an informal description that attributes a decision to a specification may instead need the actual actor. Use the draft's account of the event, without inferring new program behavior or blame for a failure. Preserve uncertainty about the actor when it matters.

## Examples

- Bad: The scheduler performs the assignment of jobs to idle workers.
- Good: The scheduler assigns jobs to idle workers.
