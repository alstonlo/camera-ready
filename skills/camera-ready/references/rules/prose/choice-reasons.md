# State the reasons for a choice

Unless the omitted items are obvious, replace "for various reasons" or a list's final "etc." with the draft's reasons or items, or flag the gap. When a claim depends on an unexplained method choice, give the draft's reason or flag its absence; do not ask authors to justify routine choices whose role is clear. A reason for a choice is not evidence that the choice caused a result ([causal language](../claims/causal-language.md)).

## Details

Check whether the draft explains the following when they are central to the method:

- Transformations that are not standard in the field.
- Steps used to prepare samples.
- Instrument settings.
- Software capabilities.

To decide whether a choice needs a reason, consider what a researcher in the field but outside the subfield would need to know. If the draft never states a procedure's overall purpose, follow [definitions and procedures](definitions-procedures.md).

## Examples

**Edit 1.** Suppose a claim depends on the rinse duration and the draft states elsewhere that shorter rinses leave salt on the glass.

> **Before.** We rinse each slide for 5 min.

> **After.** We rinse each slide for 5 min because shorter rinses leave salt on the glass.

**Edit 2.** Say the draft describes the cloth as preventing scratches on the jars.

> **Before.** We label the jars 1, 2, 3, etc., and wrap them in cloth for various reasons.

> **After.** We label the jars 1, 2, 3, etc., and wrap them in cloth to prevent scratches.

The jar labels follow a counting sequence, so readers can supply the next labels.

**Edit 3.** Imagine the draft lists heat, light, and moisture as the factors that damage the film.

> **Before.** We protect the film from heat, light, etc.

> **After.** We protect the film from heat, light, and moisture.
