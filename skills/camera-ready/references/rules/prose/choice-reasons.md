# State the reasons for a choice

Unless the omitted items are obvious, replace "for various reasons" or a list's final "etc." with the draft's reasons or items, or flag the gap. When a stated claim depends on an unexplained method choice, give the draft's reason or flag the missing reason. Do not ask authors to justify routine choices whose role is clear. A reason for a choice is not evidence that the choice caused a result. For causal claims, follow [causal language](../claims/causal-language.md).

## Details

Check whether the draft explains the following when they are central to the method:

- Transformations that are not standard in the field.
- Steps used to prepare samples.
- Instrument settings.
- Software capabilities.

To decide whether a choice needs a reason, consider what a researcher in the field but outside the subfield would need to know. If the draft never states a procedure's overall purpose, follow [definitions and procedures](definitions-procedures.md).

## Examples

> **Context.** In this draft, the text states that amber bottles block light that breaks down the dye.
>
> **Bad.** We use amber bottles for various reasons.
>
> **Good.** We use amber bottles because they block light that breaks down the dye.

> **Context.** In this draft, the text identifies three factors that damage the film: heat, light, and moisture.
>
> **Bad.** We protect the film from heat, light, etc.
>
> **Good.** We protect the film from heat, light, and moisture.

> **Context.** In this draft, a stated claim depends on rinsing for 5 min, and text elsewhere explains that shorter rinses leave salt on the glass.
>
> **Bad.** We rinse each slide for 5 min.
>
> **Good.** We rinse each slide for 5 min because shorter rinses leave salt on the glass.

> **Context.** In this draft, the text states that wrapping the jars in cloth prevents scratches.
>
> **Bad.** We label the jars 1, 2, 3, etc., and wrap them in cloth for various reasons.
>
> **Good.** We label the jars 1, 2, 3, etc., and wrap them in cloth to prevent scratches.
