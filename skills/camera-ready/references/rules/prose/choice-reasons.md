# State the reasons for a choice

Unless the omitted items are obvious, replace "for various reasons", or an "etc." that ends a list, with the reasons or items the draft supplies; if the draft omits them, flag the gap. When an unexplained method choice is central to a stated claim, give the rationale the draft states, or flag the missing rationale. Do not request justifications for routine choices whose role is clear. A stated reason for a choice is not evidence that the choice caused a result; claims of cause follow [causal language](../claims/causal-language.md).

## Details

Check whether the draft explains nonstandard transformations, sample-preparation steps, instrument settings, and software capabilities that are central to the method. Judge whether a choice needs a rationale from the perspective of a researcher in the field who works outside the subfield. When the overall purpose of a procedure is missing, follow [definitions and procedures](definitions-procedures.md).

## Examples

> **Context.** The draft states that amber bottles block light that breaks down the dye.
>
> **Bad.** We use amber bottles for various reasons.
>
> **Good.** We use amber bottles because they block light that breaks down the dye.

> **Context.** The draft lists heat, light, and moisture as the three damaging exposures.
>
> **Bad.** We protect the film from heat, light, etc.
>
> **Good.** We protect the film from heat, light, and moisture.

> **Context.** The claim depends on the 5 min rinse, and the draft states elsewhere that shorter rinses leave salt on the glass.
>
> **Bad.** We rinse each slide for 5 min.
>
> **Good.** We rinse each slide for 5 min because shorter rinses leave salt on the glass.

> **Context.** The draft states that the cloth prevents scratches.
>
> **Bad.** We label the jars 1, 2, 3, etc., and wrap them in cloth for various reasons.
>
> **Good.** We label the jars 1, 2, 3, etc., and wrap them in cloth to prevent scratches.
