# Resolve references without changing the claim's status

Make each pronoun, demonstrative, comparative, and shorthand noun phrase identify one referent from the text alone. Name ambiguous baselines or previous runs with the draft's labels and reuse those names. The first sentence under a section title or run-in paragraph title must make sense without the title. Restore context lost during editing, and flag connections you cannot resolve rather than choosing a referent.

Replace an ambiguous "this", "that", or "it" by naming the referent the draft supports. Preserve its status and evaluation: a conjecture is not a finding, and a change is not necessarily an improvement. Allow bare "This" to introduce an interpretation after one unambiguous claim, as in "This means that", "This is because", and "This suggests that".

## Details

Cover the heading and read the first sentence alone. Check that it still names its subject.

## Examples

**Edit 1.** Suppose the draft names run A as the comparison for run C.

> **Before.** Runs A and B use dry sand. Run C drains faster than the earlier run. The earlier run leaves a puddle.

> **After.** Runs A and B use dry sand. Run C drains faster than run A. Run A leaves a puddle.

**Edit 2.**

> **Before.** **Copper mesh.** This catches the larger particles.

> **After.** **Copper mesh.** Copper mesh catches the larger particles.

**Edit 3.** Suppose an earlier cut removed the identification of the red switch as the power switch, and the panel also has a blue switch.

> **Before.** Press the switch. This control then turns the pump off.

> **After.** Press the red power switch. This control then turns the pump off.

The edit restores the lost identification, giving "This control" a clear referent.

**Edit 4.** Suppose the draft identifies the conjecture as the reason for the next test.

> **Before.** We conjecture that salt weakens the seal; the lid also bends. This motivates the next test.

> **After.** We conjecture that salt weakens the seal; the lid also bends. This conjecture motivates the next test.

**Edit 5.** Suppose the draft leaves open whether higher pressure is beneficial.

> **Before.** We replaced the valve, and the pressure rose. This improvement triggered the alarm.

> **After.** We replaced the valve, and the pressure rose. This rise in pressure triggered the alarm.

**Edit 6.** Suppose the draft identifies the dish in the warm room as the one that froze.

> **Before.** We left one dish of water in a cold room and one in a warm room. It froze overnight. This suggests that the heating failed.

> **After.** We left one dish of water in a cold room and one in a warm room. The dish in the warm room froze overnight. This suggests that the heating failed.

The final "This" can stay because it refers unambiguously to the freezing of the dish in the warm room.
