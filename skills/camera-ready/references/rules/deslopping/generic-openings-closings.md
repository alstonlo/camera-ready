# Delete generic openings and closings

Delete opening or closing filler that could fit any paper in the field (such as "The future looks bright"), along with popularity claims that sound factual ("There has been much recent interest in X"). Merely naming the topic does not make a sentence necessary. If the draft gives a specific reason the topic matters, use that reason instead. If readers need background first, follow [brief setup](../prose/paragraph-development.md).

## Details

This rule has no further details.

## Examples

**Example 1.**

> **Bad.** The new bus route stops at the hospital every 10 minutes. The future of public transit looks bright.
>
> **Good.** The new bus route stops at the hospital every 10 minutes.

**Example 2.** The draft gives the roof-heating explanation elsewhere.

> **Bad.** Roof paint has attracted much attention. We test white paint.
>
> **Good.** Dark roofs absorb sunlight and heat the rooms below. We test white paint.

**Example 3.** Readers need the definition of dormancy to understand this draft's study question.

> **Bad.** Gardening is an exciting field. Dormancy is a period without growth. We test whether cold ends dormancy.
>
> **Good.** Dormancy is a period without growth. We test whether cold ends dormancy.
