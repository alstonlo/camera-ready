# Delete generic openings and closings

Delete opening or closing filler that could fit any paper in the field ("In the ever-evolving field of materials science", "The future looks bright"), including popularity claims that sound factual ("There has been much recent interest in X"). Merely naming the topic does not make a sentence necessary. If the draft gives a specific reason the topic matters, use that reason instead; if readers need prerequisite context first, follow the rule on [brief setup](../prose/paragraph-development.md).

## Details

This rule has no further details.

## Examples

> **Bad.** The new bus route stops at the hospital every 10 minutes. The future of public transit looks bright.
>
> **Good.** The new bus route stops at the hospital every 10 minutes.

> **Bad.** In the dynamic world of gardening, progress never stops. We test three watering schedules.
>
> **Good.** We test three watering schedules.

> **Bad.** There is growing interest in roof paint. We measure the reflectance of three roof paints.
>
> **Good.** We measure the reflectance of three roof paints.

> **Context.** The draft states that dark roofs absorb sunlight and heat the rooms below.
>
> **Bad.** Roof paint has attracted much attention. We test white paint.
>
> **Good.** Dark roofs absorb sunlight and heat the rooms below. We test white paint.

> **Context.** The intended readers need the definition of dormancy before the study question.
>
> **Bad.** Gardening is an exciting field. Dormancy is a period without growth. We test whether cold ends dormancy.
>
> **Good.** Dormancy is a period without growth. We test whether cold ends dormancy.
