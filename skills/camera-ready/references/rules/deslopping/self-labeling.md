# Make importance clear without self-labeling significance

Delete a sentence that merely labels an earlier result important or surprising ("This is the key result"). When the draft explains its importance, use that explanation to organize the passage; if not, delete the empty label without adding an explanation. Keep a word of genuine authorial stance that introduces a claim ("Interestingly,").

## Details

A label placed after a result supplies no mechanism or comparison. Delete it when it merely tells the reader that an earlier statement matters.

Stance expressed while introducing a claim serves a different function. Deleting empty labels after results does not permit deleting the authors' own reaction as they introduce a claim.

## Examples

- Bad: The covered pond loses half as much water as the uncovered pond. This is the key result.
- Good: The covered pond loses half as much water as the uncovered pond.
