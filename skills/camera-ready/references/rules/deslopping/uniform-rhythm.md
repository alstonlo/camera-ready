# Vary repetitive syntax through the ideas' connections

Look for successive sentences with the same opening, clause pattern, or length, and paragraphs built the same way. Connect sentences through relations already stated in the draft, following the [topic string](../prose/topic-string.md): a shared object, condition, time, or consequence. Do not invent causal, temporal, or contrastive links. Let those connections determine sentence structure instead of changing lengths at random. Preserve [parallel wording for parallel ideas](../prose/parallel-form.md) and [consistent technical terms](../conventions/terminology.md). Keep sentences complete and literal. Do not add [writerly asides](../prose/writerly-asides.md), contrasts, or emphasis, or vary a passage in ways that reduce clarity.

## Details

Sentence length is only one cue. Different lengths can conceal repeated grammatical constructions, and consecutive unusual constructions can distract even when their wording differs.

Choose an opening from the relationship to the preceding idea. Reordering around that relationship can vary the structure while preserving the topic string. Keep matching constructions when they make a real similarity easier to see.

## Examples

- Bad: The pump fills a tank. The tank supplies a fountain. The fountain drains into a pond.
- Good: The pump fills a tank that supplies a fountain. The fountain drains into a pond.
