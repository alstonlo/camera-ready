# Describe the work without narrating manuscript revisions

In the paper, replace sentences that describe manuscript revisions (diff-anchored prose), such as "In this revised version, we clarify" or "As suggested by the reviewer", with the scientific content directly. Revision history belongs in the response letter or change log. Keep all added explanations, evidence, and justifications, and keep substantive comparisons between method versions when part of the study.

## Details

This rule has no further details.

## Examples

> **Bad.** As requested by the reviewer, we add that each jar contains 200 mL of water.
>
> **Good.** Each jar contains 200 mL of water.

> **Bad.** In this revision, we explain that we weigh twice because the scale drifts.
>
> **Good.** We weigh twice because the scale drifts.

> **Context.** The draft reports a study that compares two pump designs.
>
> **Bad.** As requested by the reviewer, we note that the second pump design uses less power than the first.
>
> **Good.** The second pump design uses less power than the first.

> **Context.** In this example, the label "Reviewer response" marks an excerpt from the response letter, and the label "Manuscript" marks an excerpt from the paper.
>
> **Bad.** Reviewer response: We added the calibration test. Manuscript: In this revision, we report a calibration error of 2%.
>
> **Good.** Reviewer response: We added the calibration test. Manuscript: We report a calibration error of 2%.
