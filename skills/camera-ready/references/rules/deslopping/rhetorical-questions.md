# Replace rhetorical questions used as transitions with their answers

Replace a transition question ("How well does this scale?") with its answer as the topic sentence. A question used only to connect paragraphs is not a permitted opening, even if the next sentence answers it. Keep a research question that states what the text tests; the [paragraph rule](../prose/paragraphs.md) governs answer placement. Keep a mid-paragraph question only when it reframes details already given and the next sentence answers it. For a leading question that presumes agreement, apply the [verb ladder](../claims/verb-ladder.md) to its implied claim.

## Details

An opening research question identifies the specific relation or outcome being tested. A transition question only prompts readers to continue into the next paragraph; an answer in the next sentence does not turn it into a research question.

Within a paragraph, a question can direct attention to a relation among details already introduced. Keep it only when it performs that function and receives an immediate answer. Do not add questions to vary the prose.

A leading question can invite agreement without supplying evidence. Apply the verb ladder to the implied claim; turning the question into a confident assertion does not supply its missing support.

## Examples

The preceding paragraph describes the new window seals, and this paragraph uses a question only to lead into its finding.

- Bad: What do the seals mean in practice? With the seals, the room stays above 18 °C overnight.
- Good: With the seals, the room stays above 18 °C overnight.
