# Replace rhetorical questions used as transitions with their answers

When a question only introduces the next topic, delete it and make its answer the paragraph's first sentence. Never open a paragraph with a question that merely links paragraphs, even if the next sentence answers it. Keep a research question that states what the text tests, placing its answer as the [paragraph rule](../prose/paragraph-development.md) directs. Keep a mid-paragraph question only if it turns preceding details into a puzzle and the next sentence answers it. For questions that presume agreement, see Details.

## Details

When a question assumes that readers agree with a claim ("Does this not prove...?"), rewrite it as a statement of that claim and choose its verb from the [verb ladder](../claims/verb-ladder.md).

## Examples

**Edit 1.** The experiment tests whether salt delays germination.

> **Before.** What should we ask next? Does salt delay germination? In our tests, it delays germination by 2 days.
>
> **After.** Does salt delay germination? In our tests, it delays germination by 2 days.

**Edit 2.**

> **Before.** What do we learn? The soil is wet, but the leaves wilt. Why can these occur together? The blocked roots cannot absorb water.
>
> **After.** The soil is wet, but the leaves wilt. Why can these occur together? The blocked roots cannot absorb water.

**Edit 3.** In these tests, the measurements reported in the draft show reduced water loss.

> **Before.** Do these tests not prove that the mulch prevents water loss?
>
> **After.** The mulch reduces water loss in these tests.
