# Repair pause punctuation between a claim and its evidence

Read the clause before each colon or semicolon on its own. If it states no specific fact ("Annealing helps: conductivity rises by 40%"), join the claim to its evidence in words or delete the vague clause when the evidence states the whole claim. Replacing the mark with a period does not fix the vague clause. If the clause states a specific fact, keep a semicolon before a related fact or a colon before an explanation or example. Never use a colon that implies an unsupported identity, cause, or complete list. A colon may also introduce a list, a displayed formula under [mathematical punctuation](../conventions/math-prose.md), a quoted definition, or a label, unless it separates a vague claim from its evidence.

Restore any missing verb in a phrase after a comma ("adds 3 ms, below the limit" becomes "adds 3 ms, which is below the limit"). Keep appositives, clear participial phrases, and parallel phrases that share an earlier verb ("the tank holds 5 L, the bottle 1 L").

## Details

- Semicolons: Use one when two related independent clauses each need emphasis and belong to one thought; separate sentences would suggest a looser connection.
- Appositives: An appositive names the same thing as the preceding noun.
- Participial phrases: A phrase with a verb form such as "filling" is clear when it describes an identifiable actor or event.

## Examples

**Edit 1.**

> **Before.** The new tap is effective: it reduces water use by 18%.

> **After.** The new tap reduces water use by 18%.

**Edit 2.** Suppose the draft treats the absence of escaping water as evidence that the seal works.

> **Before.** The seal works; no water escapes during the pressure test.

> **After.** The seal works, as shown by the fact that no water escapes during the pressure test.

**Edit 3.** Suppose the draft reports that the valve opening and the lamp turning green occur together but does not explain why.

> **Before.** The valve opens: the lamp turns green.

> **After.** The valve opens; the lamp turns green.

**Edit 4.**

> **Before.** The tank holds 5 L, the bottle 1 L. The test uses 2 L, below the capacity of the tank.

> **After.** The tank holds 5 L, the bottle 1 L. The test uses 2 L, which is below the capacity of the tank.

The bottle phrase shares the verb "holds" with the tank phrase, so it already has the verb it needs.
