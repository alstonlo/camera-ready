# Name the source of a vague attribution

For "studies show", "experts believe", or "X has been shown to", name a source only when the draft cites it for that claim, usually in the same or adjacent sentence; otherwise flag the missing attribution. An explicitly hypothetical objection that the paper poses and answers needs no citation merely to identify an objector. "Some may argue" alone does not establish that case. Follow [prior-work rules](../story/prior-work.md) for who holds each position.

## Details

A citation elsewhere in the draft does not show that its source supports this claim. Name that source only when the draft links it to the claim. If several nearby sources could be intended, flag the ambiguity.

The hypothetical exception applies when the paper explicitly considers and answers a possible objection without claiming that researchers hold it. Do not infer that status from a modal phrase about unnamed people, and do not add an objector to make the passage seem argumentative.

Follow the prior-work rule for who holds a reported position and how strongly they express it. Naming a source does not require narrating the authors' intellectual history.

## Examples

The draft cites Zhao et al. (2023) for this forecast and does not assert the forecast itself.

- Bad: Experts believe that most new city roofs will be white by 2040 (Zhao et al., 2023).
- Good: Zhao et al. (2023) expect that most new city roofs will be white by 2040.
