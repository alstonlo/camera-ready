# Name the cited source when an attribution is vague

For vague attributions such as "studies show", "experts believe", or "X has been shown to", name a source only when the draft cites one for that claim, following the [citation-marker](../conventions/citation-markers.md) and [prior-work](../story/prior-work-comparisons.md) rules. If no source is cited for that claim, flag the missing attribution. An objection clearly framed as hypothetical and then answered needs no cited objector. The phrase "Some may argue" alone does not provide that framing.

## Details

The cited source is usually in the same or an adjacent sentence. Flag the ambiguity if several nearby sources could support the claim and the draft does not identify which one applies.

## Examples

**Edit 1.** Suppose the draft cites Lin (2021) for the germination claim.

> **Before.** Studies show that salt delays germination. As a hypothetical objection, we ask whether the delay is due to low temperature; the trial holds temperature constant.

> **After.** Lin (2021) reports that salt delays germination. As a hypothetical objection, we ask whether the delay is due to low temperature; the trial holds temperature constant.

The objection needs no named source because the authors explicitly pose and answer it as a hypothetical question.
