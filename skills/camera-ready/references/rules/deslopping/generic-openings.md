# Delete generic openings and closings

Delete opening or closing filler that could fit any paper in the field ("In the ever-evolving field of deep learning", "The future looks bright"), including apparently factual popularity claims ("There has been much recent interest in X"). Naming a topic does not make a sentence necessary. For prerequisite context, apply the rule on [brief setup](../prose/paragraphs.md). If the draft states a specific reason the topic matters, use that reason instead.

## Details

Formulas about popularity and importance can sound plausible while contributing nothing to the paper's argument. A sentence is not necessary merely because it shares a topic noun with the next sentence.

Use the paragraph rule to decide whether a factual premise or definition is needed before the point. This page gives no separate permission to keep broad background simply because the next sentence mentions the same field.

## Examples

- Bad: The future of public transit looks bright. The new bus route stops at the hospital every 10 minutes.
- Good: The new bus route stops at the hospital every 10 minutes.
