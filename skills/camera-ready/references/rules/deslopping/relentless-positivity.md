# Present successes and failures with comparable prominence

When the text frames every result as a success (relentless positivity or sycophantic framing), give reported failures and counterevidence comparable prominence and [calibrate each claim to its evidence](../claims/scope.md). Do not add a failure or balancing caveat, or hedge a supported finding, merely to make the tone seem cautious.

## Details

Comparable prominence does not require equal word counts or a negative statement after every success.

## Examples

> **Context.** The draft's table shows that the coating prevents rust in fresh water but fails in salt water.
>
> **Bad.** The coating prevents rust in fresh water, with results in salt water that leave room for further study.
>
> **Good.** The coating prevents rust in fresh water but fails in salt water.

> **Bad.** The seal holds at 10 °C (although it leaks at 20 °C).
>
> **Good.** The seal holds at 10 °C but leaks at 20 °C.

> **Context.** The draft reports that the seal holds at 10 °C and says nothing about prolonged use; the caveat is added only to make the tone seem cautious.
>
> **Bad.** The seal holds at 10 °C, though it may fail after prolonged use.
>
> **Good.** The seal holds at 10 °C.

> **Context.** The draft establishes that the seal holds at 10 °C and gives no contrary evidence; "may" is added only to make the tone seem cautious.
>
> **Bad.** The seal may hold at 10 °C.
>
> **Good.** The seal holds at 10 °C.
