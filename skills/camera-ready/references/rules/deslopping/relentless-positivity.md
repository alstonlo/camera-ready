# Make reported failures as noticeable as successes

When a draft presents every result as a success, give reported failures and counterevidence comparable prominence, and [match each claim to its evidence](../claims/scope.md). Never invent a failure, add a balancing caveat, or hedge a supported finding to make the tone seem cautious.

## Details

Comparable prominence does not require equal word counts for successes and failures, nor a negative statement after every success. This pattern is also called relentless positivity or sycophantic framing.

## Examples

**Example 1.** In the draft, the results table shows that the coating prevents rust in fresh water but fails in salt water.

> **Bad.** The coating prevents rust in fresh water, with results in salt water that leave room for further study.
>
> **Good.** The coating prevents rust in fresh water but fails in salt water.

**Example 2.**

> **Bad.** The seal holds at 10 °C (although it leaks at 20 °C).
>
> **Good.** The seal holds at 10 °C but leaks at 20 °C.

**Example 3.** The draft reports that the seal holds at 10 °C and mentions no tests of prolonged use. The caveat was added only to make the tone seem cautious.

> **Bad.** The seal holds at 10 °C, though it may fail after prolonged use.
>
> **Good.** The seal holds at 10 °C.

**Example 4.** The draft establishes that the seal holds at 10 °C and reports no contrary evidence. The hedge was added only to make the tone seem cautious.

> **Bad.** The seal may hold at 10 °C.
>
> **Good.** The seal holds at 10 °C.
