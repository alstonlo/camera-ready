# Preserve the author's voice

Keep the draft's [varied rhythm](repetitive-syntax.md) and the authors' genuine opinions and reactions. Never add opinions, reactions, emphasis, sentence fragments, opposing positions, or unstated details. After rewriting, [verify that the meaning is unchanged](../prose/deletion-test.md). Follow conventions on [voice](../conventions/active-voice.md), [tense](../conventions/tense.md), and [terminology](../conventions/term-consistency.md). Never treat first-person "we", present tense for the text or prior work, or repeated technical terms as defects merely because generated prose uses them. [Match confidence to evidence](../claims/scope.md) without adding hedges.

## Details

This rule has no further details.

## Examples

**Example 1.** The draft reads as in Good. An editor splits the second sentence into short sentences of similar length.

> **Bad.** The pump stops. Water then drains. The water leaves the upper tank. The water enters the lower tank.
>
> **Good.** The pump stops. Water then drains from the upper tank into the lower tank.

**Example 2.** The draft reports when the seeds sprout without judging the importance of the finding. An editor adds emphasis.

> **Bad.** Crucially, the seeds sprout after 3 days.
>
> **Good.** The seeds sprout after 3 days.

**Example 3.** The draft calls the finding surprising. An editor deletes the authors' reaction.

> **Bad.** The dry seeds sprout first.
>
> **Good.** Surprisingly, the dry seeds sprout first.

**Example 4.** The draft reads as in Good. An editor shortens the second sentence to a fragment that has no subject or verb.

> **Bad.** The pump stops. No flow.
>
> **Good.** The pump stops. Water no longer flows.

**Example 5.** The draft states only that the lid stays shut. An editor adds an unstated comparison with earlier attempts.

> **Bad.** Unlike all earlier attempts, our lid stays shut.
>
> **Good.** Our lid stays shut.

**Example 6.** The draft does not state what the lid is made of. An editor adds the detail "copper".

> **Bad.** The copper lid stays shut.
>
> **Good.** The lid stays shut.

**Example 7.** The draft consistently uses "we". An editor substitutes "the authors" to avoid an alleged AI pattern.

> **Bad.** The authors find that the seeds sprout first.
>
> **Good.** We find that the seeds sprout first.

**Example 8.** The draft describes its sections in the present tense. An editor switches to the past tense to avoid an alleged AI pattern.

> **Bad.** Section 4 described the seed trays.
>
> **Good.** Section 4 describes the seed trays.

**Example 9.** The draft calls the object "the tray" throughout. An editor introduces "receptacle" for variety.

> **Bad.** The tray holds six seeds. The receptacle stays dry.
>
> **Good.** The tray holds six seeds. The tray stays dry.

**Example 10.** The draft establishes that the tray holds six vials. An editor adds "may" to sound cautious.

> **Bad.** The tray may hold six vials.
>
> **Good.** The tray holds six vials.
