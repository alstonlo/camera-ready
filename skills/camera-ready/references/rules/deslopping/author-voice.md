# Preserve the author's voice

Keep the draft's [varied rhythm](repetitive-syntax.md) and the authors' genuine opinions and reactions; never add opinions, reactions, emphasis, sentence fragments, opposing positions, or unstated details. [Verify that each rewrite keeps the meaning](../prose/deletion-test.md). Never treat ["we"](../conventions/active-voice.md), [present tense](../conventions/tense.md) for the text or prior work, or [repeated technical terms](../conventions/term-consistency.md) as defects merely because generated prose uses them. [Matching a claim's confidence to its evidence](../claims/scope.md) adds no opinion.

## Details

This rule has no further details.

## Examples

**Edit 1.** Suppose After is the authors' original wording.

> **Before.** The pump stops. Water then drains. The water leaves the upper tank. The water enters the lower tank.

> **After.** The pump stops. Water then drains from the upper tank into the lower tank.

Before is an editor's rewrite; After restores the authors' sentence rhythm.

**Edit 2.** Imagine the draft makes no judgment about the finding's importance.

> **Before.** Crucially, the seeds sprout after 3 days.

> **After.** The seeds sprout after 3 days.

Before adds the editor's judgment; After restores the authors' account.

**Edit 3.** Say the authors describe this finding as surprising in the draft.

> **Before.** The dry seeds sprout first.

> **After.** Surprisingly, the dry seeds sprout first.

Before is an editor's version that omits the authors' reaction; After restores it.

**Edit 4.** Suppose the authors use first-person "we" throughout the draft.

> **Before.** The authors find that the seeds sprout first.

> **After.** We find that the seeds sprout first.

**Edit 5.** Imagine both sentences describe the same object, which the draft calls "the tray" throughout.

> **Before.** The tray holds six seeds. The receptacle stays dry.

> **After.** The tray holds six seeds. The tray stays dry.
