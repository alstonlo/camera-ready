# Apply prose rules to ornate words, actions written as nouns, counts, and lists

Use [literal wording](../prose/literal-wording.md) for ornate words such as "delve", "underscore", "showcase", and "pivotal", keeping an ornate word when it is precise; [character and action](../prose/character-action.md) for clauses that express several actions as nouns; [direct content](../prose/direct-content.md) for deferred counts; and [list structure](../prose/list-structure.md) for bulleted arguments.

## Details

This rule has no further details.

## Examples

**Edit 1.** Suppose the draft uses "pivotal quantity" in its technical sense from statistics.

> **Before.** We delve into a pivotal quantity for the mean.

> **After.** We study a pivotal quantity for the mean.

Here "pivotal" is part of a technical term, so replacing it with a plainer adjective could change the meaning.

**Edit 2.**

> **Before.** We perform the collection and measurement of the seeds.

> **After.** We collect and measure the seeds.

**Edit 3.** Say the draft reports elsewhere that the leaves curl and the stems bend.

> **Before.** We make two observations, which we discuss later.

> **After.** The leaves curl, and the stems bend.

**Edit 4.** Imagine the draft contains two bullet points.

> **Before.**
> - The seal leaks.
> - Water reaches the contacts because the seal leaks.

> **After.** The seal leaks, so water reaches the contacts.
