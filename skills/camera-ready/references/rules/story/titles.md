# Flag titles that are unclear or overstate the contribution

Flag a title that hides the subject, has two reasonable readings, overstates the contribution, or uses an acronym that readers outside the subfield would not know ([acronym rule](../conventions/acronyms.md)). Suggest a revision that keeps the body's claim strength and conditions, leave the title unchanged, and follow venue requirements.

## Details

Read the title on its own, as readers see it in search results. A short, established symbol may be clear, but a long formula can make the title hard to read. The title need not take a particular grammatical form: a statement of the finding, a question, two parts joined by a colon, or a playful phrase all work if the title identifies the contribution accurately.

## Examples

**Edit 1.** The study examines the use of sound to detect cracks in ceramic cups.

> **Before.** A new approach to testing ceramics
>
> **After.** Detecting cracks in ceramic cups with sound

**Edit 2.** The birds carry cameras, and the draft uses radio tags to locate them.

> **Before.** Tracking birds with cameras
>
> **After.** Tracking birds that carry cameras

**Edit 3.** Reduced leakage is reported only for the tested pipes.

> **Before.** Preventing all pipe leaks
>
> **After.** Reducing leaks in the tested pipes
