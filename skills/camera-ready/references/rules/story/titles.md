# Flag titles that are unclear or overstate the contribution

Flag a title that hides the subject, omits searchable terms for what distinguishes the work, has two reasonable readings, contains needless wording, overstates or understates the contribution, or uses an acronym unfamiliar outside the subfield ([acronym rule](../conventions/acronyms.md)). Suggest a revision that keeps the body's claim strength and conditions. Leave the title unchanged, and follow venue requirements.

## Details

Read the title on its own, as readers see it in search results. A short, established symbol may be clear, but a long formula can make the title hard to read. No particular grammatical form is required: a finding, question, two parts joined by a colon, or playful phrase can work if the title identifies the contribution accurately. Flag needless wording, such as an empty lead-in ("An investigation into"), a subtitle that repeats the main title, or secondary detail that crowds out the subject.

## Examples

**Edit 1.** Suppose the study examines the use of sound to detect cracks in ceramic cups. Propose this title to the author.

> **Before.** A new approach to testing ceramics

> **After.** Detecting cracks in ceramic cups with sound

**Edit 2.** Imagine the birds carry cameras, and the draft uses radio tags to locate them. Suggest this title to the author.

> **Before.** Tracking birds with cameras

> **After.** Tracking birds that carry cameras

**Edit 3.** If the draft reports reduced leakage only for the tested pipes, propose this title to the author.

> **Before.** Preventing all pipe leaks

> **After.** Reducing leaks in the tested pipes
