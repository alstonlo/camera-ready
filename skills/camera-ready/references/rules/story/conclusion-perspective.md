# Use the conclusion to add perspective

Keep one connected restatement of each contribution under the [triage order](../cutting/triage-order.md), then state what the established findings let readers understand or do. If readers could fully understand the conclusion without reading the paper, check whether it repeats generic statements instead of interpreting the findings; being understandable on its own is not a defect. Flag such generic repetition, a conclusion that merely repeats the abstract, new supporting evidence, or a shift to a subject not introduced earlier. Future work may identify a concrete question that arises from the findings and state what answering it would establish; do not invent a research plan. Preserve the findings' scope and [limitations](../claims/limitations.md), and label [speculative implications](../claims/speculation.md). When shortening contribution restatements, repair the connections between them; do not join copied sentences that rely on context the conclusion omits.

## Details

Distinguish a new question raised by the findings from a question the study promised to answer but did not; an unanswered promise remains a [limitation](../claims/limitations.md). When connecting restated contributions, follow [term consistency](../conventions/term-consistency.md).

## Examples

The draft reports recognition gains for one accent and asks whether those gains hold across accents; Good is the proposed closing for the author.

> Bad: Recognition improves for the tested accent. Future work will extend the evaluation.
>
> Good: Recognition improves for the tested accent. Evaluating other accents would establish whether this gain holds across accents.

The draft establishes that the tested lid halves evaporation and thus doubles the interval between refills under the tested conditions; Good is the proposed conclusion for the author.

> Bad: The tested lid halves evaporation. Water conservation is important.
>
> Good: The tested lid halves evaporation. Under the tested conditions, this doubles the interval between refills.

The draft's abstract states only that the tested filter removes sand; the discussion explains that dissolved salt remains, so the filter alone cannot make the water drinkable. Good is the proposed conclusion for the author.

> Bad: The tested filter removes sand.
>
> Good: The tested filter removes sand. Dissolved salt remains, so the filter alone cannot make the water drinkable.

The draft presents faster drying outdoors as an untested implication of the measured indoor result; Good is the proposed conclusion for the author.

> Bad: The fan halves indoor drying time. It will also shorten outdoor drying time.
>
> Good: The fan halves indoor drying time. It may also shorten outdoor drying time.

The draft promised to test the sensor in rain but reports only dry-weather tests; Good is the proposed conclusion for the author.

> Bad: The sensor detects smoke in dry weather. Rain detection is a new question for future work.
>
> Good: The sensor detects smoke in dry weather. The promised rain test remains incomplete.

The draft establishes that two insulation layers lower heat loss and that the outer layer sheds rain; Good is the proposed connected restatement for the author.

> Bad: Two insulation layers lower heat loss. The latter also sheds rain.
>
> Good: Two insulation layers lower heat loss. The outer layer also sheds rain.

The draft establishes that the brace reduces bending, and only the conclusion mentions a vibration test. Good is the proposed closing after the author moves that test and its support to the body.

> Bad: The brace reduces bending. A new vibration test also finds less shaking.
>
> Good: The brace reduces bending.

The draft studies pipe leakage only, but its conclusion shifts to bird migration. Good is the proposed closing for the author, who must decide whether to introduce that separate subject elsewhere.

> Bad: The seal stops leaks in the tested pipes. Bird migration also deserves study.
>
> Good: The seal stops leaks in the tested pipes.
