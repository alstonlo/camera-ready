# Use the conclusion to explain what the findings mean

In the conclusion, keep one statement of each contribution under the [triage order](../cutting/triage-order.md). Connect these statements, then state what the established findings let readers understand or do. A conclusion may be understandable without reading the paper, as long as it explains what the findings mean. Flag a conclusion that instead repeats general statements, merely repeats the abstract, gives new supporting evidence, or introduces a new subject. Future work may state a specific question that the findings raise and what answering it would establish, but do not invent a research plan. Preserve the findings' scope and [limitations](../claims/limitations.md), and label [speculative implications](../claims/speculation.md). When shortening restated contributions, repair the connections between them. Do not join copied sentences that depend on omitted context.

## Details

Distinguish a new question that the findings raise from a question that the study promised to answer but did not. A promise that the study did not fulfill remains a [limitation](../claims/limitations.md). When connecting the restated contributions, use the same term for the same concept, following [term consistency](../conventions/term-consistency.md).

## Examples

**Example 1.** A draft reports better speech recognition for one accent and asks whether the gain holds across accents. The Good version is a closing proposed to the author.

> **Bad.** Recognition improves for the tested accent. Future work will extend the evaluation.
>
> **Good.** Recognition improves for the tested accent. Evaluating other accents would establish whether this gain holds across accents.

**Example 2.** A draft establishes that a lid halves evaporation and thus doubles the interval between refills under the tested conditions. The Good version is a conclusion proposed to the author.

> **Bad.** The tested lid halves evaporation. Water conservation is important.
>
> **Good.** The tested lid halves evaporation. Under the tested conditions, this reduction in evaporation doubles the interval between refills.

**Example 3.** A draft's abstract states only that the tested filter removes sand. Its discussion explains that dissolved salt remains, so the filter alone cannot make the water drinkable. The Good version is a conclusion proposed to the author.

> **Bad.** The tested filter removes sand.
>
> **Good.** The tested filter removes sand. Dissolved salt remains, so the filter alone cannot make the water drinkable.

**Example 4.** A draft measures drying time only indoors and infers, without testing, that the fan also shortens drying time outdoors. The Good version is a conclusion proposed to the author.

> **Bad.** The fan halves indoor drying time. It will also shorten outdoor drying time.
>
> **Good.** The fan halves indoor drying time. It may also shorten outdoor drying time.

**Example 5.** A draft promised to test a smoke sensor in rain, but it reports tests only in dry weather. The Good version is a conclusion proposed to the author.

> **Bad.** The sensor detects smoke in dry weather. Whether it detects smoke in rain is a new question for future work.
>
> **Good.** The sensor detects smoke in dry weather. The promised test in rain remains incomplete.

**Example 6.** A draft establishes that two insulation layers lower heat loss and that the outer layer sheds rain. The Good version is a connected restatement proposed to the author.

> **Bad.** Two insulation layers lower heat loss. The latter also sheds rain.
>
> **Good.** Two insulation layers lower heat loss. The outer layer also sheds rain.

**Example 7.** A draft establishes that a brace reduces bending, but it mentions a vibration test only in the conclusion. The Good version is a closing proposed after the author moves the test and its support to the body.

> **Bad.** The brace reduces bending. A new vibration test also finds less shaking.
>
> **Good.** The brace reduces bending.

**Example 8.** A draft studies only pipe leakage, but its conclusion introduces bird migration. The Good version is a closing proposed to the author, who must decide whether to introduce bird migration elsewhere.

> **Bad.** The seal stops leaks in the tested pipes. Bird migration also deserves study.
>
> **Good.** The seal stops leaks in the tested pipes.
