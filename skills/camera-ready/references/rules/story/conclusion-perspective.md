# Use the conclusion to explain what the findings mean

In the conclusion, keep one statement of each contribution under the [triage order](../cutting/triage-order.md). Connect these statements, then state what the established findings let readers understand or do. A conclusion may be understandable without reading the paper, as long as it explains what the findings mean. Flag a conclusion that instead repeats general statements, merely repeats the abstract, gives new supporting evidence, or introduces a new subject. Future work may state a specific question that the findings raise and what answering it would establish, but do not invent a research plan. Preserve the findings' scope and [limitations](../claims/limitations.md), and label [speculative implications](../claims/speculation.md). When shortening restated contributions, repair the connections between them. Do not join copied sentences that depend on omitted context.

## Details

Distinguish a new question that the findings raise from a question that the study promised to answer but did not. A promise that the study did not fulfill remains a [limitation](../claims/limitations.md). When connecting the restated contributions, use the same term for the same concept, following [term consistency](../conventions/term-consistency.md).

## Examples

**Edit 1.** Suppose the authors already ask in the draft whether the recognition gain extends to other accents.

> **Before.** Recognition improves for the tested accent. Future work will extend the evaluation.

> **After.** Recognition improves for the tested accent. Evaluating other accents would establish whether this gain holds across accents.

**Edit 2.** Imagine this conclusion repeats the abstract's finding, while the discussion says that dissolved salt remains and prevents the filter alone from making the water drinkable.

> **Before.** The tested filter removes sand.

> **After.** The tested filter removes sand. Dissolved salt remains, so the filter alone cannot make the water drinkable.

**Edit 3.** Say the draft promises a test in rain but reports tests only in dry weather.

> **Before.** The sensor detects smoke in dry weather. Whether it detects smoke in rain is a new question for future work.

> **After.** The sensor detects smoke in dry weather. The promised test in rain remains incomplete.

**Edit 4.** Suppose the draft identifies the outer layer as the one that sheds rain.

> **Before.** Two insulation layers lower heat loss. The latter also sheds rain.

> **After.** Two insulation layers lower heat loss. The outer layer also sheds rain.
