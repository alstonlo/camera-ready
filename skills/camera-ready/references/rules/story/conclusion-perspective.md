# Use the conclusion to add perspective

Keep one connected restatement of each contribution under the [triage order](../cutting/triage-order.md), then state what the established findings let readers understand or do. If readers could fully understand the conclusion without reading the paper, check whether it repeats generic statements instead of interpreting the findings. Flag such generic repetition, a conclusion that merely repeats the abstract, new supporting evidence, or a change to a subject not introduced earlier; being understandable on its own is not a defect. Future work may identify a concrete question that arises from the findings and what answering it would establish; do not invent a research plan. Preserve the findings' scope and [limitations](../claims/limitations.md), and label [speculative implications](../claims/speculation.md). When shortening the restatements of contributions, repair the connections between them; do not join copied sentences that rely on context the conclusion omits.

## Details

A conclusion that could have been written without reading the findings may be generic repetition. This test does not ask you to make a self-contained conclusion harder to understand.

Synthesize established findings rather than presenting a last-minute experiment, comparison, or justification. Flag a substantial new topic so that the author can introduce it earlier; do not move it between sections.

For future work, identify the finding that creates the open question and what answering it would establish. Distinguish a question the findings newly reveal from one the study promised but failed to answer; the latter remains a limitation.

Retain stable technical terms when connecting contribution restatements. A coherent synthesis need not repeat the abstract's sentences or every implementation detail.

## Examples

The draft reports recognition gains for one accent and asks whether those gains hold across accents; Good is the proposed closing for the author.

- Bad: Recognition improves for the tested accent. Future work will extend the evaluation.
- Good: Recognition improves for the tested accent. Evaluating other accents would establish whether this gain holds across accents.
