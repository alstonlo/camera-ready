# Open with the problem

Name the paper's actual problem in its opening. If readers need context first, apply the [brief-setup rule](../prose/paragraphs.md). Check that the opening predicts the problem and scope that the paper develops. Delete a [generic opening](../deslopping/generic-openings.md); naming the topic alone does not establish a problem. Flag missing motivation rather than inventing an opening example.

## Details

Under the paragraph rule, a factual premise or definition may come first when it helps readers recognize the problem. A claim that the field is popular does not supply that premise merely by mentioning its topic.

A motivating case must illustrate the actual problem rather than serve as a detachable anecdote. Its placement follows the same brief-setup rule as other opening context.

Flag an opening that frames a narrower or different problem than the paper develops. Revise the wording only when the draft makes the intended focus clear.

## Examples

- Bad: Transportation plays an important role in modern society. Buses cannot pass each other on the village's narrow bridge.
- Good: Buses cannot pass each other on the village's narrow bridge.
