# Open with the problem

If readers need context first, apply the [brief-setup rule](../prose/paragraph-development.md). Delete any [generic opening](../deslopping/generic-openings-closings.md). If the draft never says why the problem matters, flag the missing motivation instead of inventing an example. For a mismatched opening, see Details.

## Details

Check that the opening describes the same problem, at the same scope, as the rest of the paper. When the opening describes a different or broader problem, revise it only if the draft makes the intended focus clear. Otherwise, flag the mismatch. A sentence that only names the topic does not state a problem. An example in the opening must illustrate the problem that the paper addresses. An anecdote that readers could remove without losing any part of that problem does not meet this requirement.

## Examples

**Edit 1.**

> **Before.** An ice lens is a layer of ice within soil. Soil science is a growing field. Ice lenses lift the tested road surface during freezing.

> **After.** An ice lens is a layer of ice within soil. Ice lenses lift the tested road surface during freezing.

The definition stays before the problem because readers need to know what an ice lens is to understand the road damage.

**Edit 2.** Suppose the study covers only leaks in household water pipes.

> **Before.** Urban water loss is difficult to prevent.

> **After.** Leaks in household water pipes are difficult to prevent.
