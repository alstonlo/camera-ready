# Open with the problem

Name what the reader cares about in the first sentence. If readers need context to understand the problem, apply the [brief-setup rule](../prose/paragraph-development.md). Delete any [generic opening](../deslopping/generic-openings-closings.md). If the draft never says why the problem matters, flag the missing motivation rather than invent an example. If the opening describes a different, broader, or narrower problem than the rest of the paper, revise it under the [rule on scope](../claims/scope.md) only when the draft makes the intended focus clear; otherwise, flag the mismatch.

## Details

A sentence that only names the topic does not state a problem. An opening example must illustrate the paper's problem; an anecdote that readers could remove without losing any part of that problem does not qualify.

## Examples

**Edit 1.**

> **Before.** An ice lens is a layer of ice within soil. Soil science is a growing field. Ice lenses lift the tested road surface during freezing.

> **After.** An ice lens is a layer of ice within soil. Ice lenses lift the tested road surface during freezing.

The definition stays because readers need it to understand the road damage.

**Edit 2.** Suppose the study covers only leaks in household water pipes.

> **Before.** Urban water loss is difficult to prevent.

> **After.** Leaks in household water pipes are difficult to prevent.
