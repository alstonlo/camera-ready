# Compare prior work fairly on named dimensions

End each paragraph or group of paragraphs about prior work with a checkable difference in an assumption, condition, scope, metric, measured result, cost, or guarantee. Describe each source only as the draft does; flag missing context or a missing difference rather than supplying it. Explain both sides enough for readers to judge the comparison, stating each source's contribution and assumptions before criticizing it. Do not write "ignores" or "fails to" for a difference in scope unless the draft shows failure. Preserve [credit](../cutting/protected-sentences.md), and keep a source's tentative verb such as "suggests" rather than strengthening it to "shows". Keep each position attributed to whoever holds it, including concessions and reported objections; a source's claim must not become the paper's assertion. For survey synthesis, see Details. A comparison that is vague on both sides is the [false concession](../deslopping/false-concession.md) pattern.

## Details

A brief summary or isolated excerpt from a source may lack the context needed to support a detailed criticism.

In a survey, end each group of related sources by stating the pattern, disagreement, or gap they establish; organizing established knowledge does not require claiming a new discovery.

For proprietary products, retain the distinction between documented capabilities and undisclosed internals. Do not infer an unknown mechanism or assume that a product's existence invalidates a research contribution.

## Examples

> **Context.** The draft states that Lee et al. deliberately limited their study to summer rainfall; Good is the proposed revision for the author.
>
> **Bad.** Lee et al. ignore winter rainfall; we examine rainfall in both summer and winter.
>
> **Good.** Lee et al. examine only summer rainfall; we examine rainfall in both summer and winter.

> **Context.** The draft states that Lee's screen blocks adult flies and that the new screen also blocks small flies; Good is the proposed ending for the author.
>
> **Bad.** Lee's screen blocks adult flies. Our screen differs from Lee's.
>
> **Good.** Lee's screen blocks adult flies. Our screen also blocks small flies.

> **Context.** The draft credits Lee with measuring heat loss in dry wool and limits the present comparison to wet wool; Good is the proposed revision for the author.
>
> **Bad.** Lee's measurements do not cover wet wool; ours do. Lee measures heat loss in dry wool.
>
> **Good.** Lee measures heat loss in dry wool. Lee's measurements do not cover wet wool; ours do.

> **Context.** The draft attributes a tentative interpretation to Lee and reports the authors' direct measurements separately; Good is the proposed revision for the author.
>
> **Bad.** Lee shows that cracks may explain the leakage; we measure leakage directly.
>
> **Good.** Lee suggests that cracks may explain the leakage; we measure leakage directly.

> **Context.** The draft reports Lee's concession about coating cost rather than endorsing that judgment; Good is the proposed revision for the author.
>
> **Bad.** Lee argues that the coating lasts longer. Although it costs too much, Lee favors it; we compare measured costs.
>
> **Good.** Lee argues that the coating lasts longer. Although Lee concedes that it costs too much, Lee favors it; we compare measured costs.

> **Context.** The draft reports that Lee raises and answers an objection about heavy batteries, whereas the present study tests light batteries; Good is the proposed revision for the author.
>
> **Bad.** Heavy batteries rule out flight. Lee rejects this objection; we test light batteries.
>
> **Good.** Lee considers the objection that heavy batteries rule out flight. Lee rejects this objection; we test light batteries.

> **Context.** The survey draft reports that Lee and Rao both measure only summer rainfall; Good is the proposed synthesis for the author.
>
> **Bad.** Lee measures summer rainfall. Rao also measures summer rainfall.
>
> **Good.** Lee measures summer rainfall. Rao also measures summer rainfall. Both studies leave winter rainfall unmeasured.

> **Context.** The draft documents that a commercial filter removes salt and notes that its internal design is undisclosed; Good is the proposed comparison for the author.
>
> **Bad.** The commercial filter removes salt with the same membrane as ours; our membrane design is public.
>
> **Good.** The commercial filter removes salt, but its internal design is undisclosed; our membrane design is public.
