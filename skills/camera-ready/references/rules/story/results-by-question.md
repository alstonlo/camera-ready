# Organize results around research questions

State which questions the Results section answers and connect each to a contribution. If a heading names only a dataset, sample, site, or setup, flag it and propose one that states a question or finding. If one result motivates the next experiment, keep that sequence and keep the interpretation beside the result. Distinguish [measurement from inference](../claims/hedges.md). Flag an experiment for cutting if it answers no identifiable question, and flag any proposed section move or reordering. Also flag a result with no described procedure or an evaluation procedure with no reported use, and delete neither.

## Details

When you propose a heading, keep the claim strength and conditions from the body text. If the draft does not connect a procedure to a result, do not conclude that the authors did not perform the work. Routine infrastructure, such as shared equipment, may support several results without producing a separate result of its own. A paper based on proofs needs no empirical methods section. To check more broadly whether readers can reproduce the work, follow [definitions and procedures](../prose/definitions-procedures.md).

## Examples

> **Context.** The draft reports that shade reduces water loss in the tested ferns. The Good version proposes a heading for the author.
>
> **Bad.** Fern experiment
>
> **Good.** Shade reduces water loss in the tested ferns

> **Context.** The draft names reduced refill frequency as its contribution. It states that the tank trial tests whether a lid reduces water loss enough to reduce refill frequency. The Good version proposes a Results opening for the author.
>
> **Bad.** We test whether the lid reduces water loss.
>
> **Good.** We test whether the lid reduces water loss enough to reduce refill frequency.

> **Context.** In the draft, cracks in the first coat motivate adding a second coat. The draft describes all methods before it reports any results, which hides that sequence. The Good version proposes an order for the author.
>
> **Bad.** Single-coat method; double-coat method; single-coat result: cracks suggest a second coat is needed; double-coat result
>
> **Good.** Single-coat method; single-coat result: cracks suggest a second coat is needed; double-coat method; double-coat result

> **Context.** The draft states that the weighing procedure in Section 2 measures the reported mass loss. The Good version proposes a revision for the author.
>
> **Bad.** The sample loses 3 g.
>
> **Good.** The sample loses 3 g, as measured by the weighing procedure in Section 2.

> **Context.** Section 4 of the draft reports leakage measured by the pressure test. The passage that describes the test does not say what the test measures. The Good version proposes a revision for the author.
>
> **Bad.** We also perform a pressure test.
>
> **Good.** We also perform a pressure test to measure the leakage reported in Section 4.

> **Context.** The draft proves the bound in Theorem 2 and needs no empirical Methods section. The Good version proposes a revision for the author.
>
> **Bad.** Every path has length at most 6 m.
>
> **Good.** Every path has length at most 6 m (Theorem 2).
