# Organize results by questions, not by experiments

State the questions that the Results section answers and connect each to a contribution. For a heading that names only a dataset or setup, flag a proposed heading that states a question or finding and keeps the body's claim strength and conditions. Preserve a sequence in which one result motivates the next experiment, and keep the interpretation of that result next to it; distinguish [measurement from inference](../claims/hedges.md). Flag experiments with no identifiable question as candidates for cutting, and flag proposed section moves or reordering. Flag a result with no described procedure or an evaluation procedure with no reported use; delete neither.

## Details

Group independent experiments by the knowledge they supply, rather than merely by dataset or execution order.

For dependent experiments, explain what the earlier result established and why the next experiment follows. A combined Results and Discussion section can preserve this logic without treating conjecture as a finding.

A dependency does not justify describing every trial the authors attempted. The contribution determines what belongs, and the request determines what may be cut.

When a measurement has no stated collection or computation procedure, flag the missing connection. Likewise, flag an evaluation procedure with no stated role in the findings. A missing connection does not establish that the work was not performed or that the passage is irrelevant.

Routine infrastructure may support several results without producing its own result. Check that results and procedures correspond where a connection is missing; this is not a request for an exhaustive audit of reproducibility. A proof-based paper need not have an empirical methods section.
