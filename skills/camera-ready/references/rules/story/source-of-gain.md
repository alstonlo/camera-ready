# Identify what produces the gain

For a method with several parts, the results must show which part produces the improvement. If an ablation or equivalent evidence is missing, flag the missing evidence and credit only the combination under the [rule on causal language](../claims/causal-language.md). Treat an untested explanation as [speculation](../claims/speculation.md). Flag any obvious alternative explanation that the text does not address. When the draft raises a counterexample, conflicting result, or rival explanation, flag the draft's answer to it if that answer gives neither evidence nor a reason the objection does not apply; restating the claim is not an answer.

## Details

Other evidence may isolate a component's effect without an experiment labeled as an ablation. Do not insist on that label or imply that every component must be necessary.

A response to an objection can supply contrary evidence, explain a relevant difference in assumptions, or acknowledge a remaining limitation. It must address the stated challenge accurately rather than substitute an easier one.

Focus on objections visible in the draft that affect its main interpretation. Do not add hypothetical reviewer objections or concessions made only out of habit. If the response lacks a needed fact or argument, flag that gap without inventing a rebuttal or weakening a supported claim.

## Examples

The draft compares bare soil with soil covered by both compost and straw and reports no test of either alone; Good is the proposed revision for the author.

- Bad: Compost reduces water loss by 15%.
- Good: Compost and straw together reduce water loss by 15%.
