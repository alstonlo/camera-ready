# Keep the argument linear

Order material by the argument's logic, not by when each part was done or how much effort it took. Apply the [rule against alternation](../prose/topic-order.md) across sections, and flag a repair that would move material between sections. The core idea may appear in every section. Keep a second round of methods and results that depends on an earlier result ([section functions](section-functions.md)), reminders of needed definitions and [section context](section-openings.md), and the protected statements of each contribution. Give each component and counterargument space proportional to its role in the core claims. Explain each side of a comparison under the [rule on comparing with prior work](prior-work-comparisons.md). Under the [triage order](../cuts/triage-order.md), keep a rejected approach in the main text only as a negative result, stating its flaw before its details.

## Details

Discuss related minor objections together so repetition does not exaggerate their importance. Explain substantial counterarguments in enough detail for readers to judge them. Preserve [counterevidence](../cuts/protected-sentences.md) against the paper's claims. A negative result reports that a tested approach failed. The triage order ranks material for cutting to meet a length limit and protects one statement of each contribution in the abstract, introduction, and conclusion.

## Examples

**Edit 1.** Suppose the rainfall measurements are independent of the wind measurements. The revision is a proposal to the author.

> **Before.** 2 Measuring rainfall; 3 Measuring wind; 4 Correcting rainfall measurements

> **After.** 2 Measuring rainfall; 3 Correcting rainfall measurements; 4 Measuring wind

**Edit 2.** Say the rigid seal is a rejected approach reported as a negative result.

> **Before.** We test the rigid seal on pipes bent to five angles. The rigid seal cracks at every angle.

> **After.** The rigid seal cracks at every tested bend angle. We test the rigid seal on pipes bent to five angles.

**Edit 3.** Suppose both tests use the temperature definition. The revision is a proposal to the author.

> **Before.** 2 Leak test; 3 Temperature definition; 4 Flexible seal motivated by the leak test; 5 Flexible-seal test

> **After.** 2 Temperature definition; 3 Leak test; 4 Flexible seal motivated by the leak test; 5 Flexible-seal test

The definition moves first because both tests use it. The flexible-seal sections stay after the leak test because that result supplies the reason for the flexible seal.
