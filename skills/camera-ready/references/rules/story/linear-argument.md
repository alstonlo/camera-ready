# Keep the argument linear

Order material by the argument's logic, not by when each part was done or how much effort it took. Apply the [rule against alternation](../prose/topic-order.md) across sections: do not return to an earlier topic after leaving it (A, B, A). The core idea may appear in every section. Keep a second round of methods and results that depends on an earlier result ([section functions](section-functions.md)), reminders of needed definitions, and protected restatements of contributions. Give each component and counterargument space proportional to its role in the core claims. Follow the [rule on comparing with prior work](prior-work-comparisons.md) when explaining each side of a comparison. Under the [triage order](../cutting/triage-order.md), keep a rejected approach in the main text only as a negative result, and state its flaw before its details. Flag moves of material between sections or changes in section order.

## Details

Discuss related minor objections together so that repeating them does not make them seem more important. Explain substantial counterarguments in enough detail for readers to judge them. Preserve [counterevidence](../cutting/protected-sentences.md), meaning evidence against the paper's claims. A negative result is a report that a tested approach failed. The triage order ranks material for cutting when a text exceeds a length limit, and it protects one statement of each contribution in the abstract, introduction, and conclusion.

## Examples

**Example 1.** The draft describes independent rainfall and wind measurements. The editor proposes a section order for the author to review.

> **Bad.** 2 Measuring rainfall; 3 Measuring wind; 4 Correcting rainfall measurements
>
> **Good.** 2 Measuring rainfall; 3 Correcting rainfall measurements; 4 Measuring wind

**Example 2.** Both sentences are in one paragraph about the rigid seal, a rejected approach that the draft keeps as a negative result.

> **Bad.** We test the rigid seal on pipes bent to five angles. The rigid seal cracks at every angle.
>
> **Good.** The rigid seal cracks at every tested bend angle. We test the rigid seal on pipes bent to five angles.

**Example 3.** In the draft, the result of the leak test is the reason the authors develop and test a flexible seal. Both tests use the definition of temperature given in the draft. The editor proposes a section order for the author to review.

> **Bad.** 2 Leak test; 3 Temperature definition; 4 Flexible seal motivated by the leak test; 5 Flexible-seal test
>
> **Good.** 2 Temperature definition; 3 Leak test; 4 Flexible seal motivated by the leak test; 5 Flexible-seal test
