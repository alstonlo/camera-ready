# State contributions as claims with their support

For each contribution, state a checkable claim and name the section that supports it. Turn a contribution that names only an activity into a claim under the [underclaiming rule](../claims/underclaiming.md), at the strength the [verb ladder](../claims/verb-ladder.md) allows. Flag contributions without evidence. Treat a major result that supports no contribution as a candidate for the [spine test](../cutting/spine-test.md); do not delete it or change the list of contributions. When each contribution names its supporting section, delete a roadmap that names no content, under the [signpost rule](../prose/signposts.md).

## Details

Check both directions: each contribution needs support, and each major result should have an identifiable role in the contributions. A list of activities alone does not state what the paper establishes.

As an optional diagnostic, consider what evidence could distinguish the claim from its opposite and what readers learn from accepting it. Do not invent a controversy, and do not reject a result because it seems unsurprising in hindsight.

A dataset, system, tool, replication, or negative result can be a contribution. State its supported property or finding without demanding a new mechanism or theoretical discovery.

## Examples

The draft shows that the coating prevents rust in the saltwater test in Section 4; Good is the proposed contribution for the author.

- Bad: We test the coating in saltwater (Section 4).
- Good: We show that the coating prevents rust in the saltwater test (Section 4).
