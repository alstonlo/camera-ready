# State contributions as claims with their support

For each contribution, state a checkable claim and name the section that supports it. Turn a contribution that names only an activity into a claim under the [underclaiming rule](../claims/underclaiming.md), at the strength the [verb ladder](../claims/verb-ladder.md) allows. Flag contributions without evidence. Treat a major result that supports no contribution as a candidate for the [spine test](../cutting/spine-test.md); do not delete it or change the list of contributions. When each contribution names its supporting section, delete any roadmap that names no content, under the [signpost rule](../prose/signposts.md).

## Details

To test a contribution claim, ask what evidence could distinguish it from its opposite and what readers learn from accepting it; a result need not be surprising to be a contribution. A resource, tool, replication, or negative result counts as a contribution through the property or finding the draft establishes for it, even without a new mechanism or theoretical discovery.

## Examples

In Section 4, the draft shows that the coating prevents rust in the saltwater test; Good is the proposed contribution for the author.

> **Bad.** We test the coating in saltwater (Section 4).
>
> **Good.** We show that the coating prevents rust in the saltwater test (Section 4).

Section 3 contains the draft's evidence for this contribution.

> **Bad.** The screen blocks all flies in our trial.
>
> **Good.** The screen blocks all flies in our trial (Section 3).

Section 4 supports the contribution; the roadmap names no additional content.

> **Bad.** The coating prevents rust in the saltwater test (Section 4). Section 4 presents the results.
>
> **Good.** The coating prevents rust in the saltwater test (Section 4).

Section 2 of the draft establishes that the released map locates every public well in the town; Good is the proposed contribution for the author.

> **Bad.** We compile a map of public wells (Section 2).
>
> **Good.** Our map locates every public well in the town (Section 2).

Section 5 of the draft reports that the independent repeat trial finds no reduction in leaks; Good is the proposed contribution for the author.

> **Bad.** We repeat the seal test independently (Section 5).
>
> **Good.** Our independent repeat trial finds no reduction in leaks (Section 5).
