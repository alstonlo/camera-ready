# Explain why the evidence supports the claim

When readers outside the subfield may not see why a measurement or reason supports a claim, explain the connecting inference and check that its assumptions hold in this study. Use only the support that the draft gives, or flag the missing connection; do not supply a plausible reason that the draft does not state. Apply the rule on [scope](../claims/scope.md) to claims that exceed what the measurement establishes, and the rule on [formal support](../claims/evidence-kind.md) to inferences from a theorem.

## Details

A connecting principle that readers dispute needs support; an uncontroversial step that readers share need not be spelled out. For causal inferences, follow [causal language](../claims/causal-language.md).

## Examples

> **Context.** The draft states that the sensor rings a bell only when the tank is full; Good is the explanation proposed to the author.
>
> **Bad.** The bell rang, so the tank was full.
>
> **Good.** The bell rang, so the tank was full: the sensor rings the bell only when the tank is full.

> **Context.** The draft states that the sample contains only sand and water and that drying removes only water; Good is the proposed explanation for the author.
>
> **Bad.** Drying reduces the sample's mass by 3 g, so it contained 3 g of water.
>
> **Good.** Drying reduces the sample's mass by 3 g, so it contained 3 g of water: the sample contains only sand and water, and drying removes only water.

> **Context.** The draft states that the green indicator lights only when the lid is sealed; the comparison of times needs no further principle. Good is the proposed explanation for the author.
>
> **Bad.** Filling takes 4 minutes and draining takes 6 minutes, so draining takes longer. The indicator is green, so the lid is sealed.
>
> **Good.** Filling takes 4 minutes and draining takes 6 minutes, so draining takes longer. The indicator is green, so the lid is sealed: the indicator lights only when the lid is sealed.

> **Context.** Some readers dispute whether this dye turns red only in acid, and the draft cites Lee's calibration to support that link; Good is the proposed explanation for the author.
>
> **Bad.** The dye turns red, so the liquid is acidic.
>
> **Good.** The dye turns red, so the liquid is acidic: Lee's calibration shows that this dye turns red only in acid.
