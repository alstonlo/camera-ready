# Explain why the evidence supports the claim

When readers outside the subfield may not see why a measurement or reason supports a claim, explain the connecting inference and check that its assumptions hold in this study. Use only the support that the draft gives, or flag the missing connection; do not supply a plausible reason that the draft does not state. Apply the rule on [scope](../claims/scope.md) to claims that exceed what the measurement establishes, and the rule on [formal support](../claims/formal-informal.md) to inferences from a theorem.

## Details

Evidence that a reason is true differs from evidence that it supports the conclusion. Check whether the connecting principle applies under the conditions of this study.

A disputed principle needs support; a shared, uncontroversial step need not be spelled out. A claim about causation still needs the evidence required by the causal-language rule.

An explanation must not turn an observed association into a mechanism. If the necessary link is absent, identify that missing link in the flag.

## Examples

The draft states that the sensor rings a bell only when the tank is full; Good is the explanation proposed to the author.

- Bad: The bell rang, so the tank was full.
- Good: The bell rang, so the tank was full: the sensor rings the bell only when the tank is full.
