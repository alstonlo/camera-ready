# Explain why the evidence supports the claim

When readers outside the subfield may not see how the evidence supports a claim, explain the reasoning and check that its assumptions hold in this study. Flag a connection the draft omits. Follow [scope](../claims/scope.md) for claims that exceed what the measurement establishes, and [formal support](../claims/evidence-kind.md) for conclusions from a theorem.

## Details

A principle that connects evidence to a claim needs support if readers dispute it. You need not explain a step readers already understand and accept. For conclusions about cause and effect, follow [causal language](../claims/causal-language.md).

## Examples

**Edit 1.** Suppose the draft states elsewhere that the indicator turns green only when the lid is sealed.

> **Before.** Filling takes 4 minutes and draining takes 6 minutes, so draining takes longer. The indicator is green, so the lid is sealed.

> **After.** Filling takes 4 minutes and draining takes 6 minutes, so draining takes longer. The indicator turns green only when the lid is sealed. The indicator is green, so the lid is sealed.

The time comparison needs no explanation beyond the two durations.

**Edit 2.** Suppose readers dispute whether this dye turns red only in acid, and the draft cites Lee's calibration as support.

> **Before.** The dye turns red, so the liquid is acidic.

> **After.** Lee's calibration shows that this dye turns red only in acid. The dye turns red, so the liquid is acidic.
