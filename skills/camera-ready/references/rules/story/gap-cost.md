# State the gap's cost

State the specific gap and its cost: what readers lose, pay, cannot do, or cannot understand because of it. "No prior work studies X" states only the gap, not its cost. Replace "little is known" with the specific unknown when the draft supplies it. Flag a missing gap or cost. If stating the gap would require rewriting the objectives or contributions, flag the proposed rewrite instead of making it. The cost may be a question readers cannot answer or the value of extending earlier work to a new case; it need not be a flaw in prior work or a hypothesis, so do not invent either. Distinguish established consequences from [speculative applications](../claims/speculation.md).

## Details

Explain what existing work establishes before identifying exactly where that knowledge stops. A vague claim that nothing is known can contradict the literature that the paper itself cites.

For conceptual work, identify the larger question that readers cannot answer without the missing knowledge. For applied work, distinguish obtaining the knowledge needed for an intervention from performing the intervention.

An objectives list may describe measurements without stating the question they answer. Flag the missing question or proposed reorganization; do not convert a contribution list into motivation.

A resource, replication, descriptive measurement, or extension can have a clear purpose without a new hypothesis about a mechanism. A prospective application remains prospective even when it helps motivate the problem.

## Examples

The draft states that the unknown load limit prevents deciding whether the bridge can carry the new buses; Good is the proposed revision for the author.

- Bad: The bridge's maximum safe load is unknown.
- Good: The bridge's maximum safe load is unknown, so we cannot determine whether the bridge can carry the new buses.
