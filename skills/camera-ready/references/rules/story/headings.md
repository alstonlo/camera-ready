# Use headings to show how the argument is organized

Make each parent heading describe all its subsections, and give headings under the same parent [parallel form](../prose/parallel-form.md). Flag a parent with only one subheading, a fourth-level heading, or a very short section when it makes the argument hard to follow. Propose a heading that states its section's finding with the body's claim strength and conditions, and any change to how sections are grouped or nested; do not make these changes yourself. Keep distinctions that help readers, and follow venue requirements.

## Details

If headings under the same parent mix objects, operations, and findings, check why before proposing replacements. Their wording may be inconsistent, or the sections may serve different purposes. Do not invent a second subheading when a parent has only one. Name the affected headings when flagging a change to grouping or nesting, such as merging sections or removing a heading level. For headings that name only a dataset, sample, site, or setup, follow [results by question](results-by-question.md).

## Examples

**Edit 1.** Suppose the user study is limited to screen-reader navigation.

> **Before.** 3 User study; 3.1 Screen-reader navigation

> **After.** 3 User study of screen-reader navigation

Propose this change to the author because it removes a heading level.

**Edit 2.**

> **Before.** 2 Drying; 2.1 Drying samples; 2.2 Weighing samples

> **After.** 2 Sample preparation; 2.1 Drying samples; 2.2 Weighing samples

**Edit 3.**

> **Before.** 2.1 Mixing the solution; 2.2 The solution is cooled

> **After.** 2.1 Mixing the solution; 2.2 Cooling the solution

**Edit 4.** Suppose the section reports that shade reduces water loss, with evidence limited to the tested ferns.

> **Before.** Water loss

> **After.** Shade reduces water loss in the tested ferns

Propose this heading to the author.
