# Use headings to show how the argument is organized

Make each parent heading describe all its subsections, and give headings under the same parent [parallel form](../prose/parallel-form.md). Flag a parent with only one subheading, a fourth-level heading, or a very short section when it makes the argument hard to follow. Propose, but do not make, a heading that states its section's finding with the body's claim strength and conditions, and any change to how sections are grouped or nested. Keep distinctions that help readers, and follow venue requirements.

## Details

When headings under the same parent mix objects, operations, and findings, check why before you propose new headings. The authors may have worded the headings inconsistently, or the sections may serve different purposes. When a parent has only one subheading, do not invent a second subheading to fix it. When you flag a change to how sections are grouped or nested, such as merging sections or removing a heading level, name the affected headings. For headings that name only a dataset, sample, site, or setup, follow [results by question](results-by-question.md).

## Examples

**Edit 1.** Suppose the user study is limited to screen-reader navigation.

> **Before.** 3 User study; 3.1 Screen-reader navigation

> **After.** 3 User study of screen-reader navigation

Propose this change to the author because it removes a heading level.

**Edit 2.**

> **Before.** 2 Drying; 2.1 Drying samples; 2.2 Weighing samples

> **After.** 2 Sample preparation; 2.1 Drying samples; 2.2 Weighing samples

**Edit 3.**

> **Before.** 2.1 Mixing the solution; 2.2 The solution is cooled

> **After.** 2.1 Mixing the solution; 2.2 Cooling the solution

**Edit 4.** Imagine the section reports that shade reduces water loss, with evidence limited to the tested ferns.

> **Before.** Water loss

> **After.** Shade reduces water loss in the tested ferns

Offer this heading to the author for review before changing it.
