# Make headings reveal the argument's structure

Use headings whose subheadings fit under them; give sibling headings (those under one parent) [parallel form](../prose/parallel-form.md). Flag any of the following when it obscures the argument: a level with only one subheading, a fourth-level heading, or a very short section. When a heading could state its section's finding, flag a proposed heading that keeps the body's claim strength and conditions; do not rewrite the heading yourself. Flag, rather than make, changes to the hierarchy, such as merging sections or removing a level; preserve distinctions that help readers, and follow venue requirements.

## Details

Read each set of sibling headings as a description of their parent's contents. Siblings that mix objects, operations, and findings may reflect inconsistent wording or sections with different functions; determine which before renaming.

A lone subheading may indicate an unnecessary level, and a fourth level may indicate that paragraphs would suffice. Do not invent a second subheading or flatten distinctions that help readers.

A structural flag should identify the affected headings and the change for the author to review.

A proposed finding heading must retain the body's qualifications and conditions. For dataset-only headings, follow [results by question](results-by-question.md).
