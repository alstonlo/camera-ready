# State each finding before its representative numbers

Describe the pattern in words: whether the effect is consistent, where it grows, and where it fails. For each claim, give one representative headline result, not an atypical best case, with its baseline and any interval or range needed to interpret it. Use the table to establish the pattern. Outside the [abstract](section-functions.md), refer to an existing table for the remaining values; remove duplicated numbers only when the prose keeps the comparison, [uncertainty](../claims/variability.md), and [statistical conclusion](../claims/statistical-significance.md). State the finding first in [quantified comparisons](../prose/quantified-comparatives.md) too. Flag numbers that support no claim and, when the request permits, move them only to an existing table or appendix.

## Details

A headline number is a sentence's main result. The sentence may still contain several numerals, such as values before and after a change.

## Examples

**Edit 1.**

> **Before.** The average stopping distance falls from 18 m to 12 m, showing that the new brakes shorten stopping distance.

> **After.** The new brakes shorten stopping distance. The average stopping distance falls from 18 m to 12 m.

**Edit 2.** Suppose Table 2 contains all six drying times.

> **Before.** The fan halves drying time for all three fabrics: from 80 to 40 minutes for wool, from 60 to 30 minutes for cotton, and from 20 to 10 minutes for nylon (Table 2).

> **After.** The fan halves drying time for all three fabrics. For cotton, drying time falls from 60 to 30 minutes (Table 2).

**Edit 3.** Say Table 3 reports reductions of 20%, 18%, and 2%. The draft identifies 18% as typical and notes that the 2% reduction misses the 10% target.

> **Before.** The fan reduces drying time by 20% in the best trial (Table 3).

> **After.** The fan typically reduces drying time by 18%, but the 2% reduction in one trial misses the 10% target (Table 3).

**Edit 4.** Suppose Table 4 contains both the individual daily yields and the summary statistics.

> **Before.** Mean yield is 12 kg (95% CI, 9–15 kg) with fertilizer and 10 kg (95% CI, 8–12 kg) without it; the difference is not statistically significant. Daily yields with fertilizer are 9, 10, 12, 14, and 15 kg (Table 4).

> **After.** Mean yields with and without fertilizer do not differ significantly: 12 kg (95% CI, 9–15 kg) with fertilizer and 10 kg (95% CI, 8–12 kg) without it (Table 4).

The intervals and statistical conclusion stay because the two means alone could overstate the evidence for a difference.
