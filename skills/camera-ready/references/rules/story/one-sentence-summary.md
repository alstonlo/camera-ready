# Summarize the argument in one sentence

Use the five-line summary as the basis: "Because the problem matters and existing work leaves a gap, we propose an idea that our result supports." If it names no gap, flag the missing problem. If it requires unrelated problems, flag competing focuses without choosing one or inventing a link.

## Details

Build this sentence from the five-line summary of the problem, the gap and its cost, the idea, the evidence, and the implication. The sentence pattern is a test of the argument, not wording that the paper must use. When the sentence requires unrelated problems, do not choose one of the problems, and do not invent a claim that connects them. Decide whether the ideas connect by reading what they say, not by counting conjunctions such as "but". A gap need not begin with "but", and several clauses that begin with "but" may describe obstacles to answering one question rather than separate problems.

## Examples

**Edit 1.** Suppose the draft states elsewhere that existing window screens fail to keep small flies out.

> **Before.** Keeping flies out protects stored food; we propose a finer window screen that blocks all flies in our trial.

> **After.** Keeping flies out protects stored food, but existing window screens let small flies through; we propose a finer window screen that blocks all flies in our trial.

**Edit 2.** Imagine the draft gives no connection between the two problems.

> **Before.** Small flies and overheating make homes uncomfortable, so we propose a finer window screen and a reflective roof paint.

> **After.** Flag for the author: The summary can be written only by naming two unrelated problems, small flies and overheating roofs, so the draft has two competing focuses.
