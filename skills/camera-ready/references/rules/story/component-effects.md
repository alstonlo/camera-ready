# Identify which part produces the effect

When a method, treatment, intervention, material, or design combines several parts or changes, the results must show which part produces the reported effect. If no evidence isolates a component's effect, flag the missing evidence and credit only the combination under the [rule on causal language](../claims/causal-language.md). Treat an untested explanation as [speculation](../claims/speculation.md). Flag any obvious alternative explanation that the text does not address. When the draft raises a counterexample, conflicting result, or rival explanation, flag the draft's reply to it if that reply gives neither evidence nor a reason why the objection does not apply; restating the claim is not an answer.

## Details

Comparisons may remove, replace, or vary one part while holding the others fixed, or vary parts together to distinguish effects and interactions. No particular design label is required; an ablation is one example. Isolating a component's effect does not establish that every component is necessary.

An answer to an objection may supply contrary evidence, point out assumptions that do not apply, or acknowledge a remaining limitation. Check that the answer addresses the objection actually raised. Focus on challenges to the main interpretation that arise from the draft itself; do not invent hypothetical reviewer objections.

## Examples

> **Context.** The draft compares bare soil with soil covered by both compost and straw, without testing either material alone; Good is the proposed revision for the author.
>
> **Bad.** Compost reduces water loss by 15%.
>
> **Good.** Compost and straw together reduce water loss by 15%.

> **Context.** The draft reports that removing the mesh while keeping the frame unchanged doubles sediment passage; Good is the proposed revision for the author.
>
> **Bad.** The framed mesh reduces sediment passage.
>
> **Good.** The mesh reduces sediment passage: removing it while keeping the frame unchanged doubles sediment passage.

> **Context.** The draft reports that the combined coating reduces leaks, but it does not test whether the resin fills pores; Good is the proposed revision for the author.
>
> **Bad.** The combined coating reduces leaks because the resin fills pores.
>
> **Good.** We conjecture that the combined coating reduces leaks because the resin fills pores.

> **Context.** The draft reports that the fertilized field also received more rain, but it never discusses rain as an explanation; Good is the proposed revision for the author.
>
> **Bad.** The fertilized field yields 20% more grain, so the fertilizer raises yield.
>
> **Good.** The fertilized field yields 20% more grain but also received more rain, so the fertilizer, the rain, or both may raise yield.

> **Context.** The draft reports that both groups start with equal moisture, which addresses the stated rival explanation; Good is the proposed revision for the author.
>
> **Bad.** The pots with covers lose less water. Unequal starting moisture cannot explain this because the pots with covers lose less water.
>
> **Good.** The pots with covers lose less water. Unequal starting moisture cannot explain this because both groups start with equal moisture.

> **Context.** The draft notes that the counterexample concerns saltwater, whereas the claim covers freshwater only; Good is the proposed revision for the author.
>
> **Bad.** The coating works in freshwater. Its failure in saltwater does not challenge this claim because the coating works in freshwater.
>
> **Good.** The coating works in freshwater. Its failure in saltwater does not challenge this claim because the claim concerns freshwater only.

> **Context.** The draft reports a failed repeat trial and states that the reason for the failure is unresolved; Good is the proposed revision for the author.
>
> **Bad.** The first trial supports the effect. The failed repeat trial does not matter because the first trial supports the effect.
>
> **Good.** The first trial supports the effect. The failed repeat trial leaves the effect uncertain because the reason for the failure is unresolved.

> **Context.** The draft reports that removing the mesh increases sediment passage even though the remaining gravel still removes some sediment; Good is the proposed revision for the author.
>
> **Bad.** The mesh improves sediment removal because no sediment can be removed without it.
>
> **Good.** The mesh improves sediment removal because removing it increases sediment passage.

> **Context.** The draft varies mesh spacing while holding the frame and flow fixed, and it reports less sediment passage at smaller spacings; Good is the proposed revision for the author.
>
> **Bad.** The frame explains the lower sediment passage at smaller mesh spacings.
>
> **Good.** The smaller mesh spacing explains the lower sediment passage while the frame and flow stay fixed.

> **Context.** The draft tests all four combinations of lid and fan and reports that the fan reduces drying time only without the lid; Good is the proposed revision for the author.
>
> **Bad.** The fan reduces drying time regardless of the lid.
>
> **Good.** The fan reduces drying time only without the lid.
