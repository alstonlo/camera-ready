# Identify which part produces the effect

When a method, treatment, intervention, material, or design combines several parts or changes, the results must show which part produces the reported effect. If no evidence separates one part's effect, flag the missing evidence and credit only the combination under [causal language](../claims/causal-language.md). Treat an untested explanation as [speculation](../claims/speculation.md). Flag any obvious alternative explanation that the draft does not address. If the draft raises a counterexample, conflicting result, or alternative explanation, check its reply. Flag the reply if it gives neither evidence nor a reason why the objection does not apply.

## Details

A comparison may remove, replace, or vary one part while keeping the others fixed. It may also vary parts together to distinguish their effects and show whether one part changes another's effect. No particular name for the design is required. An ablation, a test that removes a part, is one example. Separating one part's effect from the others does not establish that every part is necessary.

To answer an objection, authors may give evidence against it, identify assumptions that do not apply, or acknowledge a limitation that remains. Repeating the claim does not answer the objection. Check that their answer addresses the objection the draft raises. Focus on challenges to the main interpretation that arise from the draft itself. Do not invent objections that a reviewer might raise.

## Examples

**Edit 1.** The draft compares bare soil with soil covered by both materials and tests neither alone. The edit replaces "Compost reduces" with "Compost and straw together reduce" to credit the tested combination, because the comparison does not separate the materials' effects.

> **Before.** Compost reduces water loss by 15%.
>
> **After.** Compost and straw together reduce water loss by 15%.

**Edit 2.** The draft does not test whether the resin fills pores. The edit separates the reduction in leaks from its proposed explanation and introduces that explanation with "We conjecture", so that the untested mechanism is marked as uncertain.

> **Before.** The combined coating reduces leaks because the resin fills pores.
>
> **After.** The combined coating reduces leaks. We conjecture that it does so because the resin fills pores.

**Edit 3.** The draft reports equal starting moisture in both groups. The edit replaces the repeated claim that covered pots lose less water with "both groups start with equal moisture", giving evidence against the alternative explanation instead of repeating the result.

> **Before.** The pots with covers lose less water. Unequal starting moisture cannot explain this because the pots with covers lose less water.
>
> **After.** The pots with covers lose less water. Unequal starting moisture cannot explain this because both groups start with equal moisture.

**Edit 4.** The draft reports that removing the mesh increases sediment passage, although the remaining gravel still removes some sediment. The edit, a proposal to the author, replaces "no sediment can be removed without it" with "removing it increases sediment passage" to support the mesh's contribution without claiming that sediment removal requires it.

> **Before.** The mesh improves sediment removal because no sediment can be removed without it.
>
> **After.** The mesh improves sediment removal because removing it increases sediment passage.

**Edit 5.** The draft reports the difference in rainfall but does not consider its effect on yield. The edit replaces the conclusion that fertilizer raises yield with the fact that the fertilized field received more rain and the possibility that fertilizer, rain, or both explain the increase, so that the result is not attributed to fertilizer alone.

> **Before.** The fertilized field yields 20% more grain, so the fertilizer raises yield.
>
> **After.** The fertilized field yields 20% more grain, but it also received more rain. The fertilizer, the rain, or both may explain the higher yield.
