# Identify which part produces the effect

When a method, treatment, intervention, material, or design combines several parts or changes, the results must show which part produces the reported effect. If no evidence separates one part's effect, flag the missing evidence and credit only the combination under [causal language](../claims/causal-language.md). Treat an untested explanation as [speculation](../claims/speculation.md). Flag any obvious alternative explanation that the draft does not address. If the draft raises a counterexample, conflicting result, or alternative explanation, check its reply. Flag the reply if it gives neither evidence nor a reason why the objection does not apply.

## Details

A comparison may remove, replace, or vary one part while keeping the others fixed. It may also vary parts together to distinguish their effects and show whether one part changes another's effect. No particular name for the design is required. An ablation, a test that removes a part, is one example. Separating one part's effect from the others does not establish that every part is necessary.

To answer an objection, authors may give evidence against it, identify assumptions that do not apply, or acknowledge a limitation that remains. Repeating the claim does not answer the objection. Check that their answer addresses the objection the draft raises. Focus on challenges to the main interpretation that arise from the draft itself. Do not invent objections that a reviewer might raise.

## Examples

> **Context.** The draft compares bare soil with soil covered by compost and straw together. It tests neither material alone. The Good version proposes a revision for the author.
>
> **Bad.** Compost reduces water loss by 15%.
>
> **Good.** Compost and straw together reduce water loss by 15%.

> **Context.** The draft reports that twice as much sediment passes through when the mesh is removed and the frame stays unchanged. The Good version proposes a revision for the author.
>
> **Bad.** The framed mesh reduces sediment passage.
>
> **Good.** The mesh reduces sediment passage. Removing it while keeping the frame unchanged doubles sediment passage.

> **Context.** The draft reports that the combined coating reduces leaks but does not test whether the resin fills pores. The Good version proposes a revision for the author.
>
> **Bad.** The combined coating reduces leaks because the resin fills pores.
>
> **Good.** The combined coating reduces leaks. We conjecture that it does so because the resin fills pores.

> **Context.** The draft reports that the fertilized field also received more rain but never considers whether rain explains the higher yield. The Good version proposes a revision for the author.
>
> **Bad.** The fertilized field yields 20% more grain, so the fertilizer raises yield.
>
> **Good.** The fertilized field yields 20% more grain, but it also received more rain. The fertilizer, the rain, or both may explain the higher yield.

> **Context.** The draft raises unequal starting moisture as an alternative explanation and reports that both groups start with equal moisture. The Good version proposes a revision for the author.
>
> **Bad.** The pots with covers lose less water. Unequal starting moisture cannot explain this because the pots with covers lose less water.
>
> **Good.** The pots with covers lose less water. Unequal starting moisture cannot explain this because both groups start with equal moisture.

> **Context.** The draft limits its claim to freshwater but reports a failure in saltwater. The Good version proposes a revision for the author.
>
> **Bad.** The coating works in freshwater. Its failure in saltwater does not challenge this claim because the coating works in freshwater.
>
> **Good.** The coating works in freshwater. Its failure in saltwater does not challenge this claim because the claim applies only to freshwater.

> **Context.** The draft reports a failed repeat trial and states that the reason remains unresolved. The Good version proposes a revision for the author.
>
> **Bad.** The first trial supports the effect. The failed repeat trial does not matter because the first trial supports the effect.
>
> **Good.** The first trial supports the effect. The failed repeat trial leaves the effect uncertain because the reason for the failure is unresolved.

> **Context.** The draft reports that more sediment passes through when the mesh is removed, although the remaining gravel still removes some sediment. The Good version proposes a revision for the author.
>
> **Bad.** The mesh improves sediment removal because no sediment can be removed without it.
>
> **Good.** The mesh improves sediment removal because removing it increases sediment passage.

> **Context.** The draft tests different mesh spacings with the frame and flow fixed. It reports less sediment passage at smaller spacings. The Good version proposes a revision for the author.
>
> **Bad.** The frame explains the lower sediment passage at smaller mesh spacings.
>
> **Good.** Smaller mesh spacing explains the lower sediment passage when the frame and flow stay fixed.

> **Context.** The draft tests all four combinations of lid and fan. It reports faster drying with the fan only when the lid is absent. The Good version proposes a revision for the author.
>
> **Bad.** The fan reduces drying time regardless of the lid.
>
> **Good.** The fan reduces drying time only without the lid.
