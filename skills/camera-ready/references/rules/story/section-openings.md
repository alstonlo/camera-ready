# Open each section with its context, question, and answer

Connect each section's question to the preceding text by placing [familiar information before new information](../prose/old-before-new.md). State the question and answer in the first paragraph, following [paragraph opening order](../prose/paragraph-development.md); openings that only name the topic are [signposts](../prose/signposts.md). Make the introduction understandable without the abstract. Keep short reminders for readers who start at Results, Discussion, or Conclusion, including essential context when the full methods appear in supplementary material, unless a length request reaches them in the [triage order](../cutting/triage-order.md). Open a methods section with one sentence stating its key idea before the steps. Put an existing [explanatory example](../prose/definitions-procedures.md) before the general case.

## Details

You may write the question as a statement rather than a direct question. Do not add a rhetorical question just to satisfy this rule. Follow [paragraph opening order](../prose/paragraph-development.md). In a methods section, the answer can be the key idea. In a proof, the answer can be the proof strategy.

Place each section reminder according to the same paragraph opening order. The reminder can name the question, system, comparison, or essential procedure. When the full methods appear in supplementary material, keep at least this context in the main text.

## Examples

**Edit 1.** Suppose the draft reports later in this section that the lid halves water loss during boiling.

> **Before.** We test whether a lid reduces water loss during boiling.

> **After.** We test whether a lid reduces water loss during boiling. The lid halves water loss.

**Edit 2.** Imagine the Methods section explains how the lid returns condensed water to the tank to prevent evaporation.

> **Before.** We describe how the lid prevents evaporation.

> **After.** The lid prevents evaporation by returning condensed water to the tank.

**Edit 3.** Suppose this sentence opens Results, and Methods defines the schedules as feeding once or three times a day.

> **Before.** We compare fish growth under the two feeding schedules.

> **After.** We compare fish growth under the two feeding schedules: once or three times a day.

**Edit 4.** Imagine the preceding section identifies cooling time as the production limit, and this section tests a fan to shorten that time.

> **Before.** We test a fan.

> **After.** To shorten the cooling time that limits production, we test a fan.

**Edit 5.** Say this is the seal's first mention in the introduction, and the abstract defines it as a rubber ring.

> **Before.** The seal prevents leaks at the pipe joint.

> **After.** The seal, a rubber ring, prevents leaks at the pipe joint.

**Edit 6.** Imagine these sentences open Methods.

> **Before.** We attach a fan to the lid. We use airflow to shorten cooling time.

> **After.** We use airflow to shorten cooling time. We attach a fan to the lid.
