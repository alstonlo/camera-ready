# Test the argument with five lines and one sentence

Write one line each for the problem, the gap and its cost, the idea, the evidence, and the implication. Flag any line the draft does not supply. Combine the idea and evidence into core claims for the [spine test](../cutting/spine-test.md).

Combine the five lines into one test sentence: "A matters, and B is known, but C fails because D; therefore we propose E, which achieves F." The lines and sentence are notes, not text for the paper. If the sentence lists facts without a problem or gap, flag what is missing. If it requires unrelated problems, flag the competing focuses and ask which one the text addresses; do not invent a connection. Judge both cases by meaning: a gap need not use "but", and several "but" clauses may describe obstacles to answering one question.

## Details

Use only what the draft states. The gap is what existing work leaves unresolved; its cost is why that gap matters. The spine test uses the core claims to check whether each paragraph supports the argument. If the draft supplies only part of a line, write that part and flag what is missing.

## Examples

**Edit 1.** Suppose the draft says that the missing storm records leave peak water levels unknown.

> **Before.** Gap and cost: The tide gauge stops recording during storms.

> **After.** Gap and cost: The tide gauge stops recording during storms and leaves peak water levels unknown.

**Edit 2.**

> **Before.** Gap and cost: The tide gauge stops recording during storms, so flood warnings fail.

> **After.** Gap and cost: The tide gauge stops recording during storms. Flag for the author: The draft does not state why the missing records matter.

The failed flood warnings appear in the editor's summary, not in the draft. The correction removes that invented consequence from the notes.

**Edit 3.** Suppose the authors propose a lid and report that it halves evaporation in the tested tank.

> **Before.** Core claim: We propose and test a lid.

> **After.** Core claim: The lid halves evaporation in the tested tank.

**Edit 4.** Suppose the draft states elsewhere that existing window screens fail to keep small flies out.

> **Before.** Keeping flies out protects stored food; we propose a finer window screen that blocks all flies in our trial.

> **After.** Keeping flies out protects stored food, but existing window screens let small flies through; we propose a finer window screen that blocks all flies in our trial.

**Edit 5.** Suppose the draft gives no connection between the two problems.

> **Before.** Small flies and overheating make homes uncomfortable, so we propose a finer window screen and a reflective roof paint.

> **After.** Flag for the author: The summary can be written only by naming two unrelated problems, small flies and overheating, so the draft has two competing focuses.
