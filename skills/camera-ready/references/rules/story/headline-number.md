# Choose a headline number for each claim

Choose a headline number for each claim, not an unrepresentative best case. Keep its baseline and any interval or range needed to interpret it. Describe the pattern the results support, including where it fails. Refer to an existing table for the remaining values. Remove numbers that repeat the table only if the text keeps the comparison, [uncertainty](../claims/variability.md), and [statistical conclusion](../claims/statistical-significance.md).

## Details

A headline number is the main result that a sentence focuses on. The sentence may still contain several numerals, such as the values before and after a change.

## Examples

**Edit 1.** Suppose Table 2 contains all six drying times. Before:

> The fan halves drying time for all three fabrics: from 80 to 40 minutes for wool, from 60 to 30 minutes for cotton, and from 20 to 10 minutes for nylon (Table 2).

After:

> The fan halves drying time for all three fabrics. For cotton, drying time falls from 60 to 30 minutes (Table 2).

**Edit 2.** Say Table 3 reports reductions of 20%, 18%, and 2%. The draft identifies 18% as typical and notes that the 2% reduction misses the 10% target. Before:

> The fan reduces drying time by 20% in the best trial (Table 3).

After:

> The fan typically reduces drying time by 18%, but the 2% reduction in one trial misses the 10% target (Table 3).

**Edit 3.** Suppose Table 4 contains both the individual daily yields and the summary statistics. Before:

> Mean yield is 12 kg (95% CI, 9–15 kg) with fertilizer and 10 kg (95% CI, 8–12 kg) without it; the difference is not statistically significant. Daily yields with fertilizer are 9, 12, and 15 kg (Table 4).

After:

> Mean yield is 12 kg (95% CI, 9–15 kg) with fertilizer and 10 kg (95% CI, 8–12 kg) without it; the difference is not statistically significant (Table 4).

The intervals and statistical conclusion stay because the two means alone could make the evidence for a difference look stronger than it is.
