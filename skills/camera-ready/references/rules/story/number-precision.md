# Match precision to the claim

Report only the digits that the measurement and comparison support. Keep words that mark estimates or approximations. Flag unsupported precision or comparable quantities reported with different precision. Never add digits, round values, or replace numbers without the author's authorization and the source data.

## Details

Do not keep extra digits merely because software prints them. Do not require the same number of decimal places for unlike quantities, exact counts, or measurements with different uncertainty. Never append zeros to a rounded value, because a rounded value does not record the digits that rounding discarded.

## Examples

**Edit 1.** Suppose the source data report both lengths to the nearest 0.1 cm. After is a proposal to the author. Before:

> The lengths are approximately 2.300 cm and 4.1 cm.

After:

> The lengths are approximately 2.3 cm and 4.1 cm.

The stated precision does not make either length exact.

**Edit 2.** Imagine the source measurements have a precision of 0.01 g for mass and 1 cm for length. After is a proposal to the author. Before:

> The sample weighs 2.34 g and is 8.00 cm long.

After:

> The sample weighs 2.34 g and is 8 cm long.

The mass keeps two decimal places because its source measurement supports them.
