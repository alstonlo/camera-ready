# Match precision to the claim

Match reported precision to the measurement and comparison, not to the number of digits software prints. Preserve words marking estimates or approximations. Flag unsupported precision or inconsistent precision among comparable quantities; never add digits, round values, or replace numbers without the author's authorization and the necessary source data.

## Details

Do not force unlike quantities, exact counts, or measurements with different uncertainty to use the same number of decimal places. Because a rounded value does not record its discarded digits, never pad it with zeros.

## Examples

> **Context.** The draft's reporting protocol requires mean drying times rounded to whole minutes, and its source worksheet gives the rounded mean as 31 minutes; Good is the numerical correction proposed to the author.
>
> **Bad.** The paint takes 31.33 minutes to dry on average.
>
> **Good.** The paint takes 31 minutes to dry on average.

> **Context.** The draft's source data give both lengths to the nearest tenth of a centimeter, 2.3 and 4.1; Good is the numerical correction proposed to the author.
>
> **Bad.** The lengths are approximately 2.300 cm and 4.1 cm.
>
> **Good.** The lengths are approximately 2.3 cm and 4.1 cm.

> **Context.** The draft measures mass to 0.01 g and length to 1 cm; its source values are 2.34 g and 8 cm. Good is the correction proposed to the author.
>
> **Bad.** The sample weighs 2.34 g and is 8.00 cm long.
>
> **Good.** The sample weighs 2.34 g and is 8 cm long.
