# Match precision to the claim

Report only the digits that the measurement and comparison support. Keep words that mark estimates or approximations. Flag unsupported precision or comparable quantities reported with different precision. Never add digits, round values, or replace numbers without the author's authorization and the source data.

## Details

Do not keep extra digits merely because software prints them. Do not require the same number of decimal places for unlike quantities, exact counts, or measurements with different uncertainty. Never append zeros to a rounded value, because a rounded value does not record the digits that rounding discarded.

## Examples

**Example 1.** The draft's reporting protocol requires mean drying times rounded to whole minutes. Its source worksheet gives the rounded mean as 31 minutes. The Good version is a numerical correction proposed to the author.

> **Bad.** The paint takes 31.33 minutes to dry on average.
>
> **Good.** The paint takes 31 minutes to dry on average.

**Example 2.** The draft's source data give lengths of 2.3 and 4.1 cm, both to the nearest tenth of a centimeter. The Good version is a numerical correction proposed to the author.

> **Bad.** The lengths are approximately 2.300 cm and 4.1 cm.
>
> **Good.** The lengths are approximately 2.3 cm and 4.1 cm.

**Example 3.** The draft reports mass measured to 0.01 g and length to 1 cm. Its source values are 2.34 g and 8 cm. The Good version is a correction proposed to the author.

> **Bad.** The sample weighs 2.34 g and is 8.00 cm long.
>
> **Good.** The sample weighs 2.34 g and is 8 cm long.
