# Match precision to the claim

Flag precision beyond what the measurement and comparison support, and unjustified differences in precision between comparable quantities; never change a number's value or precision without the author's approval and the source data, except to replace a threshold such as p < 0.05 with the draft's reported [p-value](../claims/statistical-significance.md). Keep words that mark estimates or approximations.

## Details

Do not keep extra digits merely because software prints them. Do not require the same number of decimal places for unlike quantities, exact counts, or measurements with different uncertainty. Never append zeros to a rounded value, because a rounded value does not record the digits that rounding discarded.

## Examples

**Edit 1.** Suppose the source data report both lengths to the nearest 0.1 cm. After is a proposal to the author.

> **Before.** The lengths are approximately 2.300 cm and 4.1 cm.

> **After.** The lengths are approximately 2.3 cm and 4.1 cm.

The stated precision does not make either length exact.

**Edit 2.** Imagine the source measurements have a precision of 0.01 g for mass and 1 cm for length. After is a proposal to the author.

> **Before.** The sample weighs 2.34 g and is 8.00 cm long.

> **After.** The sample weighs 2.34 g and is 8 cm long.

The mass keeps two decimal places because its source measurement supports them.
