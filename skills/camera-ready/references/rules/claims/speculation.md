# Never state speculation as fact

Mark an explanation or mechanism that no test or argument supports as a conjecture or put it in a passage explicitly labeled speculative. If it relies on an undefined term or an unstated measurement, flag it instead of calling it a conjecture. A claim that one property does not imply another needs a counterexample, a supporting result, a reference, or a statement that the question is open. Failure under the tested conditions does not show that success is impossible, so flag any unsupported negative conclusion.

## Details

- Untested explanations: Treat an explanation as untested if the draft gives no evidence that distinguishes it from alternatives, even if the draft never calls it untested. Unclear writing alone does not make it speculative.
- Marking speculation: A conjecture is a proposed explanation that has not been established. Sentences in a passage explicitly labeled speculative do not each need a hedge.
- Formal support: A proof or an established result cited in the draft can support an explanation without a new experiment. Apply [formal-support checks](evidence-kind.md) to it.
- Counterexamples: To disprove a claim that one property implies another, a counterexample must have the first property and lack the second.
- Open questions: When the draft states that a question is open, do not rewrite it as answered. Do not infer that a question is open merely because the draft gives no support for a claim.

## Examples

**Edit 1.** Suppose the draft supports reduced cracking but provides no evidence about moisture absorption.

> **Before.** The treated boards cracked less often because they absorbed less moisture.

> **After.** The treated boards cracked less often. We conjecture that they cracked less often because they absorbed less moisture.

**Edit 2.** Say the draft supports reduced flow through the narrowed outlet as the cause of the delay but never defines "bottleneck effect".

> **Before.** The bottleneck effect explains the delay.

> **After.** Reduced flow through the narrowed outlet explains the delay.

The explanation has support; only its name is unclear. Calling it a conjecture would give readers the wrong impression about that support.

**Edit 3.** Imagine the draft gives a rectangle with sides 2 cm and 3 cm as a counterexample.

> **Before.** Four right angles do not imply four equal sides.

> **After.** Four right angles do not imply four equal sides. A rectangle with sides 2 cm and 3 cm has four right angles but unequal sides.

**Edit 4.** If the draft reports only three failed solvent tests, propose this correction to the author.

> **Before.** The coating cannot be dissolved.

> **After.** The coating did not dissolve in the three tested solvents.
