# Never state speculation as fact

Do not state a proposed explanation or mechanism as fact without a supporting test or argument. Mark it as a conjecture ("We conjecture that") or put it in a passage explicitly labeled speculative. If an explanation relies on an undefined term or an unstated measurement, flag it instead of marking it as a conjecture. A claim that one property does not imply another requires a counterexample, a supporting result, or a reference. When the draft says a question is open, keep it open. Failure under the tested conditions does not show that success is impossible, so flag any unsupported negative conclusion.

## Details

A conjecture is a proposed explanation that has not been established. Treat an explanation as untested if the draft gives no evidence that distinguishes it from alternatives, even if the draft never calls it untested. Unclear writing alone does not make an explanation speculative. When a passage is explicitly labeled speculative, its sentences do not each need their own hedge. A proof or an established result cited in the draft can support an explanation without a new experiment. Apply [formal-support checks](evidence-kind.md) to that proof or cited result.

To disprove a claim that one property implies another, a counterexample must have the first property and lack the second. When the draft states that a question is open, do not rewrite it as answered, and do not infer that a question is open just because the draft gives no support for a claim.

## Examples

**Example 1.** The draft reports that the treated boards cracked less often but supplies no evidence about moisture absorption.

> **Bad.** The treated boards cracked less often because they absorbed less moisture.
>
> **Good.** The treated boards cracked less often. We conjecture that they cracked less often because they absorbed less moisture.

**Example 2.** The draft never defines "bottleneck effect", but elsewhere it gives evidence that reduced flow through the narrowed outlet explains the delay. The Good line is a proposed revision for the author.

> **Bad.** The bottleneck effect explains the delay.
>
> **Good.** Reduced flow through the narrowed outlet explains the delay.

**Example 3.** The draft gives a rectangle with sides 2 cm and 3 cm as a counterexample. The Good line is a proposed revision for the author.

> **Bad.** Four right angles do not imply four equal sides.
>
> **Good.** Four right angles do not imply four equal sides. A rectangle with sides 2 cm and 3 cm has four right angles but unequal sides.

**Example 4.** The draft reports only that three tested solvents failed to dissolve the coating. The Good line is a proposed revision for the author.

> **Bad.** The coating cannot be dissolved.
>
> **Good.** The coating did not dissolve in the three tested solvents.
