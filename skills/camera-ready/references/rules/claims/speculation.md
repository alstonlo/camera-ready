# Never state speculation as fact

Do not present a proposed explanation as established when the draft supplies no supporting test or argument. Mark an untested mechanism without other support as conjecture ("We conjecture that"), or place it in a passage explicitly labeled speculative. Flag an explanation that relies on an undefined term or an unstated measurement; unclear writing alone does not make an explanation a conjecture. A claim that one property does not imply another needs a counterexample, supporting result, or reference; retain an explicitly open question as open. Failure under tested conditions does not establish impossibility. Flag unsupported negative conclusions.

## Details

Separate the observed result from an explanation proposed for it. If the draft reports a gain but supplies no evidence that distinguishes the proposed mechanism from alternatives, mark the explanation as conjecture. The draft need not explicitly call its explanation untested. A passage clearly labeled speculative can cover its constituent explanations without repeating a hedge in every sentence.

A proof or an established cited result can support an explanation without a new experiment. Do not weaken such an explanation merely because this paper does not test it empirically.

When an explanation depends on an undefined term or an unstated measurement, flag that gap. An unclear explanation alone does not show whether evidence supports it. If the draft's support remains unclear, flag it rather than choosing a confidence level.

To disprove an implication, a counterexample must satisfy the premise and violate the conclusion. A stated result or reference may supply the argument. If the draft explicitly identifies the implication as an open question, preserve that status rather than asserting its failure. Missing support does not itself establish that a question is open.

An unsuccessful search, implementation, or parameter sweep establishes the reported failure under its conditions. It does not settle whether the broader task is possible.

## Examples

The draft reports that the treated boards cracked less often but supplies no evidence about moisture absorption.

- Bad: The treated boards cracked less often because they absorbed less moisture.
- Good: The treated boards cracked less often; we conjecture that they did so because they absorbed less moisture.
