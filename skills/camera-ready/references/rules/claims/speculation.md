# Never state speculation as fact

Do not present a proposed explanation as established when the draft supplies no supporting test or argument. Mark an untested mechanism without other support as conjecture ("We conjecture that"), or place it in a passage explicitly labeled speculative. Flag an explanation that relies on an undefined term or an unstated measurement rather than marking it as a conjecture; unclear writing alone does not make an explanation speculative. A claim that one property does not imply another requires a counterexample, supporting result, or reference, and a question that the draft states is open must remain open. Similarly, failure under the tested conditions does not establish impossibility; flag any unsupported negative conclusion.

## Details

Treat an explanation as untested whenever the draft supplies no evidence that distinguishes it from alternatives, even if the draft does not call it untested. When a passage is explicitly labeled speculative, its individual sentences need not repeat the hedge. A proof or an established cited result can support an explanation without a new experiment; apply [formal-support checks](evidence-kind.md) to such support.

To disprove an implication, a counterexample must satisfy the premise and violate the conclusion. Missing support for a claim does not by itself establish that a question is open.

## Examples

> **Context.** The draft reports that the treated boards cracked less often but supplies no evidence about moisture absorption.
>
> **Bad.** The treated boards cracked less often because they absorbed less moisture.
>
> **Good.** The treated boards cracked less often; we conjecture that they did so because they absorbed less moisture.

> **Context.** The draft reports neither a test nor another argument for these two proposed mechanisms.
>
> **Bad.** Heat loosens the seal. Moisture weakens the glue.
>
> **Good.** Speculative explanations: Heat loosens the seal. Moisture weakens the glue.

> **Context.** The draft cites a proved result establishing the expansion mechanism but leaves the color-change mechanism untested.
>
> **Bad.** The cited result establishes the expansion mechanism and the color-change mechanism.
>
> **Good.** The cited result establishes the expansion mechanism; the color-change mechanism remains conjectural.

> **Context.** The draft never defines the term "bottleneck effect", but elsewhere it gives evidence that reduced flow through the narrowed outlet explains the delay; the revision is proposed to the author.
>
> **Bad.** The bottleneck effect explains the delay.
>
> **Good.** Reduced flow through the narrowed outlet explains the delay.

> **Context.** The draft reports measured water absorption as the established explanation for swelling; the revision is proposed to the author.
>
> **Bad.** The swelling follows from the measured change.
>
> **Good.** The swelling follows from the measured water absorption.

> **Context.** The draft gives a rectangle with sides 2 cm and 3 cm as its counterexample; the revision is proposed to the author.
>
> **Bad.** Four right angles do not imply four equal sides.
>
> **Good.** Four right angles do not imply four equal sides: a rectangle with sides 2 cm and 3 cm has four right angles but unequal sides.

> **Context.** The draft explicitly leaves open whether the surface pattern implies a crack beneath it; the revision is proposed to the author.
>
> **Bad.** The surface pattern does not imply a crack beneath it.
>
> **Good.** Whether the surface pattern implies a crack beneath it remains open.

> **Context.** The draft reports only that three tested solvents failed to dissolve the coating; the revision is proposed to the author.
>
> **Bad.** The coating cannot be dissolved.
>
> **Good.** The coating did not dissolve in the three tested solvents.
