# Reconcile conflicting claims and numerical constraints

Compare claims about the same object across sections and lists, including one passage saying that the object supports an operation and another saying that it cannot. Check totals, bounds, and trends against constraints the draft establishes. If a difference in setting or definition stated in the draft reconciles the claims, flag both passages with a revision that makes the difference explicit; otherwise flag both passages and the missing explanation. Flag numerical conflicts rather than choosing a value. Also flag an exhaustive either/or claim when the draft names another in-scope option; identify that option.

## Details

Compare the abstract, contribution list, results, captions, and conclusion. A passage can be plausible on its own yet contradict another passage about the same method or result.

Use only numerical constraints established by the draft, such as an explicit partition, admissible range, or monotonicity condition. Do not assume that categories are disjoint or that a list is exhaustive. Check reported values against the stated relationship, and flag a conflict without selecting a replacement.

First check for different settings or definitions. If the draft supplies a distinction that resolves the conflict, flag both passages with a revision that makes it explicit. Otherwise identify both locations and the missing explanation so the author can reconcile them.

An either/or statement claims a complete division only when its wording excludes other options. A comparison of two approaches need not make that claim. Use a further option already identified in the draft; do not invent one or treat overlapping category names as proof of a contradiction. A proposed revision may include the stated option, but changing the scientific alternatives is the author's decision.

## Examples

The draft states that the lamp works in dry conditions but not in rain; the revisions to the abstract and results are proposed to the author.

- Bad: Abstract: The lamp works. Results: The lamp does not work.
- Good: Abstract: In dry conditions, the lamp works. Results: In rain, the lamp does not work.

The draft lists an online form as a third way for participants to respond; the revision is proposed to the author.

- Bad: Participants must respond by phone or by mail.
- Good: Participants must respond by phone, by mail, or through an online form.
