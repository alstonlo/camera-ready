# Choose the reporting verb from the verb ladder

Choose each reporting verb from the verb ladder according to the evidence for the claim, regardless of enthusiasm for the result.

| Verb | Required support |
|---|---|
| **prove** | a theorem with stated assumptions, and nothing else |
| **show / demonstrate / establish** | direct evidence that rules out plausible alternatives |
| **find / observe / report** | a directly measured empirical result |
| **suggest / indicate**; **hypothesize / conjecture** | an inference or interpretation that could reasonably be wrong |

An empirical test, such as a benchmark or an assay, does not prove a mechanism. A figure or table shows only what it directly displays; for an interpretation drawn from it, choose the verb that matches the evidence. Replace *reveal* or *confirm* with a lower-rung verb if the evidence does not establish the stated conclusion. Flag a claimed disproof supported only by an objection or an inconclusive test. When reporting cited work, preserve whether the source presents a result as central or incidental, and whether it accepts or doubts the result. See the page for verb choices and [prior work](../story/prior-work-comparisons.md) for attribution.

A claim hedged in Results must keep its hedge in the abstract, introduction, conclusion, and every other summary. Trace each summary claim to its supporting result, and flag any summary claim that has none. When body support exists, add a missing section, table, or figure pointer, except in the abstract, which must state the result itself.

## Details

When checking a summary claim, trace it through any intermediate assertions back to a direct observation, a demonstrated result, or an established result in a cited source. Another writer's agreement does not establish a contested proposition, whereas an accepted result from prior work needs no fresh derivation.

When describing cited work, do not use "notes in passing" for a central result or "assumes" for a premise that the source establishes. Match the reporting verb to the source's treatment under [prior-work comparisons](../story/prior-work-comparisons.md).

## Examples

> **Context.** The draft reports water temperatures measured in a test of an insulated bottle and a plain bottle; it contains no mathematical proof.
>
> **Bad.** We prove that the insulated bottle kept water warmer than the plain bottle in the test.
>
> **Good.** We find that the insulated bottle kept water warmer than the plain bottle in the test.

> **Context.** The draft gives a complete proof under the stated convexity assumption.
>
> **Bad.** We conjecture that the minimum is unique when the function is strictly convex.
>
> **Good.** We prove that the minimum is unique when the function is strictly convex.

> **Context.** The draft's controlled tests isolate the coating's effect on water loss and rule out the stated alternatives.
>
> **Bad.** The controlled tests merely suggest that the coating reduces water loss.
>
> **Good.** The controlled tests establish that the coating reduces water loss.

> **Context.** The draft's figure displays slower drying, and the draft proposes trapped moisture as one possible explanation without ruling out alternatives.
>
> **Bad.** Figure 2 reveals that trapped moisture causes slower drying.
>
> **Good.** Figure 2 suggests that trapped moisture causes slower drying.

> **Context.** The draft reports that the test neither detects nor rules out an effect; the revision is proposed to the author.
>
> **Bad.** The test disproves the proposed effect.
>
> **Good.** The test does not detect the proposed effect.

> **Context.** The draft's only support for the contested claim that the foam resists fire is Lee's agreement; the revision is proposed to the author.
>
> **Bad.** Lee's agreement establishes that the foam resists fire.
>
> **Good.** Lee agrees that the foam resists fire.

> **Context.** The draft identifies the density result as Lee's central finding.
>
> **Bad.** Lee notes in passing that the foam's density falls with temperature.
>
> **Good.** Lee finds that the foam's density falls with temperature.

> **Context.** The draft states that Lee establishes the independence of the errors.
>
> **Bad.** Lee assumes that the errors are independent.
>
> **Good.** Lee establishes that the errors are independent.

> **Context.** The draft's Results section says that the measurements suggest slower diffusion.
>
> **Bad.** Conclusion: The measurements establish slower diffusion.
>
> **Good.** Conclusion: The measurements suggest slower diffusion.

> **Context.** The draft's Section 4 reports the measured drop in temperature.
>
> **Bad.** Conclusion: We find that the coating lowers temperature in the tests.
>
> **Good.** Conclusion: We find that the coating lowers temperature in the tests (Section 4).

> **Context.** The draft's Section 4 reports that the coating lowers temperature in the tests.
>
> **Bad.** Abstract: We find the result reported in Section 4.
>
> **Good.** Abstract: We find that the coating lowers temperature in the tests.
