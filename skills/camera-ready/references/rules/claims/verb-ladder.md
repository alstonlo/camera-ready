# Choose a reporting verb from the verb ladder

Match the verb to the evidence, regardless of enthusiasm for the result.

| Verb | Required support |
|---|---|
| **prove** | only a theorem with stated assumptions |
| **show / demonstrate / establish** | direct evidence that rules out plausible alternatives |
| **find / observe / report** | a directly measured empirical result |
| **suggest / indicate**; **hypothesize / conjecture** | an inference or interpretation that could reasonably be wrong |

An empirical test does not prove a mechanism. A figure or table shows only what it directly displays. When the text interprets a figure or table, match the verb to the evidence. Replace *reveal* or *confirm* with a weaker verb unless the evidence establishes the conclusion. Flag a claimed disproof supported only by an objection or an inconclusive test. When reporting cited work, preserve whether the source treats a result as central or incidental and whether it accepts or doubts the result (see Details and [prior work](../story/prior-work-comparisons.md)).

Keep any Results hedge, such as "suggests", in the abstract, introduction, conclusion, and other summaries. Trace each summary claim to its supporting result, and flag any unsupported claim. When the body supports a summary claim, add any missing section, table, or figure reference. In the abstract, state the result itself instead of citing the body.

## Details

An empirical test, such as a benchmark or a laboratory measurement, does not prove a mechanism. To check a claim in a summary, follow its chain of support, through any intermediate claims, until you reach a direct observation, a demonstrated result, or an established result in a cited source. Another writer's agreement does not establish a disputed claim. An accepted result from prior work needs no new derivation.

When describing cited work, do not write "notes in passing" if the source presents the result as central. Do not write "assumes" if the source establishes the premise. Choose a verb that preserves how the source presents the claim, and follow [prior-work comparisons](../story/prior-work-comparisons.md) when attributing a claim to a source.

## Examples

**Edit 1.** Suppose the temperatures reported in the draft were measured.

> **Before.** We prove that the insulated bottle kept water warmer than the plain bottle in the test.

> **After.** We find that the insulated bottle kept water warmer than the plain bottle in the test.

**Edit 2.** Imagine the draft contains a complete proof under the stated assumption.

> **Before.** We conjecture that the minimum is unique when the function is strictly convex.

> **After.** We prove that the minimum is unique when the function is strictly convex.

**Edit 3.** Say the test neither detects nor rules out the effect; the editor flags the claimed disproof and proposes After to the author.

> **Before.** The test disproves the proposed effect.

> **After.** The test does not detect the proposed effect.

**Edit 4.** Suppose Lee's cited work establishes this premise.

> **Before.** Lee assumes that the errors are independent.

> **After.** Lee establishes that the errors are independent.

**Edit 5.** Say Results treats slower diffusion as a tentative interpretation.

> **Before.** Conclusion: The measurements establish slower diffusion.

> **After.** Conclusion: The measurements suggest slower diffusion.

**Edit 6.** Imagine Section 4 reports that the coating lowers temperature in the tests.

> **Before.** Abstract: We find the result reported in Section 4.

> **After.** Abstract: We find that the coating lowers temperature in the tests.
