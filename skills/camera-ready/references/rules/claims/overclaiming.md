# Remove or flag unsupported empirical labels

Remove or flag unsupported empirical labels such as *solves*, *optimal*, *guarantees*, and *first to*; keep them only when the draft's evidence supports the full claim. Reserve *proves* for theorems, as the [verb ladder](verb-ladder.md) requires. Flag unsupported superlatives and *human-level* or *state-of-the-art* claims that do not name a task, benchmark, and metric. Remove *novel* when it adds only praise. Beyond removing a label, change what the claim asserts only when the draft's evidence settles its [scope](scope.md); otherwise flag it. For claims that a method is empirically unique, see the page.

## Details

A prestige label must identify a checkable distinction. For an empirical uniqueness claim, identify the alternatives, the property being compared, and the level of detail used to distinguish alternatives. Replace vague praise with an already supported distinction, or flag the missing comparison.

Keep a claim of exact mathematical uniqueness when a stated result proves it. An empirical comparison does not license a claim about all possible methods, and this rule does not forbid valid uniqueness theorems.

Keep a verified priority claim (a claim to be first). The editor's failure to recall earlier work does not verify that claim. A promotional adjective does not supply missing evidence.

## Examples

The draft compares fabrics only by the amount of water they absorb and reports that fabric A absorbs the most; the revision is proposed to the author.

- Bad: Among the tested fabrics, fabric A is the best.
- Good: Among the tested fabrics, fabric A absorbs the most water.
