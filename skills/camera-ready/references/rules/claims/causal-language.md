# Reserve causal language for causal evidence

Use causal wording such as "causes", "leads to", or "improves because" only when the evidence isolates a factor's effect. Describe a correlation with "is associated with" or "correlates with". Keep cause and effect in the order the evidence supports, and do not call one of several causes the only cause. When the draft tests several changes only together, attribute the effect to their combination, not to one component, and do not call each component necessary. Flag unsupported causal claims, and mark an untested mechanism as [speculation](speculation.md).

## Details

Causal wording also includes "produces", "results in", "results from", and "contributes to". Check whether the draft distinguishes a contributing factor from a sufficient or necessary condition, and preserve that distinction when shortening. A sufficient condition guarantees the outcome. A necessary condition must hold for the outcome to occur. A contributing factor need not do either.

A suitable controlled comparison can isolate the effect of a factor such as a design choice, a treatment, or an exposure:

- An ablation removes a component.
- A gene knockout disables a gene.
- A randomized controlled trial assigns treatments by chance.

## Examples

**Edit 1.** Suppose the draft's household survey reports only a correlation. After is a proposal to the author. Before:

> In the surveyed households, owning more houseplants caused fewer colds.

After:

> In the surveyed households, owning more houseplants was associated with fewer colds.

**Edit 2.** Say the controlled comparison in the draft adds mulch and shade together, with no separate test of either. After is a proposal to the author. Before:

> Mulch reduces water loss.

After:

> Mulch and shade together reduce water loss.

**Edit 3.** If the draft calls lubrication a contributing factor and reports wear despite lubrication, propose the revision below to the author. Before:

> Lubrication is sufficient to prevent wear.

After:

> Lubrication contributes to preventing wear.

**Edit 4.** Imagine the draft establishes that the coating reduces corrosion but does not test whether blocking oxygen explains the reduction. Before:

> The coating reduces corrosion because it blocks oxygen.

After:

> The coating reduces corrosion. We conjecture that it does so by blocking oxygen.
