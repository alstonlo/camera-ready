# Reserve causal language for causal evidence

Use "causes", "leads to", "improves because", or other wording that credits a design choice with an effect only when the evidence isolates that effect, as a suitable ablation can. Describe correlations with "is associated with" or "correlates with". Preserve the direction of cause and effect, and do not present a partial contribution as the sole cause. When several changes are tested only together, credit their combination, not a single component, and do not claim that each component is necessary. Flag unsupported attribution; mark an untested mechanism as [speculation](speculation.md).

## Details

Causal claims also occur in wording such as "produces", "results in", "results from", and "contributes to". Reversing the positions of cause and effect changes the claim. Preserve which factor acts and which outcome follows.

A contributing factor need not be sufficient, necessary, or the only cause. Shortening must preserve those distinctions.

An ablation supports the isolated effect under the reported conditions. A comparison that changes several components together supports an effect of the combination. It does not by itself show that every component is necessary or that one proposed internal mechanism explains the result.

## Examples

The draft reports an association between the number of houseplants and the number of colds in surveyed households, with no evidence isolating a causal effect; the revision is proposed to the author.

- Bad: In the surveyed households, owning more houseplants caused fewer colds.
- Good: In the surveyed households, owning more houseplants was associated with fewer colds.
