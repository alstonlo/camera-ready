# State supported contributions without underclaiming

State supported contributions directly, without hedges that the evidence does not call for. Each item in a Contributions list should state a checkable claim and refer to its support where the draft provides it. An item that merely says *study*, *explore*, or *investigate* names an activity. Replace it with the finding in the corresponding section, or flag the missing finding.

## Details

Inspect contribution items built around activities such as studying, exploring, investigating, describing, or discussing. Locate the corresponding section and identify the finding it actually supports. An activity alone does not tell readers what the paper contributes.

An artifact can itself be a checkable contribution. State its documented properties without inventing a performance gain to make it resemble an experimental result.

Removing redundant caution is different from strengthening an unresolved claim. State an established result directly; flag uncertain support rather than supplying a stronger conclusion.

## Examples

The draft's contributions list includes the item below, and its Section 4 reports that thicker wool samples retained more heat in the tests.

- Bad: We investigate whether thicker wool samples retain more heat in the tests (Section 4).
- Good: We find that thicker wool samples retain more heat in the tests (Section 4).
