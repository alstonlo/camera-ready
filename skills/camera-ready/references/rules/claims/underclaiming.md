# State supported contributions without underclaiming

State supported contributions directly, without unneeded hedges (see Details). Each item in a list of contributions should state a claim that readers can check and cite its supporting section or result when the draft provides one. If an item says only what the authors *study*, *explore*, or *investigate*, replace it with the finding from the corresponding section, or flag the missing finding.

## Details

A contribution is underclaimed when a hedge such as "may" makes it sound less certain than the evidence supports, or when an activity verb such as *study*, *explore*, or *investigate* states what the authors did without stating what they found. Remove a hedge only when the evidence establishes the claim without it. If you are unsure whether the evidence supports the stronger claim, flag the claim instead of strengthening it.

A dataset, instrument, protocol, software tool, or other resource or artifact can itself be a contribution that readers can check. State the properties that the draft documents. Do not invent a gain in performance.

## Examples

**Edit 1.** The contribution list contains this sentence. Section 4 reports greater heat retention for thicker wool samples in the tests.

> **Before.** We investigate whether thicker wool samples retain more heat in the tests (Section 4).
>
> **After.** We find that thicker wool samples retain more heat in the tests (Section 4).

**Edit 2.** For every tested panel, the draft reports direct measurements of the gap.

> **Before.** We may have found a 2 cm gap in every tested panel.
>
> **After.** We found a 2 cm gap in every tested panel.

**Edit 3.** A catalog of 200 labeled leaf images is documented as a contribution in Section 3.

> **Before.** We explore leaf images (Section 3).
>
> **After.** We present a catalog of 200 labeled leaf images (Section 3).
