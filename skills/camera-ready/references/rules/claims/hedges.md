# Hedge interpretations, not measurements or procedures

Hedge tentative interpretations, including proposed mechanisms. State measurements directly and keep their reported uncertainty. State performed procedures directly, and hedge only the reason for performing them. Make a claim more or less confident only when the evidence supports the change. In every summary of a claim, keep the claim's hedge and its [verb-ladder strength](verb-ladder.md). Use at most one hedge per uncertain claim, in a standard form such as "may", "likely", or "suggests". When you remove a redundant hedge, keep the hedges on other uncertain claims and keep every [scope condition](../cutting/protected-sentences.md). Replace vague softeners such as *somewhat* or *relatively* with the conditions or magnitudes that the draft supplies. If the draft supplies neither, keep the softener and flag the missing information.

## Details

A hedge marks a claim as uncertain, so deleting the only hedge on a claim makes the claim stronger. Reported measurement uncertainty includes values such as a standard error, and summaries of a claim include the abstract. A scope condition is a qualifier such as *usually*, *on average*, or *in most runs* that limits the conditions or cases a claim covers, so deleting it changes the claim. A vague softener is a qualifier that weakens a claim without saying by how much.

## Examples

**Example 1.** The draft reports the measured mean mass and its standard error.

> **Bad.** The mean mass may be 5.0 ± 0.2 g (standard error).
>
> **Good.** The mean mass is 5.0 ± 0.2 g (standard error).

**Example 2.** The draft states that the researchers cooled the samples but did not test whether cooling prevented decay.

> **Bad.** We may have cooled the samples because cooling may prevent decay.
>
> **Good.** We cooled the samples because cooling may prevent decay.

**Example 3.** The draft treats both steps of this proposed explanation as uncertain.

> **Bad.** Heat may possibly loosen the seal, which may allow water to enter.
>
> **Good.** Heat may loosen the seal, which may allow water to enter.

**Example 4.** The draft reports that the treated cloth absorbs 8% less water than untreated cloth.

> **Bad.** The treated cloth absorbs somewhat less water than untreated cloth.
>
> **Good.** The treated cloth absorbs 8% less water than untreated cloth.

**Example 5.** The Results section says that frost may explain the cracks, and the abstract summarizes this explanation.

> **Bad.** Abstract: Frost explains the cracks.
>
> **Good.** Abstract: Frost may explain the cracks.
