# Write captions that stand alone and state the finding

A caption that only names its figure or table is incomplete. Complete it using facts from the citing paragraph, and flag needed details absent from the draft. Identify quantities, units, axes, conditions, visual symbols like colors and line styles, and [error bars](../claims/variability.md). State whether higher or lower values are better when that is not obvious. State the supported finding, verify that it matches the body text, and name compared methods or conditions directly instead of by color alone. In tables, flag unexplained blanks, dashes, and columns with mixed or missing units. When a plot specifies axis ranges, dual vertical axes, or a fitted curve's range, flag claims drawn from visual slopes or distances that the scales do not support, and flag any extrapolated value described as measured. An axis displaying only part of its range is not by itself an error.

## Details

In a table, a blank cell or dash can mean that a value is unavailable, unsupported, or inapplicable. Never assume that it means zero. Propose a table note or header repair that explains the blanks, dashes, or units, without changing data or computing new columns. For formatting changes intended to meet a page limit, follow the [stop rules](../cutting/stop-rules.md).

For claims about a relationship estimated by fitting a model to data, follow [statistical significance](../claims/statistical-significance.md).

In the examples for Tables 3 through 5 and Figures 8 through 12, each Good line proposes a revision to the author rather than making the edit directly.

## Examples

**Example 1.** The paragraph citing Figure 2 supplies the measurements, units, and finding added here.

> **Bad.** Figure 2: Plant growth.
>
> **Good.** Figure 2: Mean plant height after four weeks, in centimeters. Plants given fertilizer were taller than untreated plants.

**Example 2.** The draft defines the dashes in Table 3 as missing measurements.

> **Bad.** Table 3: Daily air temperature at each site, in degrees Celsius.
>
> **Good.** Table 3: Daily air temperature at each site, in degrees Celsius. A dash marks a missing measurement.

**Example 3.** The vertical axis starts at 9 cm. Good uses the reported heights because the displayed bar lengths do not show the ratio of plant heights.

> **Bad.** Figure 8: The treated plants are twice as tall as the controls.
>
> **Good.** Figure 8: The treated plants are 11 cm tall and the controls are 10 cm tall.

**Example 4.** Measurements end at day 20; a fitted curve supplies the value at day 30.

> **Bad.** Figure 10: The measured height at day 30 is 18 cm.
>
> **Good.** Figure 10: The predicted height at day 30 is 18 cm.
