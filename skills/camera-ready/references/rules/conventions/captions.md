# Write captions that stand alone and state the finding

A caption that only names its figure or table ("Results on ImageNet.") is incomplete: complete it with facts from the citing paragraph, and flag those absent from the draft. Identify quantities, units, axes, and conditions (dataset, model, setting); explain each visual encoding (line styles, colors, shaded regions, [error bars](../claims/variability.md)); and state whether higher or lower is better when that is not obvious. State the supported finding, check that it matches the body text, and name compared methods or conditions directly, not by color alone. In tables, flag unexplained blanks, dashes, and columns with mixed or missing units. Flag claims drawn from visual slopes or distances that a plot's stated axis ranges, dual vertical axes, or fitted-curve range do not support, and any extrapolated value described as measured; a truncated axis is not by itself an error.

Flag table layouts that separate comparison partners or obscure groups; suggest adjacent entries and ordering that exposes the intended comparison, preserving meaningful chronological or semantic order.

## Details

In a table, a blank cell or dash can mean that a value is unavailable, unsupported, or inapplicable. Never assume that it means zero. Propose a table note or header repair that explains the blanks, dashes, or units, without changing data or computing new columns. For formatting changes intended to meet a page limit, follow the [stop rules](../cutting/stop-rules.md).

For claims about a relationship estimated by fitting a model to data, follow [statistical significance](../claims/statistical-significance.md).

In the edits for Table 3 and Figures 8 and 10, each After line proposes a revision to the author rather than making the edit directly.

## Examples

**Edit 1.** Suppose the paragraph citing Figure 2 gives the measurement, time, units, and finding shown in After.

> **Before.** Figure 2: Plant growth.

> **After.** Figure 2: Mean plant height after four weeks, in centimeters. Plants given fertilizer were taller than untreated plants.

**Edit 2.** Say the draft identifies the dashes in Table 3 as missing measurements.

> **Before.** Table 3: Daily air temperature at each site, in degrees Celsius.

> **After.** Table 3: Daily air temperature at each site, in degrees Celsius. A dash marks a missing measurement.

**Edit 3.** Imagine Figure 8's vertical axis starts at 9 cm and the draft reports heights of 11 cm for treated plants and 10 cm for controls.

> **Before.** Figure 8: The treated plants are twice as tall as the controls.

> **After.** Figure 8: The treated plants are 11 cm tall and the controls are 10 cm tall.

**Edit 4.** Suppose the draft's measurements end at day 20 and the day-30 value comes from a fitted curve.

> **Before.** Figure 10: The measured height at day 30 is 18 cm.

> **After.** Figure 10: The predicted height at day 30 is 18 cm.
