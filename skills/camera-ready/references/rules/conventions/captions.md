# Write captions that stand alone and state the finding

Complete a caption that only names its figure or table with facts from the citing paragraph; flag any needed facts absent from the draft. In each caption:

- Identify quantities, units, axes, and conditions.
- Explain each visual encoding (line styles, colors, shaded regions, [error bars](../claims/variability.md)), and state whether higher or lower is better when that is not obvious.
- State the supported finding, check that it matches the body text, and name compared methods or conditions directly, not by color alone.

In tables, flag unexplained blanks, dashes, and columns with mixed or missing units. Flag claims drawn from visual slopes or distances that the plot's stated axis ranges, dual vertical axes, or fitted-curve range do not support, and any extrapolated value described as measured; a truncated axis is not by itself an error.

Flag table layouts that separate comparison partners or obscure groups; suggest placing entries together and ordering them to show the intended comparison, preserving meaningful chronological or semantic order.

## Details

- Table cells and units: A blank cell or dash can mean that a value is unavailable, unsupported, or inapplicable. Never assume it means zero. Propose a table note or header repair that explains the blanks, dashes, or units, without changing data or computing new columns.
- Page limits: For formatting changes intended to meet a page limit, follow the [stop rules](../cuts/stop-rules.md).
- Fitted relationships: For claims about a relationship estimated by fitting a model to data, follow [statistical significance](../claims/statistical-significance.md).

## Examples

**Edit 1.** Suppose the paragraph citing Figure 2 gives the measurement, time, units, and finding shown in the edited text.

> **Before.** Figure 2: Plant growth.

> **After.** Figure 2: Mean plant height after four weeks, in centimeters. Plants given fertilizer were taller than untreated plants.

**Edit 2.** Suppose the draft identifies the dashes in Table 3 as missing measurements. The edited text proposes a revision to the author.

> **Before.** Table 3: Daily air temperature at each site, in degrees Celsius.

> **After.** Table 3: Daily air temperature at each site, in degrees Celsius. A dash marks a missing measurement.

**Edit 3.** Suppose Figure 8's vertical axis starts at 9 cm and the draft reports heights of 11 cm for treated plants and 10 cm for controls. The edited text proposes a revision to the author.

> **Before.** Figure 8: The treated plants are twice as tall as the controls.

> **After.** Figure 8: The treated plants are taller than the controls (11 cm versus 10 cm).

**Edit 4.** Suppose measurements end at day 20 and the day-30 value comes from a fitted curve. The edited text proposes a revision to the author.

> **Before.** Figure 10: The measured height at day 30 is 18 cm.

> **After.** Figure 10: The predicted height at day 30 is 18 cm.
