# Define each symbol by its first use and give it one meaning

An informal definition suffices at first use; [refresh it after a gap](shorthand-reminders.md). Give each object one symbol, and introduce no symbol the text does not use again. Flag conflicting meanings, symbols used contrary to their standard meaning in the field, unused definitions, easily confused symbols, dense notation, and functions equated with their values ("$V = 0.31$" meaning "$V(x_0) = 0.31$"). Keep local definitions near the passages that use them. Move scattered definitions of symbols used throughout the paper into an existing Preliminaries or Notation section only if the request [allows relocation](../cuts/idea-cuts.md); otherwise, flag them. Do not create a Preliminaries or Notation section. Suggest, rather than make, any renaming or other notation improvement, such as replacing an index that distinguishes nothing with words, giving a recurring complex expression a short name, or grouping related quantities in a vector, set, or other collection.

Flag unspecified factor order in noncommuting products and inconsistent use of definition signs. For ranges, flag unclear finite bounds, endpoint inclusion, index origin, element types, or empty-range conventions. Supply conventions only from the draft; never assume commutativity or merge boundary cases to shorten notation.

## Details

Check later equations, captions, figures, and appendices before flagging a symbol definition as unused.

## Examples

**Edit 1.**

> **Before.** We compare $h$ across plants. The symbol $h$ denotes plant height.

> **After.** The symbol $h$ denotes plant height. We compare $h$ across plants.

**Edit 2.** Suppose the tables use $w$ to denote moisture content.

> **Before.** Let $m$ denote mass and $m$ denote moisture content.

> **After.** Let $m$ denote mass and $w$ denote moisture content.

**Edit 4.** Say the draft defines all four symbols.

> **Before.** We compare $(a+b)/(c+d)$ across sites and plot $(a+b)/(c+d)$ over time.

> **After.** Let $R=(a+b)/(c+d)$. We compare $R$ across sites and plot $R$ over time.

The edited text proposes new notation for author review.
