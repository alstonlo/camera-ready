# Explain why formal material is needed

Flag a central equation or definition whose purpose the text never states; do not infer a purpose the draft does not give. If the same paragraph or section explains its purpose later, move that explanation before the formal material; if it appears only in another section, flag the move instead. Follow [formal support](../claims/formal-informal.md) when the claimed application depends on unstated conditions.

## Details

An expression can define every symbol yet leave the reader unsure why it matters. Place an existing explanation of its role before the reader has to work through the formal details. Focus this check on central formal material, not every intermediate step in a derivation.

A plausible interpretation of the mathematics does not establish the authors' intended purpose. If the draft supplies no explanation, flag its absence. Keep the description of purpose distinct from the conditions under which the mathematics applies; do not supply missing assumptions as an explanation of its role.

## Examples

The draft defines $v$ as speed, $d$ as distance, and $t$ as elapsed time.

- Bad: We use $v=d/t$. This equation converts each runner's distance and elapsed time into average speed.
- Good: To convert each runner's distance and elapsed time into average speed, we use $v=d/t$.
