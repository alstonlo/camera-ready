# Remind readers what shorthand means after a gap

When shorthand or a symbol returns after a long gap or section boundary, remind readers of its name and either its definition or where it was defined ("the contrastive loss L_c defined in Section 3"). Phrase this as a reminder rather than a new definition. Replace ambiguous labels such as "the rate" with the original full name, and name a distant display referred to by number alone ("the energy balance (9)").

## Details

A reference to a nearby equation needs no added description if readers can tell what the equation does. When an acronym appears again, follow [acronyms](acronyms.md).

## Examples

**Edit 1.** Suppose this passage appears in Section 5, and Section 2 defines $r$ as the rainfall rate.

> **Before.** Let $r$ denote the rainfall rate. We compare $r$ across sites.

> **After.** Recall that $r$ denotes the rainfall rate. We compare $r$ across sites.

**Edit 2.** Say the draft discusses both evaporation and infiltration rates and says that the evaporation rate increases in summer.

> **Before.** The rate increases in summer.

> **After.** The evaporation rate increases in summer.

**Edit 3.** Suppose the draft names equation (9) as the energy balance several pages before this passage.

> **Before.** Equation (3) gives the heat loss. We evaluate (3), then apply (9).

> **After.** Equation (3) gives the heat loss. We evaluate (3), then apply the energy balance (9).

Equation (3) needs no reminder because its purpose was just stated.
