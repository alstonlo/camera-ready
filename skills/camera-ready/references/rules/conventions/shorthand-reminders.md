# Remind readers what shorthand means after a gap

When shorthand or a symbol returns after a long gap or section boundary, remind readers of its name and either its definition or where it was defined. Phrase this as a reminder ("Recall that $r$ denotes...") rather than a new definition. Replace ambiguous labels such as "the rate" with the original full name. When referring to a distant display by number alone, add its name, as in "the energy balance (9)".

## Details

A reference to a nearby equation needs no added description if readers can tell what the equation does. When an acronym appears again, follow [acronyms](acronyms.md).

## Examples

**Example 1.** In this draft, equation (9) gives the energy balance several pages before this passage.

> **Bad.** We use (9) to calculate the heat loss.
>
> **Good.** We use the energy balance (9) to calculate the heat loss.

**Example 2.** The draft defines the rainfall rate $r$ in Section 2, and this passage appears several sections later.

> **Bad.** We compare $r$ across sites.
>
> **Good.** We compare the rainfall rate $r$, defined in Section 2, across sites.

**Example 3.** The draft defines the rainfall rate $r$ in Section 2, and this passage appears in Section 5.

> **Bad.** Let $r$ denote the rainfall rate. We compare $r$ across sites.
>
> **Good.** Recall that $r$ denotes the rainfall rate. We compare $r$ across sites.

**Example 4.** In this draft, the authors discuss both evaporation and infiltration rates, attributing the summer increase to evaporation.

> **Bad.** The rate increases in summer.
>
> **Good.** The evaporation rate increases in summer.

**Example 5.** In this draft, the energy balance appears several pages earlier as equation (9).

> **Bad.** Equation (3) gives the heat loss. We evaluate (3), then apply (9).
>
> **Good.** Equation (3) gives the heat loss. We evaluate (3), then apply the energy balance (9).
