# Use active voice with a consistent agent

Default to active "we" for the authors' actions and decisions. Do not use this "we" for the research community or people in general; in derivations and proofs, "we" may also mean the author and reader together. Use one agent throughout a method description, either the method or "we", and always use "we" for design choices. Preserve a single author's consistent "I" or "we". Keep passives when the actor is obvious, irrelevant, or unknown, when they keep [old information first](../prose/other-sentence-orders.md), when a long agent belongs last, or when a passive modifier ("the prompts selected by annotators") is easier to read. Do not assign an unidentified action to the authors.

## Details

Before changing a subject, distinguish the authors from participants, annotators, service providers, and researchers generally. A passive can leave an unknown actor unspecified without attributing that action to the authors. When the actor's identity matters and the draft supplies it, retain that identity.

If both voices begin with the same familiar topic, compare their endings and the next sentence's opening. Prefer the version that puts the intended emphasis at the end and supports the next sentence's topic. A passive modifier can also keep the noun close to the action describing it; do not expand it into a harder-to-follow active clause merely to remove a passive.

## Examples

- Bad: The interview prompts are selected by us.
- Good: We select the interview prompts.
