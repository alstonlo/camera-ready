# Make theorem statements self-contained

Introduce a theorem, lemma, or algorithm with a complete sentence, using a colon only after a complete clause. Make the statement self-contained: name its objects ("the transition relation R") and define symbols before use. Keep motivation and commentary outside the statement. When the hypotheses run for several lines, flag the statement and propose named conditions defined before it ("Assumption 1 (smoothness)").

## Details

A reader should be able to identify each object and the conditions on it from the statement and its explicit references. A meaningful name alongside a symbol can recall the object without repeating a full definition.

When several long hypotheses obscure the conclusion, suggest conditions with names that identify their content. Propose defining the conditions before the theorem, retaining every restriction. Move only explanatory commentary outside the statement; an assumption is part of the mathematical claim.

## Examples

The paragraph before the theorem restricts the discussion to rectangles.

- Bad: Theorem 1. The diagonals of a quadrilateral have equal lengths.
- Good: Theorem 1. The diagonals of a rectangle have equal lengths.
