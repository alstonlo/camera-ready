# Match tense to the time frame

- Use present tense for the paper's contents ("Section 4 shows", not "we will show"), results, method and setup, established facts, and prior work that still applies ("Smith et al. propose").
- Use past tense for events at a specific time outside the described procedure ("we collected 500 documents in 2024"), and keep it for reported findings ("Lee found that the alloy was weaker"), hypotheticals ("if the sample were larger"), and a draft's consistent past tense for experiments.
- Use future tense only for uncompleted plans, as in a proposal.
- Prefer simple to perfect tense ("Smith et al. show" or "showed", not "have shown"), but keep perfect tense for current relevance ("no prior work has evaluated X at this scale") or an event before another past event ("had tested").

Keep tense consistent within each time frame. Do not turn a general research recommendation into the authors' own promise. Replace a relative date, such as "last year", with the draft's calendar date, or flag it.

## Details

Do not assume that an event occurred in the publication year of the paper reporting it.

A sentence such as "No work had studied X before Lee" describes only the gap that existed before Lee's work. It does not state whether that gap remains open.

## Examples

**Edit 1.** Suppose the rest of the draft describes this experiment in the past tense.

> **Before.** We rinsed the seeds and dry them.

> **After.** We rinsed the seeds and dried them.

**Edit 2.** Imagine Section 3 is already written, but the second pilot is still planned.

> **Before.** Section 3 will describe the pilot. We will repeat the pilot at a second site.

> **After.** Section 3 describes the pilot. We will repeat the pilot at a second site.

The future tense stays for the second pilot because that work has yet to be done.

**Edit 3.** Say the draft presents both the model and the freezing-point relation as established knowledge that still applies.

> **Before.** Ortiz's model described how salt lowered the freezing point.

> **After.** Ortiz's model describes how salt lowers the freezing point.
