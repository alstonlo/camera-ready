# Write captions that stand alone and state the finding

A caption that only names its visual is incomplete. Complete it from the paragraph citing the visual; flag facts that appear nowhere in the draft. Identify quantities, units, axes, conditions, visual encodings such as colors and line styles, and [error bars](../claims/uncertainty.md); clarify whether higher or lower is better when that is not obvious. State the supported finding and check agreement with the body. Name compared methods or conditions instead of identifying them only by color. Flag unexplained blank or dash cells and mixed or missing column units. When the plot source or caption gives axis ranges, a second vertical axis, or the range of a fitted curve, flag a claim read from apparent distances or slopes that those scales do not support, and flag an extrapolated value called measured; an axis zoomed to part of its range is not by itself an error.

## Details

A reader should be able to interpret the visual without searching the body for the meanings of its labels. Use the paragraph that cites it to recover the supported conclusion, and check the draft for any conditions or definitions that the caption needs. Keep necessary context alongside the finding.

When prose identifies a result only by color, use the method or condition name that the draft assigns to that series. This repairs the reference in words; it does not establish whether the graphic itself is accessible without color. If the draft gives no mapping, flag the unresolved identity.

A blank cell or dash can mean an unavailable measurement, an unsupported operation, or an inapplicable comparison. Flag the missing meaning when the draft does not give it; do not treat an empty cell as zero. Flag ambiguous or mixed units in a column. A flag may propose a table note or header repair, without changing data or computing new columns. Page-limit formatting follows the [stop rules](../cutting/stop-rules.md).

Compare a visual claim with any axis settings or range of a fitted curve explicitly given in the supplied source or caption. Different panel scales or two vertical axes can invalidate a comparison based only on apparent distances or slopes. Name the particular unsupported reading; an axis need not begin at zero for every valid comparison. A fitted curve extending outside the measured range gives a prediction, not an observation. An explicitly labeled prediction is not an error.

Apply [statistical significance](../claims/significance.md) to a claim about a fit's significance. A nonsignificant test does not establish that no relationship exists, and it does not justify removing a displayed fit or unfavorable evidence.

## Examples

The paragraph citing Figure 2 states that it shows mean plant height after four weeks, in centimeters, and that plants given fertilizer were taller than untreated plants.

- Bad: Figure 2: Plant growth.
- Good: Figure 2: Mean plant height after four weeks, in centimeters. Plants given fertilizer were taller than untreated plants.

The draft states that dashes in Table 3 mark missing temperature measurements; the Good line proposes the caption revision to the author.

- Bad: Table 3: Daily air temperature at each site, in degrees Celsius.
- Good: Table 3: Daily air temperature at each site, in degrees Celsius. A dash marks a missing measurement.
