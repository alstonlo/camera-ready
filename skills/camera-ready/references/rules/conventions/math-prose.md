# Write mathematics as grammatical prose

Read each formula as a phrase; the surrounding text must still form grammatical sentences. Begin sentences and clauses with words, not bare symbols, and spell out sentence-initial numbers. Separate adjacent formulas with words, including across sentence boundaries. In prose, write quantifiers and relations as words ("for all", not ∀; "is at most 0.1", not "is ≤ 0.1"), except in work on logic. A displayed formula takes a period when ending its sentence and a comma when continuing it; a preceding colon needs a complete clause. A trailing "where" may define symbols, but a definition needed to understand the claim must come before the claim. For LaTeX paragraph breaks, see the page.

## Details

To check grammar, mentally replace each nontrivial formula with a phrase of the same grammatical role and reread aloud. An equality may supply a verb; a lone expression usually supplies a noun phrase. The words and replacement phrases must form a sentence. A formula's visual separation does not make an incomplete lead-in grammatical.

In running prose, write "for all", "there exists", and "implies" instead of their symbolic forms, except when the subject is logic. Do not put a relation symbol inside a verbal predicate as a substitute for its words. Across a sentence boundary, check whether a final symbol and the next symbol, name, or abbreviation could be read together; clarify the surrounding words.

In LaTeX, omit blank lines before and after a display that continues the same paragraph. A display ending a sentence does not necessarily end its paragraph. Punctuate the sentence according to its grammar, including the comma before a continuing "where" clause.

## Examples

- Bad: The area of a circle of radius $r$ is: \[\pi r^2.\]
- Good: The area of a circle of radius $r$ is \[\pi r^2.\]
