# Set off non-restrictive clauses with commas

Use commas around a clause that adds information rather than identifying which objects are meant. Preserve a restrictive clause's meaning: "which" does not require commas and need not become "that" unless the venue requirements say so. Do not put commas around a symbol that names the preceding noun ("a directed graph G with source s").

## Details

Commas around a clause present it as additional information about the already identified objects. Without commas, the clause can select a subset. Choose punctuation from that distinction rather than from a rule that every "which" must take commas. A symbol immediately naming its preceding noun is not a parenthetical aside.

## Examples

The draft states that all the seedlings are in the greenhouse.

- Bad: The seedlings which are in the greenhouse need water.
- Good: The seedlings, which are in the greenhouse, need water.
