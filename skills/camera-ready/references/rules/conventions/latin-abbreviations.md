# Use "i.e.", "e.g.", and "cf." only for their meanings

Use "i.e." for "that is" and "e.g." for "for example"; never interchange them. In US style, follow both with a comma; never italicize either. Use "cf." to mean "compare", not "see".

## Details

This rule has no further details.

## Examples

**Example 1.**

> **Bad.** Some fruits are rich in vitamin C, i.e., oranges and kiwis.
>
> **Good.** Some fruits are rich in vitamin C, e.g., oranges and kiwis.

**Example 2.**

> **Bad.** For the full protocol, cf. Section 2.
>
> **Good.** For the full protocol, see Section 2.
