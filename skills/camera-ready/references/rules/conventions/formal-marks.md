# Use punctuation and type only for their formal functions

Avoid contractions, exclamation marks, and emoji. Use title-case headings only when the venue requires them. Use italics for definitions, not decorative emphasis. If emphasis identifies what a negation denies, express that focus in words when the intended focus is clear; otherwise retain it and flag the ambiguity. Use quotation marks for quotations, mentioned words, and unendorsed terms. Keep marks on unendorsed terms (the model's "reasoning"); if the draft never says what the term refers to, flag that. Do not add scare quotes to ordinary words. Keep sentence punctuation outside exact literals, such as commands and outputs, unless it belongs to them; preserve literal characters.

## Details

Emphasis can identify which part of a negated statement is denied. Removing that emphasis may change the claim. Use literal wording that preserves the same focus when the meaning is clear; retain the cue if the draft does not settle the intended reading.

Commands, exact outputs, and quoted strings depend on their original characters. Typographic quote substitutions can change a shell argument or make an exact output inaccurate. Flag a proposed correction inside protected literal content rather than silently changing it.

Quotation marks can indicate that the authors use a term without endorsing its ordinary implications. Keep those marks while clarifying the operation or phenomenon the draft actually describes. Missing explanation calls for a flag, not removal of the marks or an invented definition.

## Examples

- Bad: The lid doesn't fit the jar.
- Good: The lid does not fit the jar.
