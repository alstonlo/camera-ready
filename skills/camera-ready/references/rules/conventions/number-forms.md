# Spell out small counts and use numerals for measurements

In prose, spell out a small count before a noun ("two passes"). Use numerals for measurements, quantities with units, mathematical values, numbered names ("Experiment 4"), and parameter settings ("3 cycles"). Follow the venue cutoff if one exists, otherwise the draft's consistent cutoff. Match forms when comparing quantities ("1.5M and 0.2M"); flag any edit that requires rounding or unit conversion.

## Details

For percentages and differences in percentage points, follow the rule on [variability](../claims/variability.md).

## Examples

> **Bad.** The tray holds 4 jars, each containing 200 mL of water.
>
> **Good.** The tray holds four jars, each containing 200 mL of water.

> **Context.** The draft spells out counts below ten. Its protocol specifies "3 cycles" as a parameter setting.
>
> **Bad.** We use 2 trays and run 3 cycles.
>
> **Good.** We use two trays and run 3 cycles.

> **Context.** The draft spells out counts below ten.
>
> **Bad.** We test 2 cases with $n=3$ in Experiment 4.
>
> **Good.** We test two cases with $n=3$ in Experiment 4.

> **Context.** The draft consistently uses numerals for counts of ten or more. The venue specifies no cutoff.
>
> **Bad.** We examine twelve sites.
>
> **Good.** We examine 12 sites.

> **Context.** The draft uses numerals for counts of ten or more, but the venue requires spelling out counts below twenty.
>
> **Bad.** We examine 12 sites.
>
> **Good.** We examine twelve sites.

> **Bad.** The counts are 2.4M and 800,000.
>
> **Good.** The counts are 2.4M and 0.8M.

> **Context.** The draft gives both lengths and states that 50 cm equals 0.5 m. The editor flags the unit conversion for the author rather than changing the unit directly.
>
> **Bad.** The rods measure 1 m and 50 cm.
>
> **Good.** The rods measure 1 m and 0.5 m.

> **Context.** The draft reports both counts and rounds 812,345 to 0.8M elsewhere. The editor flags the rounding for the author rather than rounding the count directly.
>
> **Bad.** The counts are 2.4M and 812,345.
>
> **Good.** The counts are 2.4M and 0.8M.
