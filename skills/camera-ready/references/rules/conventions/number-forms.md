# Spell out small counts and use numerals for measurements

Spell out small counts used as adjectives in prose ("two passes"). Keep numerals for measurements, hyperparameters even when small ("10 epochs"), quantities with units, mathematical values, and numbered names. Follow the draft's consistent cutoff for spelling out counts unless venue requirements set one. Write compared magnitudes in the same form ("1.5M and 0.2M", not "1.5M and 200,000"); flag a change requiring rounding or unit conversion.

## Details

An ordinary count can be spelled out without changing its quantity. Keep a numeral when it is a parameter value, a measurement, a mathematical value, or part of a name, even if the numeral is small. Use the draft's consistent cutoff for ordinary counts unless the venue specifies one.

Compared quantities are easier to read when their representations match. Expanding an abbreviation without changing value differs from rounding a number or converting its units. Do not convert units or change precision merely to make two strings look parallel. For percentages and percentage-point differences, follow [uncertainty](../claims/uncertainty.md).

## Examples

- Bad: The tray holds 4 jars, each containing 200 mL of water.
- Good: The tray holds four jars, each containing 200 mL of water.
