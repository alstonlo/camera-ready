# Use one term per concept

Use one term per concept; do not alternate "network", "model", "architecture", and "system" for variety. List each recurring concept's names: multiple names are an error unless they denote distinct concepts. Preserve differences in type, scope, or measured quantity. In a parallel or more general case, repeat the earlier wording exactly, then state the difference. A pronoun ("it") or bare demonstrative ("this") may refer back unambiguously within the same or next sentence. Do not refer back with a category noun ("this model", "the network"); repeat the name instead. Check captions and visual labels too; flag inconsistencies that prose edits cannot repair.

## Details

Compare names across the body, captions, table headers, and supplied diagram text. Build the inventory around the objects being discussed, not just similar-looking words. Two measurements with different populations or denominators remain different quantities even if their names resemble each other.

Preserve distinctions among mathematical objects. A vector's component is not interchangeable with a set's element, and a definition or inequality need not be called an equation. Repair an accidental alias without merging objects that the draft distinguishes.

Vary sentence structure and ordinary connecting words while keeping technical names stable. A local pronoun can avoid repetition when its antecedent is unambiguous; attaching a category noun to "this" does not make a new technical alias acceptable.

## Examples

The draft uses "drying cabinet" and "chamber" for the same piece of equipment.

- Bad: The drying cabinet holds the samples. The chamber stays at 40 °C.
- Good: The drying cabinet holds the samples. The drying cabinet stays at 40 °C.
