# Recheck claims, the argument, and references after cutting

After cutting, confirm that each surviving claim keeps its evidence, scope, and hedges under the [verb ladder](../claims/verb-ladder.md). When supporting evidence has been relocated, add a precise reference to its new location; weaken a claim only when the remaining evidence supports less than before, not merely because the support moved. Reread the problem, gap, approach, findings, and implications, and restore any logical steps the cut removed so that readers do not have to reconstruct them. Finally, repair dangling references, uncited figures or tables, pointers to nonexistent appendix sections, and terms used before they are defined.

## Details

This rule has no further details.

## Examples

A cut moves the recovery experiment from Section 5 to Appendix D.

> **Bad.** The recovery experiment is described in Section 5.
>
> **Good.** The recovery experiment is described in Appendix D.

A cut moves the draft's proof of the bound to an existing Appendix C.

> **Bad.** The bound holds for every input.
>
> **Good.** The bound holds for every input (Appendix C).

The draft establishes that the coating prevents rust in fresh water, and a cut moves the full supporting test to Appendix B.

> **Bad.** The coating may prevent rust in fresh water (Appendix B).
>
> **Good.** The coating prevents rust in fresh water (Appendix B).

An authorized cut removes the only test of the metal lid, leaving only the test showing that the glass lid reduces evaporation.

> **Bad.** Both lids reduce evaporation.
>
> **Good.** The glass lid reduces evaporation.

The draft presents slow drainage as a tentative explanation, and a cut removes "may".

> **Bad.** The delay reflects slow drainage.
>
> **Good.** The delay may reflect slow drainage.

A cut removes the draft's statement that the soil is too acidic for beans.

> **Bad.** We measure acidity. We therefore add lime.
>
> **Good.** We measure acidity. The soil is too acidic for beans. We therefore add lime.

A cut removes the draft's earlier definition of R as the recovery time.

> **Bad.** We record R after each trial.
>
> **Good.** We record the recovery time R after each trial.

Figure 2 remains in the draft and supports this claim, but a cut removes its only citation.

> **Bad.** The wet samples lose more mass.
>
> **Good.** The wet samples lose more mass (Figure 2).
