# Recheck claims, the argument, and references after cutting

After cutting, check that each remaining claim keeps its evidence, scope, and hedges under the [verb ladder](../claims/verb-ladder.md). If you moved supporting evidence, refer precisely to its new location. Weaken a claim only when the remaining evidence supports less, not merely because the evidence moved. Reread the problem, gap, approach, findings, and implications, and restore any reasoning steps the cut removed. Finally, repair references to cut text or nonexistent appendix sections, cite every remaining figure and table, and define each term before its first use.

## Details

This rule has no further details.

## Examples

**Example 1.** To shorten a draft, an editor moves the recovery experiment from Section 5 to Appendix D.

> **Bad.** The recovery experiment is described in Section 5.
>
> **Good.** The recovery experiment is described in Appendix D.

**Example 2.** To shorten a draft, an editor moves the proof of the bound to the existing Appendix C.

> **Bad.** The bound holds for every input.
>
> **Good.** The bound holds for every input (Appendix C).

**Example 3.** The draft establishes that the coating prevents rust in fresh water. An editor moves the full supporting test to Appendix B.

> **Bad.** The coating may prevent rust in fresh water (Appendix B).
>
> **Good.** The coating prevents rust in fresh water (Appendix B).

**Example 4.** With the author's permission, an editor cuts the draft's only test of the metal lid. The remaining test shows that the glass lid reduces evaporation.

> **Bad.** Both lids reduce evaporation.
>
> **Good.** The glass lid reduces evaporation.

**Example 5.** The draft gives slow drainage as a tentative explanation. An editor cuts "may".

> **Bad.** The delay reflects slow drainage.
>
> **Good.** The delay may reflect slow drainage.

**Example 6.** An editor cuts the draft's statement that the soil is too acidic for beans.

> **Bad.** We measure acidity. We therefore add lime.
>
> **Good.** We measure acidity. The soil is too acidic for beans. We therefore add lime.

**Example 7.** An editor cuts the draft's earlier definition of R as the recovery time.

> **Bad.** We record R after each trial.
>
> **Good.** We record the recovery time R after each trial.

**Example 8.** An editor cuts the only citation to Figure 2. The figure remains in the draft and supports the claim about mass loss.

> **Bad.** The wet samples lose more mass.
>
> **Good.** The wet samples lose more mass (Figure 2).
