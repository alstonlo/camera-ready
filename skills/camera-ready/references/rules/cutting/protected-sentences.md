# Protect sentences the argument depends on

Preserve the material below, moving it only when the [sentence-by-sentence steps](sentence-dependencies.md) allow:

- Keep hedges, qualifiers, scope conditions, limitations, supporting evidence, and counterevidence. Cut a repeated limitation only when the triage order reaches it and a full statement remains elsewhere. Because removing a caveat changes a [claim's scope](../claims/scope.md), [recalibrate the claim](post-cut-check.md) after an authorized cut only as remaining evidence requires.
- Keep the reasons for choices in the method and the details needed to reproduce the work (see Details).
- Keep definitions, topic sentences, necessary framing, [high-level summaries](../prose/direct-content.md), [logical transitions](../prose/transitions.md), and references back to earlier sections that later text relies on. Keep [standalone caption content](../conventions/captions.md) even when it repeats the body (see Details).
- Keep fair credit to prior work and accurate descriptions of it. Preserve acknowledgments, funding statements, disclosures of assistance (including help from AI tools), and disclosures of financial or other relationships that could bias the work.

## Details

Readers may need a sentence even when it adds no new facts. Details needed to reproduce the work include the statistical tests used, how the authors handle missing data, and how they correct for multiple comparisons. A topic sentence states a paragraph's point. Standalone caption content lets readers understand a caption without the body text and includes "adapted from" credits and permission notices. Before treating two results as redundant, check whether they describe different conditions or failure cases. Do not guess at assistance that the draft does not record, and do not search for financial or other relationships that the draft does not disclose.

## Examples

**Edit 1.** Before shows a proposed cut from the draft. The edit restores "It reduces growth in dry soil", so that the passage keeps the condition in which the treatment has the opposite effect.

> **Before.** The fertilizer increases growth in wet soil.
>
> **After.** The fertilizer increases growth in wet soil. It reduces growth in dry soil.

**Edit 2.** Before shows a proposed cut from a figure caption. The edit restores "Adapted from Rao et al. (2022), with permission", so that the caption acknowledges the adapted material and its permission on its own.

> **Before.** The roots (brown) spread wider than the leaves (green).
>
> **After.** The roots (brown) spread wider than the leaves (green). Adapted from Rao et al. (2022), with permission.

**Edit 3.** Before shows a proposed cut of the paragraph's opening sentence. The edit restores "Thicker walls keep the jar cooler" before the measurements, so that readers know what the measurements show even though the opening adds no new data.

> **Before.** At noon, the thick jar reads 24 °C and the thin jar reads 29 °C.
>
> **After.** Thicker walls keep the jar cooler. At noon, the thick jar reads 24 °C and the thin jar reads 29 °C.

**Edit 4.** The author has requested cuts, and the triage order has reached repeated limitations. The edit deletes the second "The results cannot establish effects in children" while keeping the first statement and its reason, so that the authorized cut removes repetition without losing the limitation.

> **Before.** Only adults participate, so the results cannot establish effects in children. The results cannot establish effects in children.
>
> **After.** Only adults participate, so the results cannot establish effects in children.
