# Protect sentences the argument depends on

Preserve the material below, moving it only when the [sentence-by-sentence steps](sentence-dependencies.md) allow:

- Keep hedges, qualifiers, scope conditions, limitations, supporting evidence, and counterevidence. Cut a repeated limitation only when the triage order reaches it and a full statement remains elsewhere. Because removing a caveat changes a [claim's scope](../claims/scope.md), [recalibrate the claim](post-cut-check.md) after an authorized cut only as remaining evidence requires.
- Keep the reasons for choices in the method and the details needed to reproduce the work (see Details).
- Keep definitions, topic sentences, necessary framing, [high-level summaries](../prose/direct-content.md), [logical transitions](../prose/transitions.md), and references back to earlier sections that later text relies on. Keep [standalone caption content](../conventions/captions.md) even when it repeats the body (see Details).
- Keep fair credit to prior work and accurate descriptions of it. Preserve acknowledgments, funding statements, disclosures of assistance (including help from AI tools), and disclosures of financial or other relationships that could bias the work.

## Details

Readers may need a sentence even when it adds no new facts. Details needed to reproduce the work include the statistical tests used, how the authors handle missing data, and how they correct for multiple comparisons. A topic sentence states a paragraph's point. Standalone caption content lets readers understand a caption without the body text and includes "adapted from" credits and permission notices. Before treating two results as redundant, check whether they describe different conditions or failure cases. Do not guess at assistance that the draft does not record, and do not search for financial or other relationships that the draft does not disclose.

## Examples

> **Context.** An editor proposes cutting the counterevidence from a draft that reports fertilizer increases growth in wet soil but reduces growth in dry soil.
>
> **Bad.** The fertilizer increases growth in wet soil.
>
> **Good.** The fertilizer increases growth in wet soil. It reduces growth in dry soil.

> **Context.** An editor proposes cutting the source attribution and permission statement from a figure caption.
>
> **Bad.** The roots (brown) spread wider than the leaves (green).
>
> **Good.** The roots (brown) spread wider than the leaves (green). Adapted from Rao et al. (2022), with permission.

> **Context.** An editor proposes cutting the scope condition "In fresh water" and the hedge "may" from a claim.
>
> **Bad.** The coating slows corrosion.
>
> **Good.** In fresh water, the coating may slow corrosion.

> **Context.** An editor proposes cutting a stated limitation to shorten a description of a trial.
>
> **Bad.** The trial includes twelve adults.
>
> **Good.** The trial includes twelve adults and cannot establish effects in children.

> **Context.** An editor proposes cutting the reason why the authors weigh each sample twice.
>
> **Bad.** We weigh each sample twice.
>
> **Good.** We weigh each sample twice because the scale drifts.

> **Context.** An editor proposes removing the name of the statistical test used to compare the groups.
>
> **Bad.** We compare the two groups.
>
> **Good.** We compare the two groups with a permutation test.

> **Context.** An editor proposes cutting the draft's only definition of recovery.
>
> **Bad.** We measure recovery after each pulse.
>
> **Good.** Recovery is the return to the initial temperature. We measure recovery after each pulse.

> **Context.** An editor proposes cutting a paragraph's opening topic sentence because the measurements that follow report the same finding.
>
> **Bad.** At noon, the thick jar reads 24 °C and the thin jar reads 29 °C.
>
> **Good.** Thicker walls keep the jar cooler. At noon, the thick jar reads 24 °C and the thin jar reads 29 °C.

> **Context.** An editor proposes cutting the framing sentence and the transition word "therefore" from a two-sentence passage.
>
> **Bad.** We measure flow.
>
> **Good.** Clogging can restrict flow. We therefore measure flow.

> **Context.** An editor proposes cutting a reference back to Section 2, where the supporting evidence appears.
>
> **Bad.** The lid halves evaporation, so we keep the lid on in the field trial.
>
> **Good.** The lid halves evaporation (Section 2), so we keep the lid on in the field trial.

> **Context.** An editor proposes replacing a citation of prior work with a vague reference to a published protocol.
>
> **Bad.** We use the published drying protocol.
>
> **Good.** We use Chen's (2021) drying protocol.

> **Context.** An editor proposes cutting the funding disclosure from the acknowledgments.
>
> **Bad.** We thank the field crew.
>
> **Good.** We thank the field crew. The pump manufacturer funded this study.

> **Context.** An editor proposes cutting the only numerical evidence that supports a claim.
>
> **Bad.** The coating slows corrosion.
>
> **Good.** The coating slows corrosion. Treated nails lose 2 mg, compared with 8 mg for untreated nails.

> **Context.** The author asks to shorten the draft, and the editor cuts repeated statements under the triage order.
>
> **Bad.** Only adults participate, so the results cannot establish effects in children. The results cannot establish effects in children.
>
> **Good.** Only adults participate, so the results cannot establish effects in children.

> **Context.** An editor proposes cutting the draft's disclosure that the authors used an AI tool to edit grammar.
>
> **Bad.** We thank the field crew.
>
> **Good.** We thank the field crew. We used an AI tool to edit grammar.
