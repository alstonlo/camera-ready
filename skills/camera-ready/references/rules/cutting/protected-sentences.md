# Protect sentences the argument depends on

Keep the material below even when it adds no new information, and move it only when the [sentence-by-sentence steps](sentence-dependencies.md) allow:

- Nonredundant hedges, qualifiers, scope conditions, limitations, supporting evidence, and counterevidence. Cut a repeated limitation only when the triage order reaches it and a full statement remains elsewhere. Removing a caveat changes a [claim's scope](../claims/scope.md); after an authorized cut, [recalibrate the claim](post-cut-check.md).
- Reasons for method choices and details needed to reproduce the work, such as statistical tests, handling of missing data, and corrections for multiple comparisons.
- Topic sentences, necessary framing, [high-level summaries](../prose/direct-content.md), [logical transitions](../prose/transitions.md), and references to earlier sections that later text relies on. When cutting a minor result, usually keep the sentence explaining why this class of question matters if it frames surviving results. Keep [standalone caption content](../conventions/captions.md), including "adapted from" credits and permission notices, even when it repeats the body.
- Fair credit to prior work and accurate descriptions of it, acknowledgments, funding statements, and disclosures of assistance (including from AI tools) and of conflicts of interest.

## Details

Before treating results as redundant, check whether they describe different conditions or failure cases. Do not infer unrecorded assistance or search for financial or other relationships the draft does not disclose.

## Examples

**Edit 1.** Suppose the draft also reports reduced growth in dry soil, and an editor proposes cutting that result.

> **Before.** The fertilizer increases growth in wet soil.

> **After.** The fertilizer increases growth in wet soil. It reduces growth in dry soil.

Without the second result, readers see only the condition in which the fertilizer helps.

**Edit 2.** Imagine an editor proposes removing a figure caption's credit to Rao et al. (2022) and its permission statement.

> **Before.** The roots (brown) spread wider than the leaves (green).

> **After.** The roots (brown) spread wider than the leaves (green). Adapted from Rao et al. (2022), with permission.

The figure needs its credit and permission statement when readers view it on its own.

**Edit 3.** Say an editor proposes cutting the paragraph's opening sentence.

> **Before.** At noon, the thick jar reads 24 °C and the thin jar reads 29 °C.

> **After.** Thicker walls keep the jar cooler. At noon, the thick jar reads 24 °C and the thin jar reads 29 °C.

The measurements alone leave readers to work out the paragraph's point.

**Edit 4.** Suppose the author has requested cuts, and the triage order has reached repeated limitations.

> **Before.** Only adults participate, so the results cannot establish effects in children. The results cannot establish effects in children.

> **After.** Only adults participate, so the results cannot establish effects in children.
