# Recheck claims, the argument, and references after cutting

Confirm that each surviving claim keeps its evidence, scope, and hedges, using the [verb ladder](../claims/verb-ladder.md). Add a precise reference to relocated support; relocation alone is no reason to weaken a claim. Weaken a claim only if the surviving evidence supports less than before. Reread the problem, gap, approach, findings, and implications, and restore lost logical steps so that readers need not reconstruct them. Repair dangling references, uncited figures or tables, references to nonexistent appendix sections, and terms used before their definitions.

## Details

A local cut can leave a distant headline claim or reference inconsistent even when adjacent sentences read smoothly. Recheck the whole argument, including references to moved evidence and definitions that first appeared in a deleted passage.

A relocated proof or result remains evidence; its location alone does not justify a hedge. If an authorized cut removes support that cannot be kept, the surviving claim must match the remaining support.

## Examples

The recovery experiment has moved from Section 5 to Appendix D.

- Bad: The recovery experiment is described in Section 5.
- Good: The recovery experiment is described in Appendix D.
