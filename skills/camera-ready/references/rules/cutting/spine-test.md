# Apply the spine test before cutting sentences

State the text's core claims, which form its spine, in one to three sentences, then give each paragraph's role in one line. Tag each paragraph **central** if it states a core claim, **supporting** if readers need it to understand or assess one, **ancillary** if it is relevant but dispensable to that assessment, or **off-spine** if it is unrelated to those claims. Paragraphs carrying counterevidence or other [protected content](protect.md) are supporting unless central; never treat them as expendable. If you cannot write a short, specific title summarizing a section, flag its competing points before cutting. Subject to [the request](idea-level.md), cut or relocate off-spine paragraphs, trim ancillary ones, then work [sentence by sentence](sentence-by-sentence.md). Keep a cut only if the argument remains at least as clear.

## Details

A true or interesting passage does not necessarily help the paper establish its core claims. Assess its role in this argument before shortening its sentences.

The title test asks whether you can summarize the section's point, not whether its printed heading is specific. A conventional heading such as Results is not itself a problem.

Supporting content includes evidence that challenges a claim, because readers need it to assess that claim. Ancillary material may describe a relevant extension that the core argument does not depend on. Off-spine material concerns a different question. Protection takes precedence over treating a passage as expendable.

## Examples

The author requests a length cut; the paper tests whether gravel reduces soil erosion and makes no claim about aquarium design.

- Bad: Gravel reduces soil erosion in our tests. Gravel also decorates aquariums.
- Good: Gravel reduces soil erosion in our tests.
