# Cut in triage order under a length constraint

Under a length constraint, cut in this order, subject to [protected content](protect.md) and [relocation safeguards](sentence-by-sentence.md):

1. **Repeated restatement.** Cut points repeated across the introduction, section openings, and conclusion. Keep the most useful instance, usually the one that develops the point. Always keep one statement of each contribution in each of the abstract, introduction, and conclusion, plus [shorthand reminders](../conventions/refresh-shorthand.md). Keep a paragraph's closing restatement if readers need the intervening explanation to understand the opening claim. If a restatement copies another passage (a conclusion repeating the abstract word for word), revise it using established results or flag it. Apply the [deletion test](../prose/deletion-test.md).
2. **Signposting left after bloat removal.** This includes the introduction's permitted [roadmap](../prose/signposts.md). Preserve pointers to evidence and proofs.
3. **Tangential background and related work.** Cut background that does not motivate the question, and cut citation lists unrelated to the gap. Keep the few works and comparisons that define the gap.
4. **Minor results, secondary analyses, and exploratory findings.** Remove from the main text results that advance no central claim. Relocate sensitivity analyses and robustness checks needed for rigor; keep in the main text any failure or counterevidence that changes a central claim. A failed approach stays in the main text only as a stated negative result, not as a history of attempts.
5. **Exhaustive parameters and implementation detail.** Keep the method reproducible in outline, including [explicit procedures](../prose/definitions-procedures.md), essential steps, and conditions; relocate full sweeps and subsidiary detail.

## Details

Keep the developed account of a point instead of only a thin introductory version. A contribution statement in the abstract or conclusion serves readers of that part alone; state the contribution using the paper's specific results rather than copying another passage.

A difficult paragraph may need both a preview and a closing statement. Keep both when the intervening explanation gives readers the concepts needed to understand the point. An ordinary paragraph does not need a closing sentence that merely repeats an already clear opening.

A list of citations without a stated connection to the gap is a cut candidate. The comparison that establishes the gap remains necessary even if it takes more words than the list.

Sensitivity analyses and robustness checks can move only if the main text still reports any failure that changes the headline claim. An inconvenient result is not an irrelevant result. Keep the negative finding about a failed alternative when it bears on a central claim; omit the chronology of unsuccessful attempts.

## Examples

The author requests a length cut; this paragraph's opening sentence is clear on its own.

- Bad: The lid reduces evaporation in both jars. In both jars, less water evaporates with the lid.
- Good: The lid reduces evaporation in both jars.
