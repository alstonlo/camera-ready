# Follow the triage order to meet a length limit

To meet a length limit, cut material in this triage order, preserving [protected content](protected-sentences.md) and following [relocation safeguards](sentence-dependencies.md):

1. **Repeated points.** Cut points repeated across the introduction, section openings, and conclusion, keeping the most informative statement (usually the one that explains the point). Preserve one statement of each contribution in each of the abstract, introduction, and conclusion, along with [shorthand reminders](../conventions/shorthand-reminders.md). Keep a closing restatement when readers need the intervening explanation to understand the opening claim. If a restatement copies another passage word for word, as when a conclusion repeats the abstract, revise it to state established results or flag it. Apply the [deletion test](../prose/deletion-test.md).
2. **Remaining signposts.** Cut signposts left after removing empty wording, including the introduction's closing [roadmap](../prose/signposts.md). Keep references to evidence and proofs.
3. **Unrelated background and related work.** Cut background that does not motivate the research question and citation lists unrelated to the gap. Keep the few works and comparisons that define the gap.
4. **Minor results, secondary analyses, and exploratory findings.** Remove results from the main text if they do not support a central claim, and relocate sensitivity analyses and robustness checks needed for rigor. In the main text, keep any failure or counterevidence that alters a central claim, and state a failed approach as a negative result rather than narrating attempts.
5. **Full parameter lists and procedural details.** Keep [explicit procedures](../prose/definitions-procedures.md), essential steps, and conditions in the main text so readers can reproduce the method in outline. Relocate complete parameter lists, full parameter sweeps, and subsidiary details.

## Details

This rule has no further details.

## Examples

**Edit 1.** The author asks to shorten the draft. The edit deletes "In both jars, less water evaporates with the lid", because it repeats the first sentence without adding information.

> **Before.** The lid reduces evaporation in both jars. In both jars, less water evaporates with the lid.
>
> **After.** The lid reduces evaporation in both jars.

**Edit 2.** An editor proposes cutting the closing restatement. The edit restores "The vent thus prevents pressure buildup", because readers need the intervening explanation of gas expansion to understand the opening claim and the closing sentence connects that explanation back to the claim.

> **Before.** An open vent prevents pressure buildup. Heating expands the gas, which escapes through the vent.
>
> **After.** An open vent prevents pressure buildup. Heating expands the gas, which escapes through the vent. The vent thus prevents pressure buildup.

**Edit 3.** The draft still exceeds the length limit after redundant restatements are cut. The edit deletes the sentence announcing Sections 2 and 3 but keeps the reference to Appendix A, so that it cuts the roadmap while preserving directions to the proof.

> **Before.** Section 2 presents the design, and Section 3 presents the tests. Appendix A proves the stability bound.
>
> **After.** Appendix A proves the stability bound.

**Edit 4.** The draft still exceeds the length limit after Tiers 1–3, and Appendix B already contains the pressure checks. The edit replaces the sentence about repeating each test at three pressures with a reference to Appendix B, so that readers can find those secondary details while the main text keeps the leak that limits the seal's success.

> **Before.** The seal holds at 10 °C and 20 °C but leaks at 30 °C. We repeat each temperature test at three pressures.
>
> **After.** The seal holds at 10 °C and 20 °C but leaks at 30 °C. Appendix B gives the pressure checks.
