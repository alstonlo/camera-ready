# Protect sentences the argument depends on

A sentence that adds no new information can still be necessary. Preserve the following, relocating only as the [sentence-by-sentence steps](sentence-by-sentence.md) allow:

- Keep hedges, qualifiers, scope conditions, limitations, supporting evidence, and counterevidence. Cut a repeated statement of a limitation only in the triage order, and only when a full statement remains elsewhere. Removing a caveat changes a [claim's scope](../claims/scope.md); after an authorized cut, [recalibrate the claim](after-the-pass.md) only as the evidence requires.
- Keep the reasons for methodological choices and reproducibility details, including statistical tests, missing-data handling, and multiple-comparison procedures.
- Keep definitions, topic sentences, necessary framing, [high-level summaries](../prose/direct-content.md), and [logical transitions](../prose/transitions.md). Also keep back-references that later text needs. Preserve [standalone caption content](../conventions/figures-tables.md), including "adapted from" credits and permission notices, even when it repeats the body.
- Preserve fair credit and characterization of prior work, acknowledgments, funding statements, disclosures of help received (including help from AI tools), and disclosures of financial or other relationships that could bias the work.

## Details

A sentence need not introduce a new result to be necessary. A qualifier can bound several later claims, a framing sentence can explain why the remaining results matter, and a transition can supply the inference between two passages.

Preserve the stated reason for a methodological choice when readers need it to judge the design. A reproducible sequence of operations may still leave the choice unjustified without that reason.

Before calling results redundant, compare their conditions and failure cases. Preserve evidence that contradicts or narrows a conclusion, even when removing it would make the account smoother.

Captions must keep their interpretive content when shortened. Keep credits for reused or adapted figures and any accompanying permission notice; a shorter caption must not imply that borrowed material is original.

Funding, assistance, and relationship disclosures may not advance a technical claim, but shortening must not erase them or change a statement of responsibility. This rule does not ask the editor to reconstruct an unrecorded history of assistance or to search for undisclosed relationships.

## Examples

The draft reports that the fertilizer increases growth in wet soil and reduces it in dry soil.

- Bad: The fertilizer increases growth in wet soil.
- Good: The fertilizer increases growth in wet soil. It reduces growth in dry soil.

The draft's caption ends by crediting the adapted diagram to Rao et al. (2022) and recording permission to reuse it.

- Bad: The roots (brown) spread wider than the leaves (green).
- Good: The roots (brown) spread wider than the leaves (green). Adapted from Rao et al. (2022), with permission.
