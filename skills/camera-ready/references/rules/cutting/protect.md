# Protect

Some sentences add no new information, but the argument depends on them. Preserve the following content, relocating only as the [sentence-by-sentence pass](../../guide.md#sentence-by-sentence) allows:

- **Hedges, qualifiers, scope conditions, and limitations.** Deleting "these results come from a single dataset and may not generalize" silently strengthens every subsequent claim. Handle a repeated limitation stated in full elsewhere according to the [triage order](../../guide.md#triage-order). Cutting a caveat is a [claims edit](../../guide.md#claims). If a length request requires such a cut, recalibrate the surviving claim [after the pass](../../guide.md#after-the-pass).
- **The evidence that supports a claim.** Cutting the supporting number or example while keeping the confident assertion leaves the claim unsupported.
- **Framing, orienting, and summary sentences.** When cutting a minor result, usually keep the sentence explaining why this class of question matters; it now frames the surviving results. Also keep a summary sentence that [states the high-level idea](../../guide.md#concision) before the specifics.
- **Caption content that repeats the body text.** Readers use captions independently of the body, so this repetition is necessary. Tighten a caption's wording while preserving its [required content](../../guide.md#figures-and-tables).
- **Genuine transitions and back-references.** A transition that [carries logic](../../guide.md#paragraphs) stays, as does a back-reference a later sentence depends on.
- **Topic sentences.** Cutting a paragraph's topic sentence leaves it incomplete.
- **Reproducibility content.** Preserve the statistical tests used, the handling of missing data and multiple comparisons, and enough detail about the method for the reader outside the subfield to judge the study.
- **Fair characterization of prior work.** Preserve sentences that credit a predecessor or accurately state what they did.
